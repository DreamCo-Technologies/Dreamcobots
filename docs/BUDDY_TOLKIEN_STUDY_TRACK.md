## Extended twelve-unit study path

1. **Tolkien's scholarly formation and intellectual context** — construct a dated, cited biography from authorized catalog records and licensed scholarship; separate established facts from interpretation.
2. **Philology as a method** — explain comparative philology, historical linguistics, textual transmission, manuscript variation and evidence standards.
3. **Historical language families** — compare independently sourced Old English, Old Norse, Finnish and other relevant language-family histories using rights-cleared linguistic data.
4. **Phonology and sound change** — explain phonemes, allophones, sound laws, sound change and reconstruction using original examples unrelated to Tolkien's protected languages.
5. **Morphology and syntax** — analyze agglutination, inflection, case, number, tense, derivation and syntax with independent language datasets.
6. **Tolkien's language invention as a scholarly subject** — summarize published scholarly analysis of language evolution, internal consistency and authorial process; do not reproduce protected lexicons, scripts or generate language content.
7. **Writing systems and manuscript studies** — compare public-domain writing systems and scholarly manuscript description; discuss Tengwar only at a high level through authorized commentary, without reproducing its characters or charts.
8. **Comparative mythology and source criticism** — compare public-domain myth traditions and independently licensed scholarship; cite variants, provenance and uncertainty.
9. **Mythopoeia and literary architecture** — evaluate how language, history, myth, narrative and culture interact as abstract literary systems; use independent public-domain works for examples.
10. **Archives, bibliography and digital humanities** — create a source graph from public catalog metadata with stable IDs, dates, editions, rights state and uncertainty.
11. **Reception, adaptation and scholarly debate** — compare peer-reviewed interpretations and adaptation criticism without copying protected dialogue, scripts, art or audiovisual materials.
12. **Research capstone and oral defense** — submit an original, cited research brief, annotated bibliography, provenance ledger, rights review and recorded rubric; defend claims and uncertainty.

## Practical assignments and evidence

- Build a source chronology with citations and distinguish fact, inference and disputed interpretation.
- Create a comparative language-family diagram using independent, rights-cleared linguistic datasets.
- Produce original phonology/morphology exercises that do not imitate or extend Tolkien's invented languages.
- Write a comparative mythology paper using public-domain source editions whose translation/edition rights have been checked.
- Build a knowledge graph from bibliographic and archival metadata, preserving source IDs and uncertainty.
- Produce a literature review that compares at least two scholarly interpretations and notes disagreements.
- Complete a capstone with citations, rights ledger, originality review and independent scoring.

Each assignment must record: lesson ID, exact resource IDs, source URLs, access date, edition/revision, item-level rights status, learner artifact hash, rubric version, evaluator version, score, reviewer, timestamp, remediation and completion state. A source being listed or opened is not a completed lesson.

## Rights and content boundaries

The Tolkien Estate's public guidance and archival repositories impose restrictions on Tolkien texts, artwork, maps, invented languages and scripts, readings and derivative materials. DreamCo must not treat public visibility, search indexing, open access, library catalog access or a course enrollment as permission to copy or train on protected materials.

- Tolkien novels, appendices, letters, scans, audio, audiobooks and transcripts are not approved training data by default.
- No verbatim reproduction or bulk ingestion of Tolkien text.
- Do not reproduce protected invented-language lexicons, scripts, charts, maps, artwork, characters, settings or distinctive story expression.
- Study Tolkien's linguistic work through licensed scholarly commentary and high-level analysis; use independent examples and public-domain linguistic corpora for hands-on language exercises.
- Every article, course module, dataset, translation, image and recording needs its own rights/terms review for the intended activity.
- Unknown or conflicting rights are BLOCKED. Course access is not a training license.
- Keep benchmark holdouts separate from study material and training.
- No model-weight training is authorized by this curriculum.
