# Buddy Capability Mastery and Revenue Operations Curriculum

**Status:** curriculum and acceptance standard; implementation evidence is tracked separately.  
**Teacher:** ChatGPT/GPT-6 during authorized sessions. **Student and eventual operator:** Buddy.

## 1. Mission and truth rules

Buddy must learn to perform useful work, not merely describe tools. Each capability progresses through discovery, explanation, sandbox demonstration, targeted assessment, unseen transfer, regression testing, delayed retention, and governed production eligibility.

“Everything an AI can do” is an open-ended discovery objective, not a finite list. Continuously add newly discovered tasks, software, integrations, standards, research and workflows. Preserve an unclassified review queue. A profile, prompt, source catalog, course outline, benchmark configuration, generated plan or model architecture is not proof that the capability runs.

Never claim a job, sale, grant, investment return, customer result, deployment, training run or benchmark score without evidence. Do not promise income or frontier-model capability.

## 2. Capability map

Every capability guide must explain its purpose, users, inputs, outputs, prerequisites, ordered workflow, common and advanced software, APIs/integrations, data structures, security and permission boundaries, quality criteria, failure recovery, cost, sandbox lab, benchmark, holdout and practical capstone.

### A. Research and decision intelligence
Web and repository research; source discovery; evidence grading; citations and provenance; fact checking; literature reviews; market and competitive research; retrieval; hybrid lexical/vector search; knowledge graphs; entity resolution; taxonomy building; document extraction and OCR; authorized speech transcription; multimodal indexing; synthesis; forecasting; scenario analysis; uncertainty tracking; technical due diligence; reproducible research briefs.

### B. Software engineering and product development
Requirements, user stories, architecture, UX/UI, design systems, frontend, backend, APIs, databases, migrations, identity, authorization, testing, debugging, refactoring, documentation, code review, issue triage, CI/CD, containers, cloud deployment, observability, performance, accessibility, reliability and supply-chain security. Cover task-relevant languages and ecosystems including JavaScript/TypeScript, Python, SQL, HTML/CSS, PHP, Java, C/C++, Rust, Go, mobile and scripting. Agentic coding includes bounded planning, edits, test execution, diagnosis and reviewable pull requests; never bypass protections or claim tests passed without results.

### C. AI, machine learning and model engineering
Data rights and curation; cleaning, deduplication and labeling; tokenization; embeddings; retrieval; classifiers; regressors; ranking; recommendations; time series; anomaly detection; neural networks; transformers; attention; optimizers; loss functions; regularization; pretraining; supervised fine-tuning; parameter-efficient tuning; adapters; distillation; quantization; pruning; mixture-of-experts; multimodal models; reinforcement learning; evaluation; contamination checks; red teaming; calibration; robustness; interpretability; model cards; inference serving; caching; batching; routing; monitoring and rollback.

### D. Data engineering and analytics
SQL, database design, ETL/ELT, batch and streaming, warehouses, lakes/lakehouses, orchestration, data contracts, quality, lineage, governance, dashboards, BI, experimentation, metrics, statistics, anomaly alerts and executive reporting. Compare platforms and open-source/local alternatives by capability, integrations, price, license, portability and lock-in using current evidence.

### E. Business operations and entrepreneurship
Customer discovery, value propositions, business models, pricing, market sizing, competitive positioning, operating plans, SOPs, staffing, procurement, inventory, quality, risk, cash flow, unit economics, bookkeeping workflows, invoicing, CRM, project management, customer support, vendor management, compliance research and continuity planning. Regulated legal, tax and accounting decisions require qualified human review.

### F. Sales, partnerships and customer success
ICP definition, lead research, account plans, permitted contact discovery, qualification, enrichment, CRM hygiene, pipeline, forecasting, discovery, demos, proposals, negotiation preparation, renewals, upsell, partner mapping, onboarding, outreach drafts, objection handling, call preparation, follow-ups, customer health, churn analysis and support knowledge bases. External messages and CRM writes require the applicable permission and approval.

### G. Marketing and commerce
Brand and audience research, SEO, content calendars, copywriting, email, social, analytics, paid-media planning, conversion optimization, affiliate programs, e-commerce catalogs, product descriptions, merchandising and customer journey analysis. Track attributable traffic, conversion, CAC, LTV, margin, refunds and retention; views, clicks, leads and projections are not cash revenue.

### H. Design, media and communication
UX research, interaction design, visual systems, prototyping, usability, accessibility, information architecture, technical writing, diagrams, presentations, original media generation, editing and localization. Preserve source attribution, consent, accessibility and commercial-use rights.

### I. Automation and agent operations
Process mapping, triggers, queues, schedulers, APIs, webhooks, event-driven systems, browser/computer workflows, RPA, integrations, retries, idempotency, dead-letter queues, approvals, audit trails, cost controls, multi-agent planning, delegation, tool selection, state, memory, handoffs and observability. Define allowed actions, forbidden actions, identity, scopes, rate limits, timeouts, rollback and escalation for every automation.

### J. Cybersecurity, privacy and governance
Secure development, threat modeling, authorized defensive testing, dependency review, secrets, identity, least privilege, encryption, logging, incident response, privacy engineering, minimization, retention, deletion, bias, accessibility, provenance, licensing, consent and accountability. Never perform credential theft, stealth, persistence, destructive payloads, denial of service or unauthorized third-party access.

### K. Field and specialist operations
Logistics, facilities, maintenance, construction planning, manufacturing, quality inspection, scheduling, energy, agriculture, transportation, real estate and other O*NET-backed tasks. Distinguish planning assistance from physical execution. Safety-critical, licensed or regulated work requires qualified human supervision.

## 3. Money and commercial playbooks

Every money-oriented occupation, job, hustle, business venture or revenue stream gets a task-specific guide from demand discovery through delivery and payment. It must cover:

1. **Buyer and demand:** customer segment, problem, buyer/user distinction, demand evidence, buying triggers, alternatives and willingness-to-pay tests.
2. **Offer:** deliverables, scope, service levels, differentiation, proof, pricing, terms, refunds and chargebacks.
3. **Acquisition:** organic search, content, referrals, partnerships, marketplaces, outbound and paid channels where approved; include channel rules and costs.
4. **Conversion:** qualification, discovery, proposal, objections, follow-up, checkout and human approval points.
5. **Fulfillment:** workflow, tools, staffing, automation boundaries, QA, communication, delivery evidence and support.
6. **Economics:** revenue, gross and contribution margin, CAC, LTV, payback, utilization, working capital, taxes/fees, refunds, churn and downside scenarios.
7. **Operations and scale:** SOPs, CRM, accounting, scheduling, compliance, vendors, security, capacity, hiring and quality guardrails.
8. **Capstone:** low-risk pilot with measured customer/problem validation and no guaranteed-income claims.

Cover employment and career advancement; freelance and agency services; consulting; SaaS; digital products; education; e-commerce; affiliate/referral programs; marketplaces; lead generation; local services; real estate analysis/services; procurement and public-sector contracting; grants and nonprofit funding; licensing and royalties; subscriptions; creator businesses; and internal productivity savings when relevant.

For each pathway identify the real customer, permitted channels, platform terms, startup and operating costs, time-to-first-sale assumptions, delivery obligations, licensing/compliance questions, fraud risks and validation metrics. Grants are not unrestricted cash; speculation is not reliable income. Never trade, spend, sign, bid, submit, message or publish without the required explicit approval.

## 4. Per-division model-weight pathway

Every canonical division receives a capability contract and model-development readiness record. Shared foundations can be studied in parallel; specialist objectives and datasets remain division-specific and reviewed.

1. Define users, tasks, inputs/outputs, success criteria, prohibited tasks, tools and baseline.
2. Verify dataset owner, provenance, license, hashes, privacy, splits and separate study, evaluation, training, commercial-use and derivative permissions.
3. Test deterministic tools and approved models before training; record quality, latency, cost and failure modes.
4. Choose from-scratch training, approved base model, adapter, retrieval, classifier, tool-using system or hybrid based on evidence. Parameter count alone is not capability.
5. Version tokenizer, architecture, seed, optimizer, learning rate, batch/sequence settings, compute budget, checkpoints and stopping criteria.
6. Run only on an owner-approved backend, runner and budget. Log loss, throughput, memory, interruptions and checkpoint hashes.
7. Evaluate against baseline, validation, untouched holdout, task benchmarks, safety, robustness, latency, cost and multiple seeds where material.
8. Promote only with reproducible gains, no unacceptable regression, provenance, model card, security review, rollback and owner approval.
9. Monitor drift, incidents, cost and regressions; retrain only when evidence supports it.

Every division must have a governed path toward weight training, but should not blindly train an inferior or unlicensed model. Shared infrastructure or adapters are valid when licenses permit and results justify them. A deterministic tool or retrieval system may be better; record why. The roadmap is not evidence that all divisions have trained weights.

## 5. Productive learning sequence for each run

Optimize verified capability gain per unit of time and cost, subject to prerequisites, rights and safety:

1. Inspect the division contract, benchmark history, prior attempts and remediation.
2. Select one high-impact gap using severity, user value, dependency centrality, failure frequency and expected gain.
3. Find the shortest prerequisite chain and choose a primary source plus corroboration when needed.
4. Verify source freshness, revision, license, study, summary, evaluation and training permissions separately.
5. Create or update an original detailed guide; never infer lesson content from a title or metadata row.
6. Study bounded sections; extract concepts, procedures, assumptions, limitations, examples and decision rules.
7. Complete retrieval practice before viewing answers.
8. Build a sandbox exercise with measurable acceptance criteria.
9. Run the targeted benchmark; classify failures as knowledge, reasoning, tool use, data, integration, latency, cost or safety.
10. On failure, select the smallest corrective lesson. On success, run unseen transfer and regression tests.
11. Record artifacts, hashes, scores, time, compute/API cost, retries and review status.
12. Schedule spaced review at 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days; reopen mastery after regressions.

Five eligible lessons per division per day is a planning target, never a quota that overrides quality. Use bounded concurrency and pause when no rights-cleared work, evaluator, compute or budget exists. Weekly source/transcript evaluation is allowed only with verified source and evaluation permission plus a configured evaluator.

## 6. Complete source-summary standard

Apply this to every resource discovered in the repository: Hugging Face models/datasets/Spaces/papers; freeCodeCamp videos, authorized transcripts, certifications, projects and articles; O*NET occupations/tasks; official vendor documentation; standards; academic papers and open textbooks; government sources; maintained GitHub repositories; benchmark suites; and user-owned materials with purpose-specific consent.

Every resource record needs:
- Stable ID, source family/type, canonical URL, publisher, publication/update and retrieval dates, pinned revision, content hash and source locator.
- License/terms, attribution, provenance, jurisdiction when relevant, and separate study, summary, evaluation, training, commercial, derivative-model and redistribution decisions with evidence.
- Executive and detailed original summaries; section notes; key concepts/definitions; prerequisites; assumptions; limitations; misconceptions; unresolved questions and related concepts.
- Procedures, worked examples, inputs/outputs, tools/APIs, configuration, failure modes, troubleshooting, security/privacy and version differences.
- Learning objectives, prerequisite order, exercises, answer key, checks, lab, capstone, acceptance tests and unseen transfer task.
- Division/capability mappings with rationale, reviewer, review date, freshness/revalidation date and confidence.
- Benchmark relationships: purpose, tasks, metrics, baseline, target, test split, contamination risk, scoring and limitations.
- Learning evidence: assigned/studied/assessed state, learner-notes hash, exercise artifact, attempt IDs, score deltas, retention and remediation.
- Training eligibility separate from study and evaluation. A summary is not automatically training data; only original, authorized and reviewed assets with explicit training permission enter a weight-training manifest.

Missing permission or content remains visible as metadata-only, rights-pending, guide-pending, review-required or blocked. Never count it as complete.

## 7. Benchmark guide standard

Create an original guide for every benchmark referenced in the repository or added by discovery. Each guide must explain purpose, intended tasks/model, maintainer, canonical version, URL, terms/license and verification date; tasks, datasets, splits, sample counts, modalities, provenance and contamination risks; metrics, scoring, normalization, aggregation, confidence and reproducibility; hardware/software, runtime, latency, token/cost budget and API needs; baseline, comparable peers, target division and interpretation limits; setup, invocation, output schema, troubleshooting and result artifact; leakage safeguards, hidden holdouts and human review; and failure diagnosis with targeted remediation resources.

Benchmark coverage must include relevant subsets of general knowledge/reasoning, mathematics, science, coding, tool use/agents, instruction following, retrieval/groundedness, long context, multilingual, vision/audio/video, extraction, classification, forecasting, robustness, calibration, safety/security, privacy, fairness/accessibility, latency, throughput, memory, reliability, cost and energy. Add commercial task benchmarks for lead qualification, research completeness, workflow completion, proposal quality, support resolution, document accuracy, analytics correctness, conversion attribution and fulfillment quality. Label these as internal task benchmarks unless independently public.

Do not combine incomparable scores into a “best AI” number without documented weighting and uncertainty. Store benchmark version, harness, prompts, tools, seeds, model revision, data split and evaluator version with every result. One run or a self-authored test cannot establish benchmark leadership.

## 8. Evidence states

Keep these states distinct: discovered metadata only; revision unpinned; rights pending; study authorized; summary in progress; guide needs review; guide approved; queued not studied; studied unassessed; sandbox passed; target benchmark passed; holdout passed; regression passed; retention verified; mastered; training rights approved; training candidate; training running; checkpoint created; model evaluated; promotion approved; blocked; rejected.

Every transition needs evidence references and append-only events. A benchmark pass is not source permission; mastery is not training permission; a checkpoint is not a production model.

## 9. Repository-wide completeness report

Reconcile this standard with existing canonical registries; do not create duplicate catalogs. Join:
- Every tracked learning resource/source → summary or explicit pending reason.
- Every summary → divisions, capabilities, exercises, assessments, benchmark guides and review evidence.
- Every benchmark/config/test/workflow reference → guide, harness, result and limitations.
- Every canonical division → capability contract, ordered curriculum, daily plan, progress ledger, benchmark suite, model strategy and weight-readiness state.
- Every job/hustle/venture → buyer/problem, tools/software, workflow, acquisition, pricing/economics, fulfillment, risk/compliance and capstone.
- Every discovery outside known taxonomies → unclassified review queue.

Report discovered, unique, summarized, approved, studied, assessed, mastered, training-eligible, weight-trained, benchmarked, blocked and stale counts. Include denominators and inventory revision for every percentage. A README, count, generated plan or catalog entry never proves completion.

## 10. Acceptance criteria and limitations

Acceptance requires schema validation; inventory of repository references and discovery records; a complete guide or explicit pending/block reason for every discovered item; prerequisite-aware and cost-bounded daily plans; append-only progress; evidence-hash tests; capability and weight-readiness records for every division; a benchmark guide and executable harness or explicit not-implemented state for every benchmark; and CI checks for schemas, registries, crosswalks, freshness, links and evidence.

This standard defines the target. It does not itself create missing summaries, download transcripts, authorize datasets, run benchmarks, train weights, configure compute or prove production readiness.
