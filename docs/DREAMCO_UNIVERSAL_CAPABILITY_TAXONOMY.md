# DreamCo Universal Capability Taxonomy

**Ownership:** DreamCo-owned original framework.

**Purpose:** organize learning and capability evaluation across humans, AI models, robots, and combined teams. This is an extensible taxonomy, not a claim that every possible task has already been cataloged.

## Actor modes

The taxonomy distinguishes seven operating modes: human-only, AI-model-only, robot-only, human+AI, human+robot, AI+robot, and human+AI+robot. A curriculum can teach a capability in more than one mode without assuming that the actors have identical permissions or abilities.

## Capability domains

DreamCo's initial capability domains are discovery and research; information and memory; reasoning and decision-making; planning and orchestration; communication and collaboration; creation and design; software and systems; physical action and manipulation; operations and maintenance; evaluation and quality; safety and governance; and learning and adaptation.

## Division-specific instruction

Every canonical division receives its own six-module specialist course, five practical exercises, two projects, and four assessments. The curriculum is generated from DreamCo's own division registry and specialist bot profiles. External course providers are not learner-facing curriculum routes.

## Learning and evidence cycle

1. Understand the task and its acceptance criteria.
2. Decompose the task into observable steps.
3. Select the permitted actor, tools, and sandbox.
4. Execute and capture evidence.
5. Assess performance on unseen inputs.
6. If a benchmark fails, create targeted retraining and repeat the assessment.

A successful lesson completion is not mastery. Mastery requires two consecutive passing assessments, an unseen holdout pass, and a complete safety gate. Model-weight updates, paid compute, deployment, and certification remain separately gated.

## Truth boundary

Generated curricula are original DreamCo drafts requiring owner review. They do not establish that model weights have been trained, that a division is production-ready, or that a capability has passed its benchmark.
