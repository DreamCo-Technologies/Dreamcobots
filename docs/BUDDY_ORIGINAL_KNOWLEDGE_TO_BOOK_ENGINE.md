# Buddy Original Knowledge-to-Book Engine

## Goal

Buddy may study books, courses, papers, websites, databases, videos and other authorized sources, then create **new original educational works** that explain what Buddy learned.

This is a knowledge synthesis system, not a book-copying system.

## Pipeline

`source → verify rights → extract knowledge → separate facts/ideas/methods from expression → compare sources → synthesize → outline → write independently → attribution/source notes → originality checks → expert review → benchmark → publish`

## What Buddy should preserve

- Facts and factual relationships
- Publicly known concepts
- Mathematical principles and algorithms
- General methods and procedures
- Lessons learned from experiments
- Its own benchmark results
- Its own examples and explanations
- Properly licensed/adapted material where the license permits reuse

## What Buddy must not reproduce as its own

- Verbatim passages from copyrighted books
- Long distinctive passages
- Recreated illustrations, tables or other expressive material without permission
- A chapter-by-chapter substitute that merely tracks the original expression
- Proprietary course packs or restricted source material

U.S. Copyright Office guidance distinguishes copyright-protected expression from unprotected ideas, facts, methods and systems. This distinction is important, but it is not a blanket permission to copy a copyrighted work; Buddy must still respect protected expression and applicable licenses/exceptions.

## Original-writing requirements

Every generated book should:

1. Start from a new outline based on the learner's goal, not the source's chapter order.
2. Synthesize multiple independent sources whenever practical.
3. Use Buddy-created examples, exercises and case studies.
4. Explain concepts in Buddy's own structure and language.
5. Identify important disagreements between sources.
6. Preserve citations/source notes for factual claims.
7. Record licenses for adapted/open material.
8. Run an originality/provenance review before publication.
9. Never claim personal experience that Buddy did not actually have.
10. Distinguish established knowledge from Buddy's hypotheses or recommendations.

## Open-license handling

For MIT OpenCourseWare material, check the exact resource license before reuse. MIT OCW states that most OCW materials are under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, allowing sharing and adaptation subject to attribution, noncommercial use and share-alike requirements. Some third-party readings and other materials have different rights and are not covered by the OCW license.

Therefore the writer should attach:

- source URL
- source title/author
- license
- adaptation status
- attribution text when required
- publication restrictions

## Benchmarking the book

A book is not considered successful merely because it was generated. Buddy should test it by asking independent learners/models to solve unseen tasks based on the concepts and compare:

- factual accuracy
- reasoning quality
- teaching clarity
- practical usefulness
- citation quality
- originality/provenance compliance
- benchmark improvement after study
- accessibility

The strongest test is whether the book helps a learner **perform better on unseen tasks**, not whether it resembles the source material.
