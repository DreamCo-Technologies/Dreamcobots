# Buddy Work-to-Value Engine

## Primary mission
Buddy is designed to be a practical economic and productivity assistant for people who need income, better work, a business, a product, or a solution to a real-world problem. It should continuously discover legitimate opportunities and help turn approved ideas into measurable outcomes.

This is not a promise of guaranteed income. Buddy must distinguish opportunities, forecasts, experiments and observed results.

## Any job title becomes a capability map

A user may enter a personal or business job title such as `landscaper`, `bookkeeper`, `restaurant owner`, `mechanic`, `software developer`, `dispatcher`, `teacher`, `property manager`, `sales representative`, `manufacturer`, or another occupation.

Buddy creates:

1. Occupation profile
2. Task inventory
3. Required skills and tools
4. AI-capable tasks
5. AI-assisted tasks
6. Human-required tasks
7. External-system tasks
8. Approval-required tasks
9. Tasks that need licensed/certified professionals
10. Tasks that are currently unreliable for automation
11. Best models/agents for each task
12. Benchmarks needed to establish capability
13. Automation opportunities
14. Productivity opportunities
15. Revenue opportunities
16. Cost-saving opportunities
17. Business opportunities created by unmet needs
18. Recommended human + AI operating model

## AI employee blueprint

For a business role, Buddy can generate a transparent AI-employee blueprint containing:

- role description
- responsibilities
- AI tasks
- human tasks
- tools/integrations
- required permissions
- input/output contracts
- quality checks
- escalation rules
- benchmarks
- expected time savings
- estimated cost
- estimated revenue opportunity
- implementation plan
- supervision requirements
- audit trail

Buddy must never imply that an AI worker can legally perform a regulated or licensed human responsibility when professional oversight is required.

The engine continuously searches approved information sources and the user's own work context for:

- paid work
- contracts
- customers
- leads
- underserved markets
- business ideas
- software ideas
- app ideas
- website ideas
- game ideas
- educational products
- creative products
- scientific research opportunities
- engineering solutions
- inventions
- automation services
- consulting opportunities
- procurement opportunities
- partnerships
- licensing opportunities
- franchise opportunities
- cost reductions
- unused assets and skills
- repetitive work that can become a service
- successful workflows that can become products

New opportunities are continuously scored against user fit, evidence, feasibility, startup requirements, time to test, competition, risk, automation potential and expected value.

## From idea to company

Buddy can move an approved opportunity through:

`Problem → Customer → Research → Competitive map → Solution → Prototype → Pilot → Feedback → Economics → Operations → Compliance → Launch → Growth → Optimization`

Every stage has explicit evidence and a stop/revise/continue decision.

## Autonomous cash goal

The repository may support bounded automation that prepares or executes approved revenue workflows, but autonomous money movement, purchases, contracts, account creation, regulated actions and other consequential actions remain subject to explicit permissions, budgets, policy checks and auditability.

The objective is to maximize **useful autonomous work**, not to remove human control.

## Continuous user dashboard

Buddy should always be able to answer:

- What can I do today to get paid?
- What can AI do for my current job?
- What still needs me?
- What should I learn next?
- What business could I start with what I already have?
- What problem near me could become a business?
- What should I test first?
- What did we try before?
- What failed and what did we learn?
- What is newly available?
- What has actual evidence of demand or revenue?

## Success metrics

Track outcomes, not idea volume:

- opportunities discovered
- opportunities researched
- opportunities tested
- useful experiments completed
- time saved
- costs avoided
- interviews/leads generated
- applications submitted
- customers acquired
- revenue observed
- profit observed
- products launched
- businesses launched
- inventions prototyped
- successful automations
- repeatability
- user satisfaction
- false-opportunity rate
- safety/policy incidents

## Continuous learning rule

Every failed attempt should produce structured learning:

`attempt → result → failure category → evidence → changed hypothesis → next experiment`

Buddy should revisit old opportunities when conditions change rather than permanently discarding them after one failed attempt.
