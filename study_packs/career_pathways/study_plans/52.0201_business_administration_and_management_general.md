## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (business communication: reports, proposals, and email to stakeholders), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Active Listening (stakeholder interviews in consulting or capstone projects); Reading Comprehension (reading business cases and industry reports); Critical Thinking (comparing strategic options with evidence and trade-offs).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Administration and Management (management, organizational behavior, operations, and strategy); Customer and Personal Service (service operations and customer experience coursework); Computers and Electronics (business analytics tools and enterprise software).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Project Management Specialists; Management Analysts; Human Resources Specialists): Personnel and Human Resources (human resource management and employment law); Administrative (spreadsheet modeling and business information systems); Mathematics (business calculus, statistics, and quantitative decision-making). Keep building Speaking (presenting case analyses and leading team meetings); Writing (writing executive summaries and project proposals); Monitoring (tracking your own project metrics and team progress in a capstone) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Project Management Specialists; Management Analysts; Human Resources Specialists; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Administration and Management, Customer and Personal Service.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Education and Training (3.09): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.

### 52.0201-P1. `13-1082.00` Project Management Specialists, O*NET task 21462 (responsibility assignment matrix)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Give project team members their duties and responsibilities.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are the project specialist for moving a 60-person insurance office to a new building in eight weeks. The team: facilities coordinator, IT technician, HR generalist, an office manager, two volunteer 'floor captains', and the moving vendor. Build a responsibility assignment (RACI) matrix for the twelve main work packages, explain how you matched duties to people's skills and workloads, how you will confirm each person accepts their assignments, and how you will rebalance if someone becomes overloaded.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete, clear matrix: Each work package has exactly one accountable owner, and responsible, consulted and informed roles are sensible.
- Thoughtful assignment: Assignments reflect skills, authority and availability rather than convenience.
- Commitment and rebalancing: Includes a step for confirming assignments and a concrete method for detecting and fixing overload.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Work packages: floor plan, IT network and phones, furniture, vendor contract, employee communication, packing, security badges, parking, mail forwarding, go-live support.
- IT technician accountable for network cutover; HR for employee communication; facilities for vendor coordination.
- Kickoff meeting where owners confirm scope and capacity.
- Weekly status check of hours and task counts per person.
- Rebalance by shifting tasks to floor captains or adding vendor support.

### 52.0201-P2. `13-1111.00` Management Analysts, O*NET task 7276 (information-gathering plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Collect information about a business problem or process and organize it so it can be analyzed.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A regional hospital's outpatient clinic says patients wait too long for appointments and staff blame 'the scheduling system'. You are the management analyst assigned to find out what is really happening. Write the information-gathering plan: who you will interview and what you will ask, what data you will pull (scheduling records, no-show rates, provider templates), what you will observe in person, and how you will organize the findings (process map, issue log, data summary) so they can be analyzed next week.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Multiple sources: Combines interviews, system data and direct observation and includes front-line staff and patients, not only managers.
- Focused questions: Questions and data requests target the problem (lead time to appointment, template rules, no-shows, cancellations).
- Organized output: Specifies the structure for organizing information so it can be analyzed and verified.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Interviews: schedulers, nurses, two providers, clinic manager, a handful of patients.
- Data: days to third-next-available appointment, no-show and late cancel rates, template blocks by provider, call abandonment.
- Observation: shadow the scheduling desk for a morning.
- Organize: current-state process map, issue log tagged by cause, summary tables.
- Validate findings with a short readback session with staff.

### 52.0201-P3. `13-1071.00` Human Resources Specialists, O*NET task 18858 (recruiting strategy)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Plan and carry out recruiting approaches that meet current and expected staffing needs.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A growing regional logistics company must hire 40 warehouse associates and 6 shift supervisors in the next four months for a new distribution center, in a town with low unemployment. Write the recruiting strategy: the workforce need by role and month, target candidate sources, the employer value proposition you would promote, the selection process with time-to-hire targets, how you ensure fair and lawful hiring, and the metrics you will track weekly.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Need-based plan: Ties sourcing volume to the hiring target using realistic funnel assumptions (applicants per hire).
- Fit to labor market: Sources and messaging fit a tight local market (referral bonuses, community partners, shift flexibility, internal promotion for supervisors).
- Fairness and measurement: Includes consistent structured selection and lawful practices, plus weekly metrics such as time-to-fill and offer acceptance.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Hiring calendar: ramp by month to match the facility opening.
- Funnel: estimated applicants per hire for associates and supervisors.
- Sources: job boards, workforce centers, community colleges, referral bonus, internal postings for supervisors.
- Structured interviews with scored questions; consistent background-check policy.
- Metrics: applicants, interviews, offers, acceptances, 90-day retention.

### 52.0201-P4. `13-1082.00` Project Management Specialists, O*NET task 21463 (stakeholder requirements workshop)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Work with key stakeholders to pin down what a project must deliver and why.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A city library system wants a project to add self-service laptop lending kiosks at four branches. Stakeholders include branch managers, IT, the finance office, the library board, and patrons. Plan a two-hour requirements workshop and its outputs: the agenda, techniques you will use to draw out needs and conflicts, how you will separate objectives from solutions, a draft project charter section listing objectives with success measures, and how disagreements will be resolved.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Inclusive design: Agenda and techniques give all stakeholder groups a voice, including patron input gathered beforehand.
- Measurable objectives: Objectives are specific and measurable (e.g. availability rate, cost per loan, damage rate), separate from solution features.
- Conflict handling: States a clear decision process for conflicting requirements, such as a priority scheme and a sponsor decision.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Pre-work: short patron survey and branch usage data.
- Agenda: purpose, current-state stories, needs brainstorming, prioritization, constraints, next steps.
- Techniques: affinity grouping, MoSCoW prioritization, dot voting.
- Charter: objectives with targets, scope, constraints (budget, privacy), assumptions.
- Escalate unresolved conflicts to the project sponsor with options documented.

### 52.0201-P5. `13-1111.00` Management Analysts, O*NET task 7277 (analysis and options paper)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Make sense of the information collected and propose fixes or other ways forward.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented findings from the clinic study: average wait to a new appointment is 34 days; 18 percent of booked slots are no-shows; providers each block 4 hours a week that are often unused; schedulers spend 30 percent of their time on reminder calls; and appointment types have 11 different lengths. Write the analysis and options paper: root causes supported by the data, three alternative solutions (for example, automated reminders, template simplification, overbooking rules or open-access scheduling), the expected impact and cost of each, risks, and the recommended sequence.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Data-driven root causes: Root causes are linked to the evidence and quantified where possible (lost capacity from no-shows and unused blocks).
- Real alternatives: Options are distinct, with impact estimates, costs and risks.
- Implementable recommendation: Recommends a sequence with quick wins and a pilot, and how success will be measured.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Lost capacity estimate from no-shows and unused blocked time.
- Option 1: automated text reminders to cut no-shows and free scheduler time.
- Option 2: simplify appointment lengths to three or four types.
- Option 3: release unused blocked time 48 hours ahead; limited overbooking based on no-show prediction.
- Pilot in one clinic for eight weeks, measure wait time and no-show rate.

### 52.0201-P6. `13-1071.00` Human Resources Specialists, O*NET task 18861 (policy explanation brief and FAQ)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Make HR rules, workplace laws and company procedures understandable to employees and managers.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A 200-employee company is introducing a new paid leave policy because of a new state paid sick leave law, and managers are confused about how it interacts with existing vacation days and with federal family and medical leave. Write a one-page manager brief and a ten-question employee FAQ: what the policy provides, accrual and use rules, documentation that can and cannot be requested, non-retaliation, and where to get help. Note which points you would confirm with legal counsel, and invent no specific statutory numbers without labeling them as placeholders.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Accurate and careful: Explains rules clearly, labels placeholders for state-specific figures, and flags items needing legal confirmation.
- Audience fit: The manager brief focuses on what managers must do; the FAQ uses plain language employees can act on.
- Compliance safeguards: Covers non-retaliation, confidentiality of medical information and consistent application.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Brief: what changes, effective date, manager duties (approve, record, never discourage use).
- Accrual rate placeholder, carryover and caps, permitted uses including family care.
- Documentation: limits on asking for medical details; follow the law's thresholds.
- Interaction with vacation and family and medical leave explained with an example.
- FAQ ends with HR contact and a confidential reporting channel.
