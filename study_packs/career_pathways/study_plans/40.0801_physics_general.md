## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (scientific writing in the physics lab sequence: lab reports and a research paper), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading physics texts and research articles); Speaking (presenting problem solutions and research talks); Critical Thinking (estimating answers and checking units and limits).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Mathematics (calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, and mathematical methods for physics); Computers and Electronics (computational physics and electronics lab); Education and Training (physics teaching assistant or learning-assistant program).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Water Resource Specialists): Engineering and Technology (applied physics or engineering electives such as fluid mechanics or hydrology); Physics (classical mechanics, electricity and magnetism, quantum mechanics, and thermal physics with labs). Keep building Active Listening (collaborative problem-solving in study groups); Writing (writing lab reports with error analysis); Active Learning (learning new instruments and simulation codes in advanced labs) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Water Resource Specialists; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Mathematics, Computers and Electronics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Customer and Personal Service (3.27): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administration and Management (2.75): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).

### 40.0801-P1. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (physics classroom routines plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You teach high school physics with frequent hands-on labs (carts on ramps, projectile launchers, springs, a low-power laser). Write the classroom rules and routines plan: expectations stated positively, routines for lab groups, equipment checkout and return, a clear stop signal, what happens when a rule is broken during a launch activity, and how you will teach and practice these routines during the first two weeks.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Physics-lab specific: Rules address launch zones, eye protection, laser safety and equipment handling.
- Clear routines: Group roles, checkout and return procedures and signals are concrete.
- Consistent consequences: Responses are graduated for general behavior and immediate for safety risks, and routines are rehearsed.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Expectations: respect launch zones, goggles for projectiles and springs, never look into or point the laser at anyone.
- Group roles: materials manager, recorder, safety checker.
- Checkout sheet; equipment returned and counted before dismissal.
- Stop signal: lights off or a bell; hands off equipment.
- Safety violation leads to removal from the launch station with an alternative data-analysis task.

### 40.0801-P2. `11-9121.02` Water Resource Specialists, O*NET task 15615 (field crew supervision plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Supervise crews that withdraw water from groundwater wells and rivers.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are the water resource specialist supervising a three-person field crew running a 72-hour pumping test on a new municipal well and taking river-flow measurements nearby to see if pumping affects the river. Write the supervision plan: crew roles and shift schedule, equipment and calibration checks (pressure transducers, flow meter), the measurement schedule, safety (traffic, river wading, electrical generator), data recording and handoffs between shifts, and how you decide to stop or extend the test.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Organized crew work: Roles, shifts and handoffs cover 72 hours without gaps or fatigue risks.
- Measurement quality: Instruments are calibrated and the schedule captures rapid early drawdown and recovery.
- Safety and decisions: Covers field hazards with controls and defines stop or extend criteria.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Three 8-hour shifts with overlap for handoff; supervisor on call.
- Transducers in pumping and observation wells, calibrated beforehand; manual checks with a water-level meter.
- Log-spaced readings early, then hourly; recovery monitoring after shutdown.
- Safety: traffic cones, PFDs and no wading at high flow, generator grounding.
- Stop if discharge cannot be held constant or drawdown nears the pump intake.

### 40.0801-P3. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6652 (equipment safety instruction plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach students to use equipment and materials properly and supervise them so no one is hurt and nothing is damaged.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Before a unit on electric circuits, your physics students will use DC power supplies, breadboards, multimeters and small resistors that can get hot. Plan the instruction and supervision: a short demonstration of correct use, the common mistakes that cause injury or damage (short circuits, wrong meter setting), a 'license' check each student passes before independent use, how you position yourself and monitor during lab time, and what to do if a component overheats.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Accurate technical instruction: Explains correct use of power supplies and meters (current limit, measuring current in series, voltage in parallel).
- Verification before use: Students show competence in a check before independent use.
- Active supervision: Describes monitoring strategies and a clear response to overheating or smoke.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Demo: set current limit low, build circuit with power off, check before switching on.
- Mistakes: meter on current setting across a source, shorted leads, touching hot resistors.
- License check: build a simple circuit and measure voltage and current correctly.
- Teacher circulates with a scan pattern; groups raise a card for a circuit check.
- Overheating: switch off at the supply, do not touch, let cool, report.

### 40.0801-P4. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6640 (lesson plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach a subject through explanation, discussion and demonstration.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan a 50-minute physics lesson on Newton's second law. Include a demonstration (for example, a cart pulled by a hanging mass with a motion sensor or phone accelerometer), a short explanation that connects net force, mass and acceleration, a discussion that confronts the common belief that motion requires a continuing force, and a quick formative check. Explain how students use the demonstration's data in the explanation.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Physics accuracy: Correctly relates net force, mass and acceleration and addresses the force-implies-motion misconception.
- Data-driven demo: The demonstration produces data (acceleration for different masses) that students analyze.
- Formative check: Includes a meaningful check, such as predicting acceleration for a new mass, and adapts based on results.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Prediction prompt before the demo.
- Demo: vary hanging mass and cart mass; record acceleration.
- Plot acceleration against net force for constant mass; slope relates to mass.
- Discussion: a hockey puck on ice keeps moving without a push.
- Check: two quick prediction problems with mini whiteboards.

### 40.0801-P5. `11-9121.02` Water Resource Specialists, O*NET task 15617 (negotiation preparation brief)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Bargain with towns or water utilities over water rights so that supply covers demand.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: in a drought year, your regional water authority needs an extra 2 million cubic meters over the summer, and a neighboring irrigation district holds senior rights to the river. Prepare a negotiation brief: each side's interests and constraints, your best alternative if no deal is reached, options for a deal (temporary lease, fallowing payments, exchange of treated wastewater, dry-year option contracts), how you would value the water, legal or regulatory approvals required, and how you will communicate with affected farmers and residents.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Interest-based analysis: Identifies interests of both sides, not just positions, and a realistic alternative to agreement.
- Creative, valued options: Proposes several deal structures with a reasoned price range.
- Legal and community awareness: Notes required approvals under water law and plans fair, transparent communication.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Authority interests: reliable summer supply, cost; district interests: crop income, long-term rights, community.
- Alternative: emergency conservation and groundwater pumping.
- Options: one-year lease, payments for fallowing specific fields, recycled water exchange, multi-year dry-year option.
- Value using farm net income lost plus a premium; compare with alternative supply cost.
- Approvals from the state water agency; public meetings and written FAQs.

### 40.0801-P6. `11-9121.02` Water Resource Specialists, O*NET task 15618 (hydraulic model calculation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Model the hydrology, hydraulics or water quality of a system.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A town wants to know whether a grassy drainage channel can carry the peak runoff from a new 12-hectare housing development. Using stated, labeled assumptions, estimate the peak flow with the rational method (runoff coefficient, rainfall intensity for the design storm, area), then use Manning's equation to check whether a trapezoidal channel with given dimensions and slope can carry it. Show all calculations, discuss the sensitivity of the answer to the runoff coefficient and roughness, and state when a more detailed model would be needed.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct equations: Rational method and Manning's equation are applied with consistent units and correct geometry.
- Sensitivity: Shows how the result changes with plausible ranges of runoff coefficient and roughness.
- Model limits: Explains when a hydrograph or computer model (such as for larger areas or storage) is required.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Peak flow equals C times i times A with unit conversion; e.g. C around 0.5 for suburban lots.
- Channel area and hydraulic radius for the trapezoid; Manning flow with n around 0.03 to 0.04 for grass.
- Compare capacity with peak flow; freeboard.
- Sensitivity table for C and n.
- Rational method is limited to small catchments; use hydrograph modeling for detention design.
