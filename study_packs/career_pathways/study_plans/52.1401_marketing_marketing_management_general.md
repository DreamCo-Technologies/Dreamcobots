## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (copywriting and marketing communications), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading market research reports and platform documentation); Active Listening (customer interviews and focus group moderation); Critical Thinking (analyzing campaign data and testing assumptions).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Sales and Marketing (principles of marketing, consumer behavior, and marketing research); Customer and Personal Service (customer experience and service marketing); Computers and Electronics (digital marketing tools, web analytics, and CRM systems).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists; Fundraisers; Search Marketing Strategists): Communications and Media (advertising, social media strategy, and content production); Administration and Management (marketing management and campaign budgeting); Mathematics (marketing analytics: statistics, survey sampling, and A/B test analysis). Keep building Speaking (pitching campaign concepts to clients); Writing (writing creative briefs and campaign reports); Active Learning (learning new marketing platforms and analytics tools through certification courses) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists; Fundraisers; Search Marketing Strategists; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Sales and Marketing, Customer and Personal Service.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Administrative (2.97): ranked below the knowledge areas the outline already covers (the outline gives at most six course slots to knowledge); worth an elective if a target role needs it.

### 52.1401-P1. `13-1161.00` Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists, O*NET task 5433 (market segmentation study plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Gather and study data on customers' characteristics, preferences, needs and purchasing patterns to find promising markets and what drives demand.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A small company making plant-based protein snacks sells mostly through two grocery chains and wants to know which new customer groups to target next year. Design the research and a mock segmentation: research questions, data sources (store sales data, a customer survey, public census or category reports), the survey sample and key questions, the segmentation variables and method, three resulting segments described with clearly invented figures, and which segment you would prioritize and why.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Research design: Questions, sources and sample are appropriate and the survey avoids leading or double-barreled questions.
- Segmentation logic: Segments are based on meaningful variables (needs, usage occasions, price sensitivity, demographics) and are distinct and reachable.
- Prioritization: Chooses a segment using explicit criteria (size, growth, fit, competition, profitability) and notes data limits.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Questions: who buys, when and why, what alternatives, price tolerance.
- Sources: retailer scan data, loyalty-card summaries if available, a 600-person online panel survey, census demographics.
- Method: cluster analysis on needs and behavior variables, profiled by demographics.
- Example segments: fitness-focused snackers, busy parents buying for lunchboxes, flexitarian grazers.
- Priority segment with sizing logic and next step such as a test campaign.

### 52.1401-P2. `13-1131.00` Fundraisers, O*NET task 18907 (donor database design and cleanup plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set up or refresh the database that tracks donors.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. An animal shelter tracks 6,000 donors in three spreadsheets (event sign-ups, online gifts, and a volunteer list), with duplicates, missing addresses, and no record of who asked not to be mailed. Write a plan to create a single donor database in a low-cost donor-management tool: the record structure (individuals, households, gifts, communications, preferences), the deduplication and cleanup rules, how consent and do-not-contact preferences are stored, data-entry standards for staff, and three reports the development director will get monthly.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Sound data model: Separates people, households, gifts and interactions, and captures gift type, date, amount, campaign and soft credits.
- Data quality and consent: Has clear matching and merge rules and stores communication preferences and opt-outs so they are honored.
- Useful outputs: Monthly reports support fundraising decisions (retention, lapsed donors, campaign results).

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Entities: constituent, household, gift, pledge, campaign, communication preference.
- Matching on name plus email or address; manual review for uncertain matches; merge log.
- Opt-out and channel preferences as required fields; suppression lists applied to every mailing.
- Entry standards: naming format, address verification, gift acknowledgment within 48 hours.
- Reports: donor retention rate, lapsed donors over 12 months, gifts by campaign.

### 52.1401-P3. `13-1161.01` Search Marketing Strategists, O*NET task 16175 (marketing technology evaluation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Find, assess or buy the hardware and software needed to run online marketing campaigns.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A family-owned outdoor-gear store with an online shop wants to grow search traffic and run its first paid search campaigns with a tool budget of 300 per month. Evaluate the software it needs: list the functions required (keyword research, rank tracking, site audit, analytics, ad management, reporting), compare at least three real tool options or bundles on features, cost, learning curve and data privacy, describe a two-week trial plan, and recommend a stack within budget.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Needs-first evaluation: Starts from campaign needs and maps tools to functions rather than listing popular products.
- Fair comparison: Compares options on consistent criteria with current, verifiable pricing or noted as estimates.
- Trial and recommendation: Defines success measures for the trial and recommends a stack that fits budget and staff skills.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Functions mapped to free platform tools (search console, analytics, ad platform) plus one paid SEO suite.
- Comparison table with monthly cost, features, user seats, data handling.
- Trial: run a site audit, research 50 keywords, set up conversion tracking.
- Success: setup time, data accuracy versus platform data, ease of use for the owner.
- Recommendation and review after three months.

### 52.1401-P4. `13-1161.00` Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists, O*NET task 5434 (research findings report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Write up research findings, showing the data in charts and turning complicated results into clear prose.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented results: a survey of 800 gym members about a new app feature found 62 percent interested, but only 24 percent willing to pay 5 per month; interest was highest among members under 35 and lowest among those who visit fewer than twice a week; open-ended answers mention privacy worries. Write a two-page findings report for the product team: an executive summary with three key insights, two or three charts you would include (describe them, with correct chart types), the margin of error, what the data do not show, and recommendations.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear insight: Leads with decision-relevant insights rather than restating every number.
- Correct presentation: Chooses suitable charts, reports margin of error correctly for the sample, and avoids overstating subgroup differences.
- Honest limits and next steps: States limits (stated versus actual willingness to pay, sample bias) and gives actionable recommendations.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Insight: strong interest but low willingness to pay; freemium or bundling may fit better.
- Charts: bar chart of interest by age group, stacked bar of willingness to pay by visit frequency.
- Margin of error about plus or minus 3.5 points at 95 percent for the full sample; wider for subgroups.
- Limits: survey intent overstates purchase; members only, not prospects.
- Recommend a pricing test and addressing privacy in the feature design.

### 52.1401-P5. `13-1131.00` Fundraisers, O*NET task 18913 (online giving campaign plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Lead or coordinate fundraising that happens on the web, for example web auctions and giving pages.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A youth music nonprofit wants to raise 40,000 in a 24-hour online giving day plus a week-long online auction of donated items. Write the campaign plan: goal breakdown and donor targets, platform requirements (donation page, auction tool, payment fees, receipts), the communications calendar across email and social media, how matching gifts and peer-to-peer fundraisers are used, volunteer roles, and how you measure results and thank donors.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Realistic goal math: Breaks the goal into donor counts and average gifts by source with a plausible conversion assumption.
- Execution detail: Covers platform setup, fees, legal or tax receipt requirements and a day-by-day communications calendar.
- Stewardship and measurement: Includes metrics (donors, new donors, conversion, cost per dollar raised) and a prompt thank-you plan.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Goal split: matching gift pool, board giving, peer fundraisers, general donors, auction proceeds.
- Platform: mobile-friendly donation page, auction tool with bidding rules, fee coverage option, automatic receipts.
- Calendar: save-the-date, launch, hourly updates on the day, final push, results post.
- Volunteers: social media monitors, auction item intake, thank-you callers.
- Measure and thank within 48 hours; report impact to donors.

### 52.1401-P6. `13-1161.01` Search Marketing Strategists, O*NET task 16188 (link outreach plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Work with merchants, site owners, bloggers and online editors to earn well-placed links to a site.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. The outdoor-gear store from your earlier work published a detailed guide to choosing a first backpacking tent. Write a link-outreach plan for it: how you identify relevant sites (trail clubs, hiking blogs, local tourism pages), a short personalized outreach email, what value you offer in return, how you track contacts and results, and the practices you will avoid because they break search-engine guidelines or advertising disclosure rules (such as paying for followed links without disclosure).

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Relevant targeting: Targets sites whose audience genuinely benefits, with criteria for relevance and quality.
- Effective, honest outreach: Email is personalized, concise and offers real value without manipulation.
- Compliance and tracking: Avoids link schemes and undisclosed paid placements and tracks outreach in a simple log with outcomes.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Prospect list from searches for trail guides, club gear lists and local outdoor event pages.
- Email: specific reason the guide helps their readers, offer of a quote or local workshop.
- Value: expert input, discount for club members disclosed, guest article.
- Tracker with site, contact, date, response, link earned.
- Avoid buying links, link exchanges at scale, hidden links; paid placements marked as sponsored.
