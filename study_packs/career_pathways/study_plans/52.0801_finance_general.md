## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading annual reports and financial news daily); Critical Thinking (valuation case studies and stress-testing assumptions); Speaking (pitching stocks in a student investment club).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: English Language (financial writing: investment memos and credit reports); Economics and Accounting (financial accounting, corporate finance, investments, and macroeconomics); Mathematics (business statistics, financial mathematics, and spreadsheet modeling).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Financial and Investment Analysts; Credit Analysts; Loan Officers: Customer and Personal Service (client-facing practice in student investment funds or financial literacy programs); Law and Government (securities regulation and business law); Administration and Management (financial management and business strategy). Keep building Active Listening (mock client meetings in a financial planning course); Writing (writing equity research or credit memos) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Financial and Investment Analysts; Credit Analysts; Loan Officers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows English Language, Economics and Accounting.

### 52.0801-P1. `13-2051.00` Financial and Investment Analysts, O*NET task 21590 (financial model and memo)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Use financial models to solve financial problems or to judge how a transaction would affect finances or capital.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A regional coffee-roasting company (invented figures: revenue 12 million, EBITDA 1.8 million, debt 3 million) is deciding whether to buy a competitor for 6 million funded by a new term loan, expecting 400,000 in yearly cost savings after year one. Build a simple model (describe its structure and show key numbers): pro forma revenue and EBITDA, debt and leverage ratios, interest coverage, and a five-year discounted cash flow or payback view of the deal. Write a memo with your conclusion and a sensitivity table for savings and interest rate.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Model structure: Model has clear inputs, calculations and outputs, with assumptions stated and kept separate.
- Correct finance: Leverage, coverage and valuation figures are computed correctly and consistently.
- Decision-useful conclusion: Sensitivity analysis shows how the conclusion changes under plausible downside cases.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Inputs tab: prices, growth, savings ramp, loan rate and term.
- Pro forma EBITDA combined plus synergies; leverage equals total debt over EBITDA.
- Interest coverage equals EBITDA over interest; compare with lender comfort ranges.
- Net present value of incremental cash flows minus price; payback years.
- Sensitivity grid of savings achieved versus interest rate; recommendation with conditions.

### 52.0801-P2. `13-2041.00` Credit Analysts, O*NET task 1251 (credit risk report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Write reports that assess how risky it is to extend credit or lend money.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a regional bank is asked for a 2 million working-capital line by an auto-parts distributor whose largest customer accounts for 35 percent of sales, whose inventory has grown faster than sales, and whose current ratio fell from 1.8 to 1.3 in two years. Write the credit risk report: business and industry overview, financial ratio analysis (liquidity, leverage, efficiency), qualitative risks, a risk rating on a stated scale with justification, and the structure and covenants that would make the risk acceptable.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Ratio analysis: Uses relevant ratios (current ratio, inventory days, debt to equity, coverage) and interprets trends, not just levels.
- Risk identification: Recognizes customer concentration and inventory build-up as key risks and assesses their potential impact.
- Structuring: Proposes structure (borrowing base, covenants, reporting) that directly addresses the identified risks.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Overview: products, customers, supplier terms, cyclical auto sector.
- Ratios: declining liquidity, rising inventory days, leverage trend.
- Concentration risk: impact if the top customer leaves.
- Rating with rationale on the bank's scale.
- Structure: borrowing base tied to receivables and inventory with advance rates, minimum coverage covenant, monthly aging reports.

### 52.0801-P3. `13-2072.00` Loan Officers, O*NET task 3407 (loan decision memo)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Approve loans that fall within one's lending authority and send those above it to management.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are a loan officer with authority to approve consumer auto loans up to 40,000 with debt-to-income up to 43 percent and credit scores of 660 and above. Three invented applications arrive: (A) 28,000, DTI 38 percent, score 702; (B) 45,000, DTI 31 percent, score 760; (C) 22,000, DTI 46 percent, score 690 with a recent job change. Write a decision memo for each: approve, decline, or refer, with reasons tied to policy, any conditions, and how the referral to your manager is documented. Add a note on fair lending consistency.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct use of authority: Approves only within limits, refers B for amount, and handles C's DTI exception properly (refer or decline per policy).
- Reasoned decisions: Each decision cites the specific policy criteria and any compensating factors.
- Fair and documented: Applies criteria consistently regardless of protected characteristics and documents referrals and exceptions.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- A: within all limits; approve with standard verification of income and insurance.
- B: strong credit but over the 40,000 limit; refer to the manager with a recommendation.
- C: DTI above policy; refer as an exception with compensating factors or decline with an adverse action notice.
- Referral memo: summary, policy exception, recommendation.
- Fair lending: same criteria and documentation for every applicant.

### 52.0801-P4. `13-2051.00` Financial and Investment Analysts, O*NET task 21600 (industry briefing)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Report orally or in writing on economic trends, particular companies and whole industries.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Prepare a 10-minute investment-committee briefing on one publicly traded industry you choose (for example, regional airlines, or solar equipment). Using public sources (annual reports, government statistics, industry data), cover: current industry drivers, two companies compared on growth, margins and leverage, key risks over the next two years, and your view. Deliver it as slide titles with key points plus a one-page written summary, with every figure cited.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Evidence quality: Uses cited, current public data and distinguishes facts from forecasts and opinions.
- Comparative analysis: Company comparison uses consistent metrics and explains differences.
- Clear view: Ends with a defensible view, key risks, and what evidence would change the view.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Industry drivers such as fuel costs, demand trends, regulation or interest rates.
- Comparison table: revenue growth, operating margin, debt to EBITDA, free cash flow.
- Risks: cyclical demand, input costs, policy changes.
- View: overweight, neutral or underweight with reasoning.
- Sources listed with dates.

### 52.0801-P5. `13-2041.00` Credit Analysts, O*NET task 1258 (dispute resolution procedure)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Talk with customers to settle complaints and confirm credit and financial transactions.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a commercial customer of a bank says three wire transfers totaling 18,000 on its statement were never authorized, and also disputes a fee for exceeding its credit-line limit. As the credit analyst on the relationship team, write the procedure you would follow: immediate steps to protect the account, how you verify each transaction (authorization logs, callbacks, IP or device records), how the over-limit fee is reviewed, communication with the customer at each stage, and the final resolution letter.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Protective first steps: Secures the account promptly (block, credential reset) and escalates potential fraud to the right team.
- Verification rigor: Checks authorization evidence for each transfer and reviews the fee against the credit agreement and actual balances.
- Clear communication: Gives the customer timelines and a professional, specific resolution letter.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Freeze online wire capability; refer to the fraud team; preserve records.
- Pull wire authorization logs, approval user IDs, callback records, device data.
- Fee review: compare balances with the line limit and agreement terms; reverse if posted in error.
- Update the customer within set timeframes.
- Letter stating findings, any credits, and security recommendations such as dual approval.

### 52.0801-P6. `13-2072.00` Loan Officers, O*NET task 3408 (applicant interview guide)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Meet loan applicants to collect information for their applications and respond to their questions on how lending works.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A first-time home buyer couple, both in their twenties with one gig-economy income and one salaried income, books a meeting to apply for a mortgage. Write your interview guide: the information and documents you need (and why for each), how you explain the steps from application to closing, the questions they are likely to ask (down payment, rate locks, credit checks) with plain-language answers, how you handle variable gig income, and the follow-up checklist you hand them.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete information needs: Lists the documents and data needed and explains why, including how variable income is documented.
- Clear process explanation: Explains the stages and timelines accurately in plain language.
- Fair and helpful: Answers questions without steering or promising approval, and gives a clear follow-up checklist.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Documents: ID, pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, two years of 1099 and bank statements for gig income, asset statements.
- Process: application, disclosures, appraisal, underwriting, conditions, closing.
- Explain rate locks, down payment options, and credit inquiry effects.
- Gig income: typically averaged over two years; explain why.
- Checklist with dates and contact information.
