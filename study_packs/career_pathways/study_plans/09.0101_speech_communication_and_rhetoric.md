## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (rhetoric, persuasive writing, and editing), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Speaking (public speaking, debate, and broadcast practice); Writing (writing press releases, scripts, and speeches); Reading Comprehension (analyzing speeches and media texts).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Communications and Media (public relations, media writing, and digital media); Computers and Electronics (audio, video, and social media production tools); Sales and Marketing (persuasion, marketing communication, and audience analysis).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Public Relations Specialists; Writers and Authors; Broadcast Announcers and Radio Disc Jockeys): Telecommunications (digital and broadcast media channels: streaming, podcasting, and how messages travel across platforms). Keep building Active Listening (interviewing and audience research); Critical Thinking (rhetorical analysis of arguments); Active Learning (adapting to new media platforms by producing content on each) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Public Relations Specialists; Writers and Authors; Broadcast Announcers and Radio Disc Jockeys; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Communications and Media, Computers and Electronics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Customer and Personal Service (3.02): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administration and Management (2.99): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).
- Administrative (2.81): office procedures and records handling; usually learned on the job, so it gets no course slot.

### 09.0101-P1. `27-3043.00` Writers and Authors, O*NET task 22653 (client copy review meeting)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Talk with the client about the product, the ad concepts and approach, and what the copy should change.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You wrote three radio-spot scripts for a family-owned furniture store's spring sale, and the owner wants to 'make it louder and mention every brand'. Plan the client meeting and write the follow-up: questions to clarify goals and audience, how you explain why fewer messages work better in a 30-second spot, two revised directions you will propose, how you capture agreed changes and approvals, and the revised script for one direction.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Client understanding: Questions uncover goals, audience, offer details and legal requirements (sale terms).
- Persuasive guidance: Explains copy choices respectfully with reasons, offering options rather than refusing.
- Clear outcome: Agreed changes and approvals are documented, and the revised script fits 30 seconds.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Questions: who buys, what single reason to come in, sale dates and exclusions.
- Explain one big idea per spot and a memorable call to action; brand list on the website.
- Options: a warm family-business story or a focused price-and-urgency spot.
- Change log with approval sign-off and deadline.
- Revised script of about 75 words with sale dates and a clear call to action.

### 09.0101-P2. `27-3031.00` Public Relations Specialists, O*NET task 1764 (media inquiry protocol)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Answer information requests from journalists or assign the right spokesperson or source.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a water main break has closed the main campus of a community college for the day, and three reporters are calling. As the PR specialist, write the media response protocol: how inquiries are logged, who is authorized to speak on which topics, a holding statement you can release within 30 minutes, key messages and facts to confirm, how you handle questions you cannot answer yet, and when and how you issue updates.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Fast, accurate response: Holding statement is timely, factual and avoids speculation.
- Clear spokesperson roles: Authorizes the right people for each topic and routes reporters accordingly.
- Ongoing updates: Defines an update schedule, channels and how corrections are handled.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Inquiry log: outlet, reporter, deadline, question, response.
- Spokespeople: college president for decisions, facilities director for technical details, PR for logistics.
- Holding statement: what happened, safety, closure, where updates are posted.
- Unknowns: say what is being checked and when more will be shared.
- Updates at set times on the website and social channels; final statement on reopening.

### 09.0101-P3. `27-3011.00` Broadcast Announcers and Radio Disc Jockeys, O*NET task 3924 (broadcast news rewrite)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Write and present on-air news, sports or weather items, collecting source material and reworking it so listeners get the facts within the time slot.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Take a real recent press release from a local government or university (cite it) and rewrite it as (1) a 30-second radio news story and (2) a 15-second headline version. Follow broadcast style: short sentences, active voice, attribution first, numbers rounded and written for the ear, phonetic spelling of difficult names. Then note what you would verify or add by calling a source before air, and time your read.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Broadcast style: Copy is conversational, active, uses attribution first and is written for listening.
- Accurate condensation: Keeps the most important facts and does not distort the source.
- Verification and timing: Lists facts to confirm and shows that the read fits the time slots.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Lead with the most important change for listeners.
- Attribution before claims: 'City officials say...'.
- Round numbers; avoid lists; phonetic guide for names.
- Verify dates, numbers and quotes with the source office.
- Timed read: about 75 words for 30 seconds, 35 to 40 for 15.

### 09.0101-P4. `27-3043.00` Writers and Authors, O*NET task 22666 (long-form outline)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Plan the structure of a writing project and organize the material to fit it.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are writing a 2,500-word magazine feature on how a small town rebuilt its main street after a flood. You have 14 interview transcripts, city records, and photographs. Create the project plan and outline: the central angle, the structure (opening scene, nut graf, sections), where each source and quotation fits, what you still need, a fact-checking plan, and a writing schedule to deadline.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Strong structure: Clear angle, nut graf and section logic suited to a feature.
- Material organization: Maps sources and quotations to sections and identifies gaps.
- Process plan: Includes fact-checking and a realistic schedule.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Angle: the rebuild as a story of choices and trade-offs, not just recovery.
- Opening scene with a business owner on the morning after the flood.
- Sections: the flood, the debate over rebuilding, money and rules, what changed, what remains.
- Gaps: insurance figures, city engineer interview, before-and-after data.
- Fact-check names, numbers and timeline; schedule draft, edit and revision dates.

### 09.0101-P5. `27-3031.00` Public Relations Specialists, O*NET task 1765 (stakeholder relations plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Build and keep cooperative relationships with community, consumer, employee and public-interest groups.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A regional hospital plans to expand its emergency department, which will mean two years of construction next to a residential neighborhood. Write a stakeholder relations plan: map stakeholder groups (neighbors, patient advocates, employees, local businesses, city officials), what each cares about, the channels and frequency for each, a community advisory group design, how feedback is tracked and answered, and how you will measure trust over time.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Stakeholder mapping: Identifies groups with their interests and influence and tailors engagement.
- Two-way engagement: Emphasizes listening, response and follow-through, not just announcements.
- Measurable relationships: Defines indicators such as complaint resolution time, survey trust scores and advisory group participation.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Map: neighbors (noise, traffic, parking), patients (access during construction), employees (parking, safety).
- Channels: monthly newsletter, construction hotline, text alerts, quarterly open house.
- Advisory group with neighbors and advocates; published meeting notes.
- Feedback tracker with response times.
- Indicators: complaints resolved within a set time, semiannual survey, attendance.

### 09.0101-P6. `27-3011.00` Broadcast Announcers and Radio Disc Jockeys, O*NET task 3928 (interview research brief)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Research background information to get ready for programs and interviews.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You will host a 10-minute live radio interview with a real local official or researcher of your choice about a current issue. Prepare the research brief: the guest's background from reliable sources (cited), the issue's key facts and competing viewpoints, five open-ended questions in a logical order with follow-ups, one fact you checked twice, pronunciation notes, and how you would handle an evasive answer on air.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Reliable research: Uses credible, cited sources and presents the issue's viewpoints fairly.
- Effective questions: Questions are open-ended, ordered logically and include follow-ups.
- Live readiness: Includes verification, pronunciation and a respectful strategy for evasive answers.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Guest profile with role, track record and relevant statements, cited.
- Issue background with numbers and timeline.
- Questions from context to tough accountability question with follow-ups.
- Double-checked fact with two sources.
- Evasion strategy: polite restatement, specific follow-up, note for listeners.
