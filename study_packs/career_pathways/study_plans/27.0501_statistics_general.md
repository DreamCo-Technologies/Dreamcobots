## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (statistical reporting and technical writing), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Critical Thinking (checking model assumptions and data quality); Reading Comprehension (reading statistical methods papers and documentation); Mathematics (derivations and simulation exercises).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Mathematics (probability, mathematical statistics, regression, and linear algebra); Computers and Electronics (statistical programming in R or Python and SQL); Economics and Accounting (economics and financial mathematics, useful for actuarial and business roles).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Data Scientists; Actuaries; Business Intelligence Analysts): major electives. Keep building Active Listening (clarifying research questions with clients); Speaking (presenting analyses to non-statisticians); Writing (writing statistical analysis reports for non-technical readers) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Data Scientists; Actuaries; Business Intelligence Analysts; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Mathematics, Computers and Electronics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Administration and Management (2.84): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).
- Customer and Personal Service (2.76): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administrative (2.52): office procedures and records handling; usually learned on the job, so it gets no course slot.
- Education and Training (2.48): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.

### 27.0501-P1. `15-2051.00` Data Scientists, O*NET task 21823 (public health data analysis)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Analyze and process large datasets with statistical software.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Using a large public health dataset (for example, the CDC's behavioral risk factor survey with several hundred thousand respondents), analyze the relationship between hours of sleep and self-reported poor mental health days. Write the analysis report: how you load and subset the data, why and how you apply survey weights and design variables, handling of missing and 'don't know' responses, the model used, results presented with confidence intervals, and limitations. Cite the data source and year.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct survey methods: Uses survey weights and design variables properly for population estimates and standard errors.
- Data handling: Recodes special response codes and handles missing data transparently.
- Valid interpretation: Interprets associations without causal claims and notes self-report and cross-sectional limits.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Load the annual file; subset adults with valid sleep and mental health items.
- Recode codes for don't know and refused to missing; define sleep categories.
- Survey-weighted estimates with strata and cluster variables.
- Model: survey-weighted regression with age, sex and other controls.
- Limitations: cross-sectional, self-reported, possible reverse causation.

### 27.0501-P2. `15-2011.00` Actuaries, O*NET task 3502 (pricing and soundness exercise)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Design and review insurance, annuity and pension products, checking that they are financially sound and setting premiums.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A startup wants to sell one-year accident-and-illness pet insurance for dogs with a 250 deductible and 80 percent reimbursement up to 5,000. Using clearly invented claim data (claim frequency and average claim by age band), compute a pure premium per age band, add expense and profit loads to get a gross premium, test sensitivity to a 20 percent rise in claim frequency, and write a memo on whether the plan is financially sound, what data you would need before launch, and what regulatory review applies.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct premium math: Pure premium equals frequency times severity after deductible and coinsurance; loads are applied correctly.
- Risk and sensitivity: Sensitivity analysis is computed and its effect on soundness explained, including adverse selection.
- Professional judgment: Memo identifies data gaps and that rates and forms need regulatory filing and actuarial sign-off.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Expected paid claim: frequency times (severity minus deductible) times 0.8, capped.
- Gross premium equals pure premium divided by (1 minus expense and profit ratio).
- Sensitivity: 20 percent frequency increase raises the loss ratio by about the same proportion.
- Risks: older dogs, breed effects, anti-selection, small data.
- Need credible claims data or reinsurance; state filing and qualified actuary review.

### 27.0501-P3. `15-2051.01` Business Intelligence Analysts, O*NET task 16134 (dashboard support ticket resolution)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Give technical help for existing reports, dashboards and other analysis tools.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented ticket: the sales director says the regional revenue dashboard shows 4.2 million for last month while the finance report shows 4.6 million, and she presents to the board tomorrow. Write how you would handle the ticket: the first response to the director, a step-by-step diagnosis (filters, refresh status, definitions such as booked versus invoiced revenue, time zones, currency, returns), how you document the root cause and fix, and how you prevent this kind of mismatch in the future.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Responsive communication: Acknowledges urgency, sets expectations and gives a timeline.
- Systematic diagnosis: Checks likely causes in a sensible order and reconciles the two numbers to the source.
- Fix and prevention: Documents root cause and adds definitions, reconciliation checks or data alerts.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Reply within the hour with a plan and time of next update.
- Check refresh timestamp, filters, date ranges, revenue definition, returns and currency conversion.
- Reconcile both figures to the general ledger; identify the gap's components.
- Fix dashboard logic or add a note; tell the director which number to use for the board and why.
- Prevention: shared metric definitions, automated daily reconciliation, data freshness label.

### 27.0501-P4. `15-2051.00` Data Scientists, O*NET task 21836 (stakeholder recommendation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Recommend data-based solutions to key stakeholders.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented results: an online bookstore ran a three-week A/B test of free shipping over 25 versus over 35. Conversion rose from 3.1 to 3.4 percent (about 120,000 visitors per arm), average order value fell from 41 to 37, and shipping costs per order rose by 0.80. Write a one-page recommendation to the executive team: statistical significance and confidence intervals, the profit impact per 1,000 visitors under stated margin assumptions, risks such as novelty effects, and your recommendation with a follow-up plan.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct statistics: Tests the conversion difference and reports uncertainty correctly for the stated sample sizes.
- Business translation: Converts results into profit per visitor with explicit margin assumptions.
- Clear recommendation: Makes a decision-oriented recommendation that weighs uncertainty and proposes follow-up.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Difference 0.3 points; standard error from both proportions; significant at the stated sample size.
- Revenue per 1,000 visitors: 34 orders times 37 versus 31 orders times 41.
- Profit using a margin assumption minus extra shipping cost.
- Risks: novelty, seasonality, effects on repeat purchases.
- Recommendation depends on profit result; follow-up test of threshold 30.

### 27.0501-P5. `15-2011.00` Actuaries, O*NET task 3501 (rate estimation exercise)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Estimate rates of death, accidents, illness, disability and retirement from statistical data.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented data: a pension plan observed 2,000 members aged 60 to 64 for three years with total exposure of 5,700 person-years and 34 deaths, and 1,500 members aged 65 to 69 with 4,200 person-years and 52 deaths. Estimate the central death rate for each age band with a confidence interval, compare them with a published national life table (cite which one you would use) via actual-to-expected ratios, explain whether the plan's data are credible enough to use alone, and recommend how to blend plan experience with the standard table.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct estimates: Computes rates as deaths over exposure and gives a sensible interval (e.g. Poisson-based).
- Comparison: Calculates actual-to-expected ratios against a cited standard table correctly.
- Credibility judgment: Discusses credibility given the small number of deaths and recommends a reasoned blending approach.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Rates about 0.0060 and 0.0124 per person-year.
- Poisson interval: deaths plus or minus about 1.96 times the square root of deaths, divided by exposure.
- Expected deaths from the table's rates times exposure; actual-to-expected ratios.
- Limited credibility with under 100 deaths per band.
- Blend with credibility weighting or apply a scaling factor to the standard table.

### 27.0501-P6. `15-2051.01` Business Intelligence Analysts, O*NET task 16140 (data delivery schedule and service levels)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Make sure business-intelligence information reaches its users on time.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A regional grocery chain's BI team delivers nightly sales dashboards, a weekly inventory report and monthly finance extracts to 300 users, and data have arrived late four times this month. Write a plan to manage the flow: map the data pipeline steps and dependencies, set delivery times and service levels for each product, define monitoring and alerting for late or failed loads, a communication process when data are late, and a monthly review of reliability metrics.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Pipeline understanding: Maps sources, transformations and dependencies and identifies likely bottlenecks.
- Clear service levels: Each product has a delivery time, freshness indicator and owner.
- Monitoring and communication: Includes automated alerts, status notices to users and reliability metrics with review.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Pipeline: point-of-sale export, warehouse load, transformations, dashboard refresh.
- Service levels: dashboards by 6:00, inventory Monday 8:00, finance on business day 2.
- Alerts if a job fails or runs late; on-call rotation.
- User status banner and email when data are late, with estimated time.
- Monthly on-time rate, incidents and root causes reviewed with stakeholders.
