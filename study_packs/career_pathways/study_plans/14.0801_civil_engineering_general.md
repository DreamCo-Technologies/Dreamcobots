## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading building codes, design standards, and plan sets); Speaking (presenting projects at public-meeting-style mock hearings); Critical Thinking (evaluating design alternatives for cost, safety, and sustainability).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Engineering and Technology (statics, mechanics of materials, fluid mechanics, geotechnical, and structural engineering); Design (civil CAD and BIM, steel and concrete design, and a capstone site design); Mathematics (calculus through differential equations, statistics, and numerical methods).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Civil Engineers; Transportation Engineers; Water/Wastewater Engineers: Building and Construction (construction materials, methods, estimating, and site visits); English Language (technical reports and permit-style documentation); Physics (calculus-based mechanics with labs, applied later to structures, fluids, and soils). Keep building Active Listening (stakeholder interviews in community-based design projects); Writing (writing calculation packages and design reports) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Civil Engineers; Transportation Engineers; Water/Wastewater Engineers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Engineering and Technology, Design.

### 14.0801-P1. `17-2051.00` Civil Engineers, O*NET task 147 (design calculation package)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Work out loads, slopes, flows and stresses in materials so the design requirements can be fixed.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A park needs a 6 m long timber footbridge, 1.5 m wide, carrying a pedestrian live load of 4 kPa plus its own dead load. Prepare a calculation package for the two main beams: load takedown, maximum bending moment and shear, required section modulus for a stated allowable bending stress, deflection check against span/360, and a short note on what a full design would also check (lateral stability, connections, durability, local code requirements). Show units throughout.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct loads and statics: Tributary width, load combination and moment and shear for a simply supported beam are calculated correctly.
- Sizing and serviceability: Section is sized from the section modulus and checked for deflection with a stated modulus of elasticity.
- Professional completeness: Identifies the remaining checks and that the design must follow the governing code and be reviewed by a licensed engineer.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Each beam carries half the deck: live load 4 kPa times 0.75 m plus dead load estimate.
- Uniform load w; moment w L squared over 8; shear w L over 2.
- Required section modulus equals moment over allowable stress; select a glulam or sawn size.
- Deflection 5 w L to the fourth over 384 E I compared with 6000/360 mm.
- Other checks: bearing, connections, lateral torsional buckling, vibration, preservative treatment.

### 14.0801-P2. `17-2051.01` Transportation Engineers, O*NET task 16303 (public meeting presentation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Share data, maps and other information at public meetings or hearings about construction projects.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. The city proposes converting a four-lane arterial into two travel lanes, a center turn lane and bike lanes (a 'road diet'). Neighbors are worried about traffic backing up. Prepare a 10-minute presentation outline for the public hearing: the problem the project addresses with crash and volume data (use clearly invented example figures), maps or diagrams you will show, expected effects on travel time, how you will answer the three most likely objections, and how public comments will be recorded and answered.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear evidence: Presents crash, speed and volume data in understandable visuals with honest labeling of estimates.
- Responsive to concerns: Anticipates objections (congestion, emergency access, business deliveries) with evidence-based answers.
- Transparent process: Explains next steps and how comments influence the decision.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Slides: existing cross-section and proposed cross-section diagrams, corridor map with crash locations.
- Data: daily volume within the range where road diets usually work, crash history by type.
- Expected travel-time change at peak hours from a traffic model, with uncertainty.
- Answers on emergency vehicles using the center lane, loading zones, and a monitoring period.
- Comment cards and an online form; responses published before the council vote.

### 14.0801-P3. `17-2051.02` Water/Wastewater Engineers, O*NET task 16324 (technical report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Produce technical reports and articles about developing water supplies or cutting water waste.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A small town water utility produces 2.0 million liters per day but bills customers for only 1.5 million. Write a technical report (executive summary and four sections, about two pages) on the water loss: a water balance using the standard categories of real and apparent losses, likely sources in this system (with invented but plausible figures), the field and data work needed to confirm them, the costs and savings of three reduction options, and a recommended plan.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Sound water balance: Breaks non-revenue water into real losses (leaks, main breaks) and apparent losses (meter error, unbilled use) with consistent numbers.
- Evidence plan: Specifies how losses will be confirmed (district metering, acoustic leak detection, meter testing).
- Decision support: Options have costs, savings and payback, and the recommendation follows from them; the summary is understandable to the town council.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Non-revenue water 0.5 million liters per day, 25 percent of production.
- Split estimate: leakage, customer meter under-registration, unbilled authorized use such as flushing.
- Field work: night flow analysis, acoustic survey, testing a sample of old meters.
- Options: leak detection and repair, meter replacement, pressure management; cost and payback each.
- Recommendation and a monitoring indicator for annual reporting.

### 14.0801-P4. `17-2051.00` Civil Engineers, O*NET task 148 (site inspection checklist)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Visit project sites to track progress and confirm the work matches the design and meets safety and sanitation requirements.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are the owner's field engineer on a two-story school addition, and tomorrow the contractor pours the second-floor concrete slab. Create the pre-pour and during-pour inspection checklist: items to verify against drawings and specifications (rebar size, spacing, cover, embedded items), concrete delivery and testing checks, site safety items, what you photograph and record, and what you do if you find nonconforming work an hour before the pour.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Spec conformance: Items reference the design documents and include measurable checks (bar size and spacing, cover, slab thickness, mix ticket).
- Safety and testing: Includes fall protection, shoring and formwork checks, and field testing of concrete (slump, temperature, cylinders).
- Handling nonconformance: Defines a clear authority and procedure for stopping or delaying the pour and documenting the issue.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Rebar per drawings, chairs for cover, laps, openings and embedded conduits in place.
- Formwork and shoring inspected; edge protection; access routes.
- Delivery tickets: mix design, time since batching, water added; slump and air tests; cylinders made per specification.
- Daily report with photos, weather, crew counts.
- If nonconforming: notify the contractor and engineer of record, issue a written notice, re-inspect before pour proceeds.

### 14.0801-P5. `17-2051.01` Transportation Engineers, O*NET task 16304 (traffic impact review)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Review development plans to estimate how they will affect traffic.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A developer submits plans for a 220-unit apartment complex with one driveway onto a two-lane collector road near an elementary school. Write the city's traffic impact review memo: estimate weekday peak-hour trips using a stated trip-generation rate (cite the source type you would use and label invented rates), distribute trips to the network, identify intersections and pedestrian routes to check, list conditions of approval you might require, and note what you would ask the developer's consultant to revise.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Trip estimate: Uses a stated rate per unit for AM and PM peaks, shows the arithmetic, and distributes trips reasonably.
- Safety focus: Considers school pedestrian routes, sight distance at the driveway and turn-lane needs.
- Actionable conditions: Proposes specific, justified conditions or study revisions rather than general concerns.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Rate assumption such as about half a trip per unit in each peak hour, giving roughly 110 peak trips.
- Distribution by direction based on the street network and destinations.
- Check the driveway, two adjacent intersections and the school crossing.
- Conditions: left-turn lane if warranted, sidewalk connection, crosswalk improvements, sight-triangle clearance.
- Ask for counts taken when school is in session and a queuing analysis.

### 14.0801-P6. `17-2051.02` Water/Wastewater Engineers, O*NET task 16329 (water efficiency program plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Create plans for new water supplies or for programs that make water use more efficient.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A growing town of 30,000 people expects summer demand to exceed its well capacity within six years. Develop a water efficiency program plan as an alternative or complement to drilling a new well: baseline demand by sector (invented but labeled figures), four efficiency measures (for example, irrigation scheduling, fixture rebates, tiered pricing, leak repair), estimated savings and cost per unit of water saved for each, an implementation timeline, and how you would measure actual savings.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Baseline and targets: States baseline demand by sector and a savings target linked to the capacity gap.
- Comparable measures: Each measure has estimated savings and cost per unit saved so they can be compared with the cost of a new well.
- Measurement and verification: Savings are measured against weather-normalized baseline data, not assumed.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Baseline: residential indoor, outdoor irrigation peak, commercial, and losses.
- Measures: smart irrigation controllers, rebates for high-efficiency fixtures, seasonal tiered rates, leak repair program.
- Cost per cubic meter saved for each versus the new well's cost.
- Phased timeline over three years with outreach.
- Monthly billing data compared with a weather-normalized baseline; annual report to council.
