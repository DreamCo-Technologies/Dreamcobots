## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (business writing: audit memos, client letters, and financial statement notes), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading accounting standards and audit guidance); Critical Thinking (evaluating evidence in auditing case studies); Active Listening (client interviews in volunteer tax preparation programs).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Economics and Accounting (financial and managerial accounting, intermediate accounting, auditing, and taxation); Mathematics (business statistics and financial mathematics); Administration and Management (management principles and accounting information systems).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Accountants and Auditors; Budget Analysts; Credit Analysts): Administrative (spreadsheet modeling and accounting software practice); Law and Government (business law and tax regulation); Customer and Personal Service (client service in accounting: communicating findings to clients, a VITA tax-clinic shift, or an audit internship). Keep building Speaking (presenting financial analyses in case competitions); Mathematics (working accounting problem sets: ratios, present value, and cost allocation); Active Learning (keeping up with new accounting standards through case updates and CPA-review material) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Accountants and Auditors; Budget Analysts; Credit Analysts; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Economics and Accounting, Mathematics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Computers and Electronics (3.06): ranked below the knowledge areas the outline already covers (the outline gives at most six course slots to knowledge); worth an elective if a target role needs it.

### 52.0301-P1. `13-2031.00` Budget Analysts, O*NET task 3394 (budget variance analysis)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Review a department's monthly budget and accounting reports to keep spending under control.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented data: a county parks department's year-to-date report at the end of month 7 (of 12) shows: personnel 4.1 of 7.2 million budgeted, seasonal staff 0.62 of 0.80 million, utilities 0.55 of 0.84 million, equipment repair 0.31 of 0.36 million, and program revenue 0.95 of 2.0 million expected. Write a variance analysis memo for the parks director: compute expected spending at this point (straight-line and, where it applies, seasonal), identify which lines are on track or at risk, explain likely causes and questions to ask, project year-end results, and recommend controls.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct variance math: Calculates percentage used versus elapsed time correctly and adjusts for seasonality where it obviously applies (seasonal staff, summer program revenue).
- Insightful diagnosis: Separates timing differences from real overruns and names plausible causes and follow-up questions.
- Actionable controls: Recommends specific controls (hiring freeze on certain lines, purchase approvals, revenue review) and a year-end projection.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Elapsed time about 58 percent; personnel at 57 percent is on track.
- Seasonal staff at 78 percent may be normal after summer but must be checked against the season calendar.
- Equipment repair at 86 percent is a real overrun risk; ask about aging mowers or one-time repairs.
- Program revenue at 48 percent with peak season over suggests a shortfall; project year-end.
- Controls: pre-approval for repairs above a threshold, monthly revenue review, possible mid-year budget amendment.

### 52.0301-P2. `13-2011.00` Accountants and Auditors, O*NET task 21505 (audit findings report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Write thorough reports on what an audit found.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented engagement: an internal audit of a university's purchasing cards (500 cardholders) tested 60 transactions and found 7 without receipts, 3 split purchases that avoided the bid threshold, 2 personal purchases later repaid, and approvers who signed off on their own statements in one department. Write the audit findings report: an executive summary, each finding written as condition, criteria, cause, effect and recommendation, a risk rating for each, management's response section (with placeholders), and the follow-up timeline.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Standard finding structure: Each finding states condition, criteria (the policy breached), cause, effect and recommendation clearly.
- Risk judgment: Ratings reflect severity and pervasiveness; self-approval and split purchases are treated as control failures, not paperwork issues.
- Professional tone and follow-up: Factual, non-accusatory language, room for management responses, and dates for follow-up testing.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Executive summary with overall conclusion and the top two risks.
- Missing receipts: 7 of 60 (about 12 percent) against the receipt policy; cause and recommendation.
- Split purchases avoiding bid rules: high risk; recommend analytics to detect splitting.
- Self-approval: segregation-of-duties failure; recommend system-enforced approver hierarchy.
- Personal use repaid: policy violation; recommend training and card suspension rules; follow-up in six months.

### 52.0301-P3. `13-2041.00` Credit Analysts, O*NET task 1254 (credit memo for loan committee)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Put together loan applications, including the credit analysis and a summary of the request, and forward them for the loan committee's decision.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a family-owned bakery with three years of tax returns (revenue 820k, 910k, 1.04 million; net income 41k, 55k, 72k), existing debt payments of 3,000 per month, and a request for 180,000 over seven years to buy a second oven line. Write the credit memo you would submit to the loan committee: the request summary, analysis of profitability, cash flow and debt service coverage (show the calculation), collateral and guarantees, key risks and mitigants, and your recommendation with any conditions.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Sound financial analysis: Calculates a debt service coverage ratio from cash available for debt service and new plus existing payments, with assumptions stated.
- Risk assessment: Identifies business-specific risks (owner dependence, ingredient costs, single location) and mitigants.
- Committee-ready recommendation: Clear recommendation with conditions, such as covenants or a personal guarantee, consistent with the analysis.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Summary: borrower, purpose, amount, term, rate assumption, repayment source.
- Trend: revenue up about 27 percent over two years; margins improving.
- Debt service: estimate the new monthly payment; coverage equals cash flow (net income plus depreciation and interest) over total annual debt payments; compare with a typical minimum near 1.25.
- Collateral: equipment lien; personal guarantee from owners.
- Recommend approval with a coverage covenant and annual financial statements if coverage is adequate.

### 52.0301-P4. `13-2031.00` Budget Analysts, O*NET task 3395 (budget reporting workplan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Lead the preparation of routine and special-purpose budget reports.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are the budget analyst who must deliver two products for a mid-size nonprofit: the regular quarterly budget-to-actual report for the board finance committee, and a special report on the cost of opening a new after-school site. Write the workplan: the data you need and from whom, the report templates and key tables for each, a timeline working back from the committee meeting date, review and sign-off steps, and how you will explain the numbers to board members without finance backgrounds.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear workplan: Tasks, owners, data sources and deadlines are specific and work back from the meeting date.
- Fit-for-purpose content: The quarterly report and the special report have different, appropriate structures (variance tables versus a cost model with assumptions).
- Quality and communication: Includes reconciliation and review steps and a plain-language narrative for non-finance readers.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Data: general ledger close, payroll, program enrollment, grant restrictions; owners in accounting and programs.
- Quarterly: budget versus actual by program, variance explanations above a threshold, cash position.
- Special: start-up and annual operating costs, staffing ratios, funding sources, break-even enrollment, sensitivity table.
- Timeline: close plus five days, draft, director review, CFO sign-off, packet sent one week before meeting.
- Narrative: three key messages and a one-page summary chart.

### 52.0301-P5. `13-2011.00` Accountants and Auditors, O*NET task 21508 (accounting system review checklist)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Examine the books and accounting systems to judge whether transactions are recorded efficiently, effectively and according to accepted accounting practice.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are reviewing the accounting system of a 25-employee landscaping company before its first external review engagement. The owner keeps the books in a small-business accounting package, the office manager both writes checks and reconciles the bank account, and revenue is recorded when cash comes in. Create the review checklist and a short findings memo: items covering revenue recognition, accrual versus cash basis, segregation of duties, bank reconciliations, fixed assets and depreciation, payroll, and system access; then summarize the top three issues with recommendations sized for a small company.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Relevant checks: Checklist covers recognition policies, reconciliations, cutoff, asset records, payroll and access controls with what evidence to inspect.
- Accounting judgment: Correctly identifies cash-basis revenue as an issue if accrual-basis statements are needed, and the duty overlap as a control weakness.
- Practical recommendations: Recommendations are realistic for a small business, such as owner review of reconciliations and bank statements, instead of hiring more staff.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Revenue: when work is completed versus when paid; unbilled and deferred amounts at year-end.
- Reconciliations: monthly, reviewed and signed by the owner; check the most recent three.
- Fixed assets: trucks and mowers on a schedule with depreciation method.
- Payroll: tax filings timely, seasonal worker classification.
- Top issues: cash-basis revenue, duty overlap, no access controls; compensating controls suggested.

### 52.0301-P6. `13-2041.00` Credit Analysts, O*NET task 1258 (customer call script and resolution note)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Talk with customers to settle complaints and confirm credit and financial transactions.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a small-business customer of a credit union calls angry that her line-of-credit application was declined and that a payment she made last month is shown as late on her statement. Write the call script you would follow as the credit analyst: how you verify her identity, how you investigate the late-payment record, what you can and cannot explain about the credit decision (including adverse action notice rules), how you de-escalate, and the written resolution note for the file.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Verification and investigation: Verifies identity before discussing details and traces the payment through records (posting date, method, cutoff time).
- Compliant explanation: Explains the decline using the actual reasons in the adverse action notice, without promising outcomes or sharing other customers' data.
- Resolution and documentation: De-escalates professionally, offers concrete next steps (correction if posting error, reapplication guidance) and documents the outcome.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Identity check using the institution's procedure.
- Look up the payment: if received before the due-date cutoff, correct the record and the credit-bureau report if it was reported.
- Decline reasons from the notice, such as debt-to-income or time in business; how to request the credit report used.
- Acknowledge frustration, summarize, agree on a follow-up date.
- File note: issue, findings, corrections, commitments, date and analyst name.
