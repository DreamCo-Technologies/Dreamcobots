## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading standards, material property tables, and technical drawings); Critical Thinking (checking analysis results against hand calculations and physical intuition); Speaking (presenting design reviews to faculty and sponsors).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Engineering and Technology (statics, dynamics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, and heat transfer); Mathematics (calculus through differential equations, linear algebra, and numerical methods); Design (engineering graphics and CAD, machine design, and a capstone design project).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Mechanical Engineers; Aerospace Engineers; Automotive Engineers: Mechanical (mechanics of materials, manufacturing processes, and machine-shop or makerspace work); Physics (calculus-based mechanics and electricity and magnetism with labs); English Language (engineering reports and technical memos). Keep building Active Listening (capturing customer requirements in capstone sponsor meetings); Mathematics (weekly problem sets in engineering analysis courses) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Mechanical Engineers; Aerospace Engineers; Automotive Engineers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Engineering and Technology, Mathematics.

### 14.1901-P1. `17-2011.00` Aerospace Engineers, O*NET task 1335 (test campaign coordination plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Lead and coordinate the engineers and technicians who design, build, modify or test aircraft and aerospace products.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are the junior engineer leading a five-person team (two design engineers, a composites technician, a test technician and an instrumentation intern) through a static load test of a new carbon-fiber wing spar for a small uncrewed aircraft. The test must show the spar carries 1.5 times the limit load without failure. Write the coordination plan for the three-week campaign: who does what, the sequence from specimen fabrication to test report, the safety controls for loading to failure, the instrumentation plan, and how you handle a schedule slip if the first specimen has a manufacturing flaw.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear roles and sequence: Each person's responsibilities and handoffs are explicit, and the sequence (fabricate, inspect, instrument, test, analyze, report) is logical.
- Technical soundness: Instrumentation (strain gauges, load cells, deflection) and load steps are appropriate for verifying the 1.5 factor, with predicted values to compare against.
- Safety and contingency: Includes test-area exclusion zones and stop criteria, and a realistic fallback for a flawed specimen.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Week 1: fabricate two specimens, ultrasonic or visual inspection, strain gauge placement plan.
- Week 2: test-rig checkout, calibration of load cell, dry run at low load, test-readiness review.
- Week 3: incremental loading to limit load, then to 1.5 times, holding and recording; optional test to failure.
- Safety: shields, exclusion zone, abort criteria on unexpected strain or cracking sounds.
- Contingency: second specimen built in parallel; report delays communicated with a revised date.

### 14.1901-P2. `17-2141.00` Mechanical Engineers, O*NET task 1411 (drawing interpretation review)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Read and make sense of blueprints, technical drawings, schematics and computer-generated reports.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Use a publicly available mechanical drawing of a machined bracket or shaft (or sketch an invented one with at least one hole pattern, a tolerance block, a surface-finish note and two geometric tolerances such as position and flatness). Write a review memo for the machine shop: explain in plain words what each critical dimension and geometric tolerance requires, identify the datum features and how the part would be fixtured for inspection, flag any ambiguity or missing information, and list questions you would ask the designer before release.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct reading: Geometric tolerances, datums, tolerance block and surface-finish notes are interpreted correctly.
- Manufacturing insight: Links requirements to how the part is made and inspected (fixturing on datums, measurement method).
- Ambiguity detection: Identifies real gaps or conflicts in the drawing and turns them into specific questions.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Datum A primary face, B and C as hole or edge features; part located on these for inspection.
- Position tolerance on the hole pattern relative to A, B, C explained as a tolerance zone; flatness on the mounting face.
- Default tolerances from the title block apply to unlabeled dimensions.
- Ambiguities such as missing material spec, unclear thread callout or conflicting dimensions.
- Questions for the designer and a recommendation to fix before release.

### 14.1901-P3. `17-2141.02` Automotive Engineers, O*NET task 16429 (root cause analysis report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Investigate why parts fail or vary and find the underlying cause.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: warranty data show a compact car's rear wheel bearings failing at about 30,000 km in one region, three times the normal rate, starting with vehicles built after a supplier change in March. Write a root-cause analysis using a structured method (such as 8D, fishbone and five whys): the problem statement, containment actions, the candidate causes across material, process, design and usage, the data and teardown tests you would request to confirm or rule out each, and how you will verify the permanent fix.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Structured method: Uses a recognized method with a clear problem statement (what, where, when, how many) and containment.
- Evidence-driven causes: Candidate causes are specific and each has a test or data check to confirm or eliminate it, rather than jumping to conclusions.
- Verified correction: The permanent fix is verified with tests or field data and the process is changed to prevent recurrence.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Problem statement with build dates, region, mileage and failure rate.
- Containment: inspect stock from the new supplier, quarantine suspect lots.
- Causes: seal design letting in road salt (region), heat treatment change at the supplier, press-fit tolerance at assembly.
- Checks: teardown of failed parts for corrosion versus fatigue, hardness tests, compare press-fit force logs.
- Verify fix with accelerated life testing and monitor warranty claims for six months.

### 14.1901-P4. `17-2011.00` Aerospace Engineers, O*NET task 1339 (analysis model and design check)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Build mathematical or computer models to create, assess or change a design so it meets the customer's engineering requirements.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A customer needs a quadcopter arm made of an aluminum tube that is 250 mm long, carries a 1.2 kg motor thrust at the tip, and must deflect less than 3 mm with a safety factor of 2 on yield. Build a simple analytical model (cantilever beam) to size the tube's outer diameter and wall thickness, show the equations and numbers, compare two candidate tube sizes, then explain how you would check the hand calculation with finite-element analysis and what extra effects (vibration, fatigue, joint stiffness) the simple model misses.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct model: Uses correct cantilever bending stress and deflection relations with consistent units and a stated material property.
- Design decision: Compares candidates against both requirements and chooses one with mass considered.
- Model limits: Explains how FEA or testing would validate the model and names the missing effects with their likely impact.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Load about 11.8 N; moment at root is load times 0.25 m.
- Stress from bending moment times outer radius divided by second moment of area; deflection from load times length cubed over 3 E I.
- Two tube sizes tabulated with stress, safety factor, deflection and mass.
- FEA with matching boundary conditions; mesh check; compare with hand result within a few percent.
- Missing effects: motor vibration and resonance, fatigue at the clamp, clamp compliance.

### 14.1901-P5. `17-2141.00` Mechanical Engineers, O*NET task 1412 (technical conversation script)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Work with engineers and other staff to put operating procedures in place, solve system malfunctions or give technical information.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a hospital's chilled-water system keeps tripping one of two chillers on low refrigerant pressure during hot afternoons, and the operating rooms are drifting above their temperature limit. You are the mechanical engineer meeting the facilities supervisor and the chiller service technician. Write a meeting script and follow-up note: what information you ask for (trend logs, alarm history, condenser water temperatures), likely causes you will discuss, the interim operating procedure to protect the operating rooms, and the agreed next steps.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Diagnostic questioning: Asks for specific data and observations that would distinguish likely causes rather than guessing.
- Technical reasoning: Discusses plausible causes (fouled condenser, low charge, failing expansion valve, cooling tower performance) correctly.
- Practical outcome: Defines a safe interim procedure for critical spaces and clear next steps with owners and dates.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Request: alarm times, suction and discharge pressures, condenser water supply temperature, recent service history.
- Likely causes include a refrigerant leak, a faulty expansion valve or poor heat rejection on hot days.
- Interim procedure: stage the healthy chiller first, pre-cool operating rooms in the morning, portable cooling backup.
- Next steps: leak test, condenser tube inspection, cooling tower check; owners and dates.
- Follow-up note circulated the same day with decisions recorded.

### 14.1901-P6. `17-2141.02` Automotive Engineers, O*NET task 16431 (specification and cost estimate)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Turn early vehicle design ideas into engineering requirements and estimates of what they will cost.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your team is considering replacing a stamped-steel battery-tray bracket in an electric vehicle with a die-cast aluminum version to save mass. Write a short engineering specification for the new bracket (loads, crash and vibration requirements, corrosion protection, mass target, interface dimensions) and a rough per-unit cost estimate for both designs at 100,000 vehicles per year, including material, processing, tooling amortization and joining. State your assumptions and recommend whether to proceed.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Testable specification: Requirements are quantified and verifiable (load values, test standards, mass limit, interface tolerances).
- Cost logic: The estimate separates material, process, tooling amortization and assembly with stated assumptions and correct arithmetic.
- Balanced recommendation: Weighs mass savings against cost, galvanic corrosion and supply risk and gives a clear decision.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Specification: static and crash loads, vibration profile, salt-spray requirement, mass under a target, bolt pattern tolerances.
- Steel: low material cost, cheap stamping, welding cost; aluminum: higher material cost, die-casting tooling amortized over volume.
- Assumptions for prices per kilogram, cycle times and tooling cost.
- Cost per kilogram of mass saved compared with the program's target.
- Recommendation with risks such as dissimilar-metal joints.
