## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (environmental reporting and technical writing), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading regulations, permits, and scientific literature); Speaking (presenting findings at public meetings); Active Listening (stakeholder meetings in community projects).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Law and Government (environmental law, policy, and permitting); Computers and Electronics (GIS, environmental data analysis, and modeling); Mathematics (calculus and environmental statistics, including spatial data analysis).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Environmental Scientists and Specialists, Including Health; Environmental Science and Protection Technicians, Including Health; Compliance Officers): Biology (ecology, conservation biology, and field sampling methods); Chemistry (general and environmental chemistry: water, soil, and air quality analysis). Keep building Writing (writing field reports and environmental assessments); Critical Thinking (evaluating environmental data quality and impacts); Active Learning (learning new field and GIS methods each term and applying them in projects) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Environmental Scientists and Specialists, Including Health; Environmental Science and Protection Technicians, Including Health; Compliance Officers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Law and Government, Computers and Electronics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Customer and Personal Service (3.21): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administration and Management (3.02): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).

### 03.0104-P1. `19-2041.00` Environmental Scientists and Specialists, Including Health, O*NET task 1514 (complaint investigation plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Audit or inspect sites for environmental performance and look into suspected breaches of the rules.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: residents report muddy water flowing from a housing construction site into a creek after storms. As the environmental specialist for the county, write the investigation plan: what you review before the visit (the site's stormwater permit and erosion control plan), what you inspect on site (silt fences, inlet protection, stabilized entrances, sediment basins), samples or photos you collect and how, interviews, how you determine whether a violation occurred, and the documentation for possible enforcement.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Preparation: Reviews the permit, plan and past inspection history before visiting.
- Evidence collection: Collects defensible evidence (dated photos, sampling with chain of custody, field notes) tied to permit requirements.
- Sound determination: Links observations to specific permit conditions and documents findings for enforcement.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Review permit coverage, erosion and sediment control plan, inspection logs.
- Inspect controls and discharge points; photograph with GPS and time.
- Turbidity samples upstream and downstream with chain of custody.
- Interview site superintendent; request maintenance records.
- Findings report referencing permit conditions and required corrective actions.

### 03.0104-P2. `19-4042.00` Environmental Science and Protection Technicians, Including Health, O*NET task 1549 (water quality data report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Log measurement data and produce reports, summaries and graphs explaining what the results mean.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented data: you sampled dissolved oxygen, temperature, pH, conductivity and E. coli at four creek sites monthly for a year. Write the data report for the watershed council: how data were recorded and quality-checked (calibration logs, duplicates, holding times), a summary table, two charts you would include (with types and axes), comparison with the state water quality criteria (cite the source type), and plain-language findings with limitations.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Data quality: Describes recording, calibration and QC checks and how flagged data were handled.
- Clear presentation: Tables and charts are appropriate, labeled and compare sites and seasons.
- Interpretation: Compares with applicable criteria correctly and states limitations and next steps.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Field sheets, meter calibration records, field duplicates, E. coli holding time compliance.
- Table of seasonal medians by site.
- Charts: time series of dissolved oxygen by site; bar chart of E. coli geometric mean.
- Criteria from the state water quality standards; exceedances noted.
- Findings: downstream site lower oxygen in summer; recommend source investigation.

### 03.0104-P3. `13-1041.00` Compliance Officers, O*NET task 21447 (permit application compliance review)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Review submitted applications and supporting records to decide whether an applicant qualifies or is liable.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a small furniture-finishing shop applies for a minor-source air permit, reporting that it uses 3,000 liters of solvent-based coatings a year. Write the review: the information you check for completeness, how you estimate emissions from coating usage and solvent content (show the calculation with labeled assumptions), whether the shop's estimate qualifies it for the requested permit type, missing records you would request, and the decision memo.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Completeness review: Checks required application elements and supporting records (safety data sheets, purchase records).
- Correct estimate: Emission estimate uses volume, density and VOC content correctly with stated assumptions.
- Defensible decision: Decision memo ties conclusions to rules and identifies deficiencies or conditions.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Checklist: facility information, process description, materials, controls, signatures.
- Emission estimate: liters times density times VOC fraction gives kilograms per year.
- Compare with the permit threshold in the applicable rules.
- Request safety data sheets and 12 months of purchase records.
- Memo: approve with recordkeeping conditions or request more information.

### 03.0104-P4. `19-2041.00` Environmental Scientists and Specialists, Including Health, O*NET task 1526 (impact monitoring plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Monitor how development projects affect the environment.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A state highway widening will fill 0.8 hectares of wetland and cross a trout stream; the permit requires monitoring during construction and for five years afterward. Write the monitoring plan: indicators (turbidity, temperature, macroinvertebrates, wetland vegetation, hydrology at the mitigation site), locations and frequency, methods and quality control, thresholds that trigger corrective action, and annual reporting.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Relevant indicators: Indicators match the impacts and permit conditions.
- Design: Uses control and impact sites, before and after data, and appropriate frequency.
- Adaptive management: Defines thresholds and corrective actions and a reporting schedule.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Before-after-control-impact design with upstream reference site.
- Continuous turbidity and temperature during construction; macroinvertebrates spring and fall.
- Mitigation wetland: vegetation cover, invasive species, groundwater levels.
- Thresholds: turbidity over background triggers inspection and stopping work; low native cover triggers replanting.
- Annual reports to the permitting agency.

### 03.0104-P5. `19-4042.00` Environmental Science and Protection Technicians, Including Health, O*NET task 1560 (results conversation script)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Talk customers through test results and what the analysis means.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a family's private well test shows nitrate at a level above the federal drinking water standard for public systems and detects total coliform bacteria. The parents have a 3-month-old baby. Write the phone conversation script and follow-up letter: how you explain the results clearly without alarming or minimizing, immediate precautions (especially for infant formula), what the coliform result may mean, retesting and treatment options, and where they can get further help.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear, accurate explanation: Explains results and standards correctly in plain language.
- Protective advice: Prioritizes the infant's safety with correct precautions (do not boil water for nitrate; use an alternative source for formula).
- Next steps and resources: Gives retesting, treatment options and referrals to health department or certified contractors.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Result explanation with units and the standard as a comparison.
- Nitrate: boiling concentrates it; use bottled water for formula and drinking now.
- Coliform: indicator of possible contamination; shock chlorination and retest.
- Treatment options for nitrate such as reverse osmosis or ion exchange; well inspection.
- Letter with results, advice, contacts and retest date.

### 03.0104-P6. `13-1041.00` Compliance Officers, O*NET task 21446 (notice of violation and conversation plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Tell violators about their infractions and the penalties they face.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Continuing the construction runoff case, the inspection confirmed failed silt fences and sediment reaching the creek. Write the notice of violation letter and a plan for the conversation with the site manager: the facts and the permit conditions violated, required corrective actions with deadlines, potential penalties under the applicable rules (refer to the rule type without inventing amounts), the right to respond, and how you keep the tone firm but professional.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Factual and specific: Notice cites dates, observations, evidence and exact permit conditions.
- Clear requirements: Corrective actions and deadlines are specific and verifiable.
- Professional enforcement: Explains penalties and rights accurately, keeps a professional tone and documents the conversation.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Heading, permit number, inspection date and observations with photo references.
- Conditions violated, quoted by number from the permit.
- Corrective actions: repair silt fence within 48 hours, stabilize slopes, clean creek sediment under guidance.
- Penalty language referencing the enforcement rules; right to respond within a set period.
- Conversation: firm facts, listen, agree on reinspection date, written follow-up.
