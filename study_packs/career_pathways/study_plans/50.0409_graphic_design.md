## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (writing design rationales and working with copy), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Active Listening (capturing client briefs and critique feedback); Reading Comprehension (reading creative briefs and brand guidelines); Critical Thinking (evaluating design options against goals in critiques).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Computers and Electronics (design software for layout, vector, raster, motion, and prototyping); Design (typography, layout and grid systems, color theory, and visual identity); Communications and Media (visual communication, advertising, and digital media).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Graphic Designers; Web and Digital Interface Designers; Special Effects Artists and Animators): Sales and Marketing (branding and marketing fundamentals); Fine Arts (drawing, color theory, and art history as a visual vocabulary for design). Keep building Active Learning (learning a new tool or technique each term); Speaking (presenting design concepts to clients); Writing (writing design rationales and client briefs) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Graphic Designers; Web and Digital Interface Designers; Special Effects Artists and Animators; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Computers and Electronics, Design.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Customer and Personal Service (3.43): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administration and Management (2.95): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).

### 50.0409-P1. `27-1014.00` Special Effects Artists and Animators, O*NET task 6882 (animated explainer production plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Produce sophisticated motion graphics on the computer, relying on one's own creative judgment.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A city transit agency wants a 20-second silent animated explainer, for station screens, showing how to tap a contactless card or phone to pay. Write the production plan and creative rationale: a short storyboard (six to eight frames with timing), the visual style and color palette tied to the agency's brand and accessibility, how motion guides attention without text, the software and file formats you will use, and how you will test that riders understand it without sound.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear visual storytelling: Storyboard communicates the steps in sequence with timing that fits 20 seconds.
- Craft and accessibility: Style, contrast and motion choices support comprehension, including for viewers with low vision or limited English.
- Production and testing: Names tools and output specs and includes a comprehension test with riders.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Frames: approach gate, hold card near reader, light or check mark feedback, gate opens, phone variant.
- Brand palette with high contrast; large icons; no essential text.
- Motion: highlight reader with gentle pulse; ease-in timing; loop cleanly.
- Tools: vector design and motion graphics software; export at screen resolution and frame rate.
- Test with ten riders: can they describe the steps after one viewing?

### 50.0409-P2. `27-1024.00` Graphic Designers, O*NET task 300 (layout and typography spec)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Decide how big images and text should be, how they are arranged on the page, and which typefaces and sizes to use.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Design a poster (24 by 36 inches) for a free community jazz festival with a headline, date and location, five performer names, a sponsor logo strip and a QR code. Write the layout and typography specification: grid and margins, the visual hierarchy (what is read first, second, third), typeface choices and sizes in points with reasons, image placement and scale, color and contrast, and how the design adapts to a 1080 by 1920 pixel social media version.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Strong hierarchy: Arrangement makes the most important information readable first from a distance.
- Justified type choices: Typefaces, sizes and spacing are specified and justified for legibility and mood.
- Adaptability: Explains how the layout adapts to a vertical digital format without losing hierarchy.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Grid: 12 columns, margins about 1 inch, generous whitespace.
- Hierarchy: festival name, then date and place, then performers, then sponsors and QR code.
- Type: display face for the headline at large size; clean sans serif for details; at most two families.
- Image: full-bleed illustration with text on a calm area; contrast checked.
- Social version: stacked layout, larger date, QR code replaced by link.

### 50.0409-P3. `15-1255.00` Web and Digital Interface Designers, O*NET task 21686 (design-development collaboration plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Work with front-end and back-end developers so a website project is delivered completely.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You designed an online exhibit for a local history museum with scroll-triggered animations, an interactive timeline and an image zoom viewer, to be built by one front-end and one back-end developer. Write the collaboration plan: how you specify animations (timing, easing, reduced-motion alternatives), the content model the back end must support (timeline entries, image sets, captions, credits), performance budgets for images and scripts, how you pair with developers during build, and acceptance testing.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Precise specifications: Animation and interaction specs are concrete enough to build and include accessibility alternatives.
- Content and performance: Defines a content model and performance budgets that developers can implement.
- Working process: Describes pairing, feedback loops and acceptance tests.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Motion specs with durations and easing; prefers-reduced-motion fallback.
- Content model fields: date, title, text, images, alt text, credits.
- Budgets: page weight limit, responsive images, lazy loading.
- Weekly pairing sessions and a shared issues board.
- Acceptance: device matrix, keyboard navigation, screen reader check, performance score.

### 50.0409-P4. `27-1014.00` Special Effects Artists and Animators, O*NET task 6884 (lighting and texturing plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Make digital objects and characters look believable through careful handling of lighting, surface color and texture, shadows and see-through materials, or by bringing still images to life.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A kitchenware company needs a photorealistic 3D render of a glass-and-steel kettle for its website, matching studio product photography. Write the lighting, materials and rendering plan: the light setup (key, fill, rim, environment map), physically based material settings for brushed steel, clear glass with water inside and a rubber handle, how you handle reflections, caustics and shadows, the render settings, and how you compare the result with reference photos.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Lighting design: Light setup is purposeful and mimics studio product photography.
- Material realism: Material parameters (roughness, index of refraction, anisotropy) are appropriate for each material.
- Reference comparison: Describes how renders are evaluated against real photos and adjusted.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Large soft key, fill card, rim light for edges, neutral HDRI for reflections.
- Steel: metallic with anisotropic roughness; glass: refractive index around 1.5; water slightly higher.
- Shadows: soft contact shadow; caustics enabled or faked for speed.
- Render samples and denoising settings; output color space.
- Side-by-side with reference shots; adjust highlights and reflections.

### 50.0409-P5. `27-1024.00` Graphic Designers, O*NET task 308 (concept sketches and client review)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Draw early sketches or illustrations, talk them through with the client or art lead, and rework them.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A neighborhood bakery wants a new logo and storefront sign. Describe your concept phase: how you gather the brief (values, audience, competitors), six thumbnail directions in words, how you narrow to three rough sketches, how you present them to the owners and capture feedback, and how you revise without losing the concept. Include the questions you ask when feedback is vague (such as 'make it pop').

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Brief-driven exploration: Concepts clearly connect to the brief and are distinct.
- Effective review: Presentation explains rationale and gathers specific, usable feedback.
- Disciplined revision: Revisions respond to feedback while protecting legibility and concept, with documented approvals.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Brief questionnaire and a mood board.
- Thumbnails: hand-lettered script, wheat icon, vintage badge, minimalist wordmark, illustrative storefront, geometric bread shape.
- Three roughs shown on a mockup sign at real scale.
- Clarifying questions: which part should stand out, what feeling is missing, show an example you like.
- Revision round with change log and owner sign-off.

### 50.0409-P6. `15-1255.00` Web and Digital Interface Designers, O*NET task 21699 (pre-production plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Lead pre-production work such as mood boards, storyboards and the project timeline.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A food-rescue nonprofit hires you to redesign its website to recruit volunteers and donors. Write the pre-production plan: discovery activities, two mood boards described in words with the reasoning behind each, a storyboard of the volunteer sign-up journey from home page to confirmation, content and asset inventory, roles and approvals, and a six-week timeline with milestones and review points.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Purposeful discovery: Discovery identifies users, goals and constraints before visual work.
- Clear visual and journey direction: Mood boards and storyboard are specific and tied to goals.
- Realistic timeline: Timeline has milestones, review points and dependencies on client content.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Discovery: stakeholder interview, analytics review, three volunteer interviews.
- Mood board A: warm, community photography; B: bold, data-driven impact graphics.
- Storyboard: home, volunteer page, choose shift, form, confirmation email.
- Inventory: photos, impact numbers, copy, logos.
- Weeks 1 to 6 with approvals after mood boards and wireframes.
