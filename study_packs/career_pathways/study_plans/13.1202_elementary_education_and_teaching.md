## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Speaking (delivering mini-lessons in field placements); Learning Strategies (designing lessons with scaffolding and retrieval practice); Reading Comprehension (reading curriculum standards and education research).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: English Language (children's literature and literacy instruction methods); Education and Training (teaching methods, curriculum design, and classroom field experience); Psychology (child development and educational psychology).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education: Mathematics (mathematics for elementary teachers and math methods); Customer and Personal Service (family engagement and communication practice); Therapy and Counseling (social-emotional learning and supporting students with special needs). Keep building Active Listening (listening to children's explanations during formative assessment); Monitoring (tracking student progress with running records and checklists) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows English Language, Education and Training.

### 13.1202-P1. `25-2021.00` Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education, O*NET task 6535 (classroom management plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep the classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are starting the year with 24 second graders. Write your classroom management plan for the first two weeks: four or five positively stated expectations, the routines you will teach (arrival, transitions, bathroom, getting help, dismissal) and how each is modeled and practiced, how you recognize positive behavior, a calm and consistent response ladder for misbehavior, and how you partner with families. Note how you adjust for a student with ADHD and a student new to English.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Age-appropriate expectations: Expectations are few, concrete, positively worded and suitable for seven-year-olds.
- Taught routines: Each routine is explicitly modeled, practiced and reinforced during the first weeks.
- Consistent, supportive responses: Responses to misbehavior are calm, predictable and respectful, with individual supports for the two students.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Expectations: safe bodies, kind words, listening ears, try your best, take care of our things.
- Routines taught with model, practice, feedback; visual schedule.
- Recognition: specific praise, class goal chart.
- Response ladder: nonverbal cue, reminder, reset spot, conference, family contact.
- Supports: movement breaks and checklists for the student with ADHD; picture cues and a buddy for the English learner.

### 13.1202-P2. `25-2021.00` Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education, O*NET task 6536 (observation and record plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Watch and assess students' learning, behavior, social growth and physical well-being.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A third grader's reading progress has stalled, she seems tired in the mornings, and she has recently been alone at recess. Write an observation plan for the next three weeks: what you will observe and when (academic, behavioral, social, physical signs), the recording tools (running records, anecdotal notes, a simple frequency chart), how you keep notes objective, who you would consult (reading specialist, nurse, counselor, family), and when concerns would require a referral or a mandated report.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Whole-child observation: Covers academic, social, behavioral and health indicators at different times of day.
- Objective records: Tools and examples show objective, dated notes rather than judgments.
- Appropriate collaboration: Involves the right specialists and family, and correctly states that suspected abuse or neglect must be reported under mandated-reporter rules.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Running records twice a week; note fluency and errors.
- Anecdotal notes at arrival, recess and after lunch; frequency of yawning or head down.
- Objective phrasing: what was seen and heard, not interpretations.
- Consult reading specialist and nurse; family conversation about sleep and well-being.
- Referral to student support team if no progress; immediate report if signs of abuse or neglect.

### 13.1202-P3. `25-2021.00` Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education, O*NET task 6537 (station setup plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Get materials and the classroom ready for learning activities.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan the materials and room setup for a third-grade science morning with four rotating stations on how plants grow (seed dissection, measuring seedlings, light versus dark comparison, recording observations in journals). List every material per station with quantities for 24 students, the room layout, safety and cleanup considerations, labeled instructions students can follow without you, setup and reset timing, and a backup plan if seedlings did not sprout.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete materials: Materials and quantities are complete and fit group sizes.
- Independent stations: Layout, instruction cards and timing let students rotate and work independently.
- Safety and contingency: Includes safety (seed allergies, tools), cleanup and a realistic backup plan.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Station lists: soaked bean seeds, hand lenses, rulers, plants in light and dark, journals.
- Layout with traffic flow and a teacher check-in station.
- Picture-based instruction cards; rotation timer every 12 minutes.
- Safety: check allergies, no tasting, safe tools; cleanup kits.
- Backup: photos of growth stages or fast-sprouting seeds started earlier.

### 13.1202-P4. `25-2021.00` Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education, O*NET task 6538 (differentiated math lesson)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Adjust teaching approaches and materials to suit students' different needs and interests.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your fourth-grade class is learning to compare fractions with unlike denominators. Assessment shows three groups: students who still struggle with what a fraction represents, students ready for the grade-level goal, and students who already compare fractions easily. Plan one 60-minute lesson with differentiation: the shared launch, three tiered tasks using manipulatives, number lines or real-world problems, how you move between groups, supports for a student with dyscalculia, and a common exit task.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Mathematically sound: Uses accurate representations (fraction strips, number lines, benchmark fractions, common denominators).
- Meaningful tiers: Tasks differ in support and complexity while all target the same concept.
- Responsive teaching: Explains how groups are monitored and adjusted, with specific accommodations and a common exit task.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Launch: which is more pizza, two-thirds or three-fifths?
- Tier 1: fraction strips to build and compare; Tier 2: benchmarks and number lines; Tier 3: create and justify comparison problems.
- Teacher rotates with guiding questions; flexible regrouping mid-lesson.
- Dyscalculia supports: manipulatives, visual models, fewer problems, extra time.
- Exit task: compare two fractions and explain using a model.

### 13.1202-P5. `25-2021.00` Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education, O*NET task 6539 (inquiry unit plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Plan and run a balanced mix of teaching, demonstrations and hands-on time so students can observe, ask questions and investigate.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan a one-week first-grade unit on weather. Each day should balance short direct teaching, a demonstration, and work time in which children observe, ask questions and investigate (for example, a class weather chart, a cloud-in-a-jar demonstration, measuring rainfall). Write the daily plan with times, the guiding question for the week, how children record observations at their level, how you capture and use their questions, and how you assess learning once the week is over.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Balanced design: Each day includes brief instruction, a demonstration and substantial student investigation.
- Inquiry focus: Children's questions are collected and used, and observations are recorded in age-appropriate ways (drawings, symbols, tallies).
- Assessment: End-of-week assessment checks understanding through talk, drawings or a simple performance task.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Guiding question: how does the weather change, and how can we tell?
- Daily routine: weather chart with symbols, 10-minute mini-lesson, 15-minute investigation.
- Demonstrations: cloud in a jar, wind direction with a streamer, thermometer reading.
- Question board that the class returns to each day.
- Assessment: draw and tell about today's weather; class graph of the week.

### 13.1202-P6. `25-2021.00` Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education, O*NET task 6540 (small-group and individual instruction plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach students both one-on-one and in groups using methods such as explanation, discussion and demonstration.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Design a week of guided reading for a second-grade class with five reading groups and two students needing one-on-one support. Write the plan: how groups are formed from assessment data, the focus skill and text level for two of the groups, the structure of a 20-minute guided reading session, what the rest of the class does independently, how you deliver the one-on-one sessions, and how you track progress and regroup.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Data-informed grouping: Groups are formed from specific assessment data and are flexible.
- Effective sessions: Session structure includes before, during and after reading with explicit teaching of a skill.
- Management and tracking: Independent work is meaningful and manageable, and progress tracking leads to regrouping.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Groups from running records and phonics screening; regroup every few weeks.
- Example: group A decoding long vowels at an early level; group C fluency and retell at grade level.
- Session: book introduction, word work, supported reading, discussion, quick check.
- Independent: literacy stations with clear routines and accountability.
- One-on-one: daily 10-minute sessions with targeted phonics; progress chart.
