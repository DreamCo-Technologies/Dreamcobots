## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (argumentative and policy writing), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading legislation, court opinions, and political science research); Active Listening (deliberative discussions on contested issues); Critical Thinking (evaluating arguments and evidence in policy debates).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Computers and Electronics (data analysis for political research); Mathematics (statistics and research methods for political science: survey analysis and regression); Law and Government (American government, comparative politics, constitutional law, and public policy).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Social Science Research Assistants): Psychology (political psychology: public opinion, voting behavior, and persuasion); Sociology and Anthropology (political sociology: social movements, identity, and civil society). Keep building Speaking (debate, mock trial, or model United Nations); Writing (writing policy memos and research papers); Active Learning (applying new research and current events to policy memos) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Social Science Research Assistants; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Computers and Electronics, Mathematics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Education and Training (3.9): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.
- Customer and Personal Service (3.18): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administrative (2.93): office procedures and records handling; usually learned on the job, so it gets no course slot.

### 45.1001-P1. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (classroom norms plan (civics discussion))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You teach 11th-grade government and will hold weekly structured discussions on contested policy questions during an election year. Write the classroom norms and procedures plan: how students help set discussion norms, the routines for structured formats (such as structured academic controversy or Socratic seminar), how you keep discussion civil and evidence-based, your own stance on sharing personal political views, what you do when a comment targets a classmate's identity, and how you communicate the approach to families.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Co-created, specific norms: Norms are specific behaviors, co-created with students, and tied to evidence-based argument.
- Structured routines: Uses structured discussion formats with clear roles, timing and preparation.
- Fair responses and transparency: Handles harmful comments promptly and fairly, keeps teacher neutrality appropriate to school policy, and informs families.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Norm-setting lesson; norms posted and revisited.
- Formats: structured academic controversy pairs, Socratic seminar inner and outer circles.
- Evidence rule: claims backed by sources from a vetted reading set.
- Response to identity-targeting comments: stop, restate norm, private follow-up, restorative conversation, school policy if repeated.
- Family letter explaining purpose, materials and neutrality policy.

### 45.1001-P2. `19-4061.00` Social Science Research Assistants, O*NET task 12952 (roll-call vote dataset build)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Build, clean, transform and look after large research datasets.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A political science faculty member is studying party unity in state legislatures and needs a dataset of all recorded floor votes in three state houses over two sessions. Write the data plan: where the vote records come from (state legislature websites or open legislative data projects), the data structure (bills, roll calls, legislators, individual votes), unique identifiers for legislators who change districts or names, how you code abstentions and absences, how you validate the data against official journals, and how you document it.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Appropriate structure: Uses linked tables or long format with unique IDs for legislators, bills, roll calls and votes.
- Coding decisions: Defines consistent coding for yes, no, abstain, absent and paired votes and documents edge cases.
- Validation: Checks a random sample against official journals and reconciles vote totals.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Sources: state legislature journals and an open legislative data service, with retrieval dates.
- Tables: legislators (stable ID, party, district by session), roll calls (date, bill, motion), votes (legislator, roll call, vote code).
- Name matching across sessions with a manual review list.
- Validation: recompute totals per roll call and compare with official totals; sample 5 percent against journals.
- Codebook and change log.

### 45.1001-P3. `19-3094.00` Political Scientists, O*NET task 5515 (seminar session design (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Give instruction in the political science discipline.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice: imagine you are a teaching assistant invited to lead one 75-minute session of an introductory American politics course on 'Why turnout differs across groups and elections'. Design the session: learning goals, two short readings you would assign (real, cited), a mini-lecture outline, an activity using public turnout data, discussion questions that require evidence, and a short assessment. Explain how you would keep the session nonpartisan.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Substantive content: Covers recognized explanations of turnout (costs of voting, mobilization, resources, election type, institutional rules) accurately.
- Active learning: Includes a data activity and evidence-based discussion questions.
- Assessment and neutrality: Has an assessment aligned to the goals and a clear approach to nonpartisan teaching.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Goals: explain three drivers of turnout and interpret turnout data.
- Readings: a classic article on the calculus of voting and a recent study on registration rules, cited.
- Activity: compare midterm and presidential turnout by age using census voting supplement tables.
- Discussion: which reforms would most change the gaps, and what evidence supports that?
- Assessment: one-paragraph data interpretation; neutrality by focusing on evidence and multiple perspectives.

### 45.1001-P4. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6644 (differentiated lesson plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Adjust teaching approaches and materials to suit students' different needs and interests.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your 9th-grade civics class of 30 includes four English learners at intermediate proficiency, two students with reading-related learning disabilities, and several students who already follow politics closely. Take a lesson on the First Amendment's freedoms and adapt it: the common learning goal, three versions of the main reading or source set, a choice-based task with different entry points, supports and extensions, grouping strategies, and how you assess everyone against the same goal.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Common goal, varied paths: All students work toward the same core goal with differentiated materials and processes.
- Specific supports: Supports for English learners and students with disabilities are concrete (glossaries, audio, sentence frames, chunked texts).
- Fair assessment: Assessment measures the same goal for all students, with accommodations that do not lower expectations.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Goal: explain how a First Amendment freedom applies in a realistic scenario.
- Materials: adapted text, original text with annotations, audio version; images and case summaries.
- Task choice: case brief, short podcast script, or annotated poster on a student-speech case.
- Groups mixed by skill with roles; extension using an actual court opinion excerpt.
- Rubric shared by all products, focused on applying the freedom correctly.

### 45.1001-P5. `19-4061.00` Social Science Research Assistants, O*NET task 12956 (coding verification and reliability check)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Check that data entered into research databases are accurate and valid, and fix the errors found.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A project codes local news articles into a protest-event dataset (date, location, issue, size estimate, police presence). Four coders have entered 1,200 events. Design the verification procedure: a double-coding sample, which variables you compute intercoder reliability for and with which statistic, logic and range checks (dates outside the study period, size estimates as text), how disagreements are resolved and recorded, and the reliability threshold below which a variable's coding rules must be revised.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Reliability method: Uses an appropriate statistic (such as Krippendorff's alpha or Cohen's kappa) on a sufficient random sample, per variable.
- Error checks: Specifies automated validity checks and manual review of flagged records.
- Documented resolution: Disagreements are resolved by rule with an audit trail, and low-reliability variables trigger codebook revision and recoding.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Random 15 percent of articles double-coded blind.
- Alpha per variable; categorical issue codes and size bins.
- Checks: dates in range, valid place names geocoded, numeric size fields.
- Adjudication by a lead coder; log of original codes and final decisions.
- Threshold around 0.8 for key variables, 0.67 minimum for tentative use; revise rules and retrain otherwise.

### 45.1001-P6. `19-3094.00` Political Scientists, O*NET task 5517 (research agenda memo (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Find the questions that are worth researching and analyzing.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice: a county elections office asks a local university for help understanding why turnout in municipal elections is below 20 percent. Write a research agenda memo: three candidate research questions ranked by importance and feasibility, what is already known (cite at least two real studies), the data that exist or could be collected, which question you would pursue first and why, and ethical or political sensitivities in working with an elections office.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Well-formed questions: Questions are specific, answerable and policy-relevant (e.g. effect of election timing or mail ballots).
- Grounded in literature and data: Cites real prior work and identifies feasible data sources.
- Judgment and ethics: Justifies the choice with explicit criteria and addresses neutrality, privacy of voter files and independence.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Questions: does moving local elections to coincide with state elections raise turnout; do mailed reminders work; who are habitual non-voters?
- Literature on election timing and turnout, and on low-cost mobilization field experiments.
- Data: voter files with turnout history, election calendars, mail ballot records.
- Pursue first: a feasible randomized reminder study with the office.
- Ethics: protect voter data, nonpartisan messages, pre-registration of the study.
