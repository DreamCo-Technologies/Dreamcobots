## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading economics journal articles and government data releases); Critical Thinking (evaluating causal claims in empirical studies); Active Listening (seminar discussions and research team meetings).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: English Language (economic writing: policy briefs and research papers); Mathematics (calculus, statistics, and econometrics); Computers and Electronics (data analysis in R, Stata, or Python).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Social Science Research Assistants; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education: Education and Training (teaching assistant or economics tutoring experience); Customer and Personal Service (community-facing work such as volunteer tax assistance or financial literacy programs); Administrative (research data management and documentation). Keep building Writing (writing an empirical research paper with revisions); Speaking (presenting findings in seminars) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Social Science Research Assistants; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows English Language, Mathematics.

### 45.0601-P1. `19-4061.00` Social Science Research Assistants, O*NET task 12952 (panel dataset build)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Build, clean, transform and look after large research datasets.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. An economics professor needs a county-by-month panel for 2015 to 2024 combining unemployment rates from the Bureau of Labor Statistics local area statistics, county population from the Census Bureau, and a list of counties that raised local minimum wages (with effective dates). Write the data-build plan: where each file comes from, the merge keys (county FIPS codes and dates) and pitfalls such as changed county definitions, how you handle missing and revised values, the structure of the final panel, and how the build is scripted and documented for replication.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct sources and keys: Names real public sources and merges on FIPS codes and time correctly, noting county boundary changes and annual versus monthly frequency.
- Data handling: Handles revisions, missing values and the event-date variable sensibly and transparently.
- Reproducibility: Build is fully scripted with a codebook and version notes so others can rebuild it.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Download files with retrieval dates; store raw copies unchanged.
- Standardize FIPS as five-digit strings; crosswalk changed counties such as recent Connecticut changes.
- Interpolate annual population to months or keep annual and merge by year, documented.
- Minimum-wage treatment indicator switches on at the effective month.
- Script in R or Python, codebook, README and checksums.

### 45.0601-P2. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (classroom routines plan (economics simulation))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You teach high school economics and plan a lively in-class market simulation where students act as buyers and sellers trading cards for play money. Write the routines and rules that keep it productive: how roles and materials are assigned, signals to start and stop trading, how trades are recorded, rules about respectful bargaining and no side deals, what happens if a student disrupts or cheats, and how you debrief so the activity stays focused on learning.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear structure: Roles, materials, signals and recording procedures are specific enough for 30 students to follow.
- Behavior expectations: Rules are stated positively, fit a competitive activity, and consequences are proportionate and consistent.
- Learning focus: Routines protect time for data collection and debrief that connects to economic concepts.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Assign buyer and seller cards with private values and costs; trade-recording slips.
- Start and stop signal, timed rounds, trades reported to a recorder.
- Rules: honest reporting, polite bargaining, stay in the trading area.
- Consequences: warning, become a recorder for a round, follow-up conversation.
- Debrief: plot recorded prices to see convergence toward equilibrium.

### 45.0601-P3. `19-3011.00` Economists, O*NET task 7536 (applied data analysis)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Dig into economic and statistical evidence within a specialty such as labor, finance or agriculture.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Using public data (for example, state minimum wages and teen employment from government statistics), write a short applied analysis on whether recent minimum-wage increases in a few states coincided with changes in employment of 16 to 19 year olds. State the question, the data and time period, a simple comparison method (difference-in-differences with comparison states, or an event study), the results you found or would expect to check, threats to causal interpretation, and what the analysis cannot conclude. Cite all data sources.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear question and data: Question is specific and the data sources, variables and period are clearly identified.
- Appropriate method: Uses a comparison method suited to policy evaluation and explains its key assumption (parallel trends).
- Careful interpretation: Discusses confounders, small samples and the difference between correlation and causation, and ties conclusions to the evidence.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Question: did teen employment fall relative to comparison states after increases?
- Data: state minimum wage histories, employment survey estimates for teens, with sources.
- Method: difference-in-differences with pre-trend check.
- Threats: regional shocks, sampling error in small groups, policy anticipation.
- Conclusion stated with uncertainty and links to the published literature.

### 45.0601-P4. `19-4061.00` Social Science Research Assistants, O*NET task 12953 (tables, figures and replication package)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Help prepare reports, manuscripts and presentations for research projects.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. The professor's working paper on minimum wages is due to a conference in ten days and asks you to produce the descriptive-statistics table, two figures (an event-study plot and a map), and the slides' data appendix. Write your production plan: the specifications for each table and figure (variables, labels, notes, units), the style conventions you will follow, how every number is generated by code from the final dataset, how you check the figures against the regression output, and what goes in the replication package.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Publication-ready specs: Tables and figures have clear titles, labeled axes and units, source notes and consistent formatting.
- Accuracy controls: Every number comes from code, and there is a cross-check between tables, figures and text.
- Replication: Package includes data or access instructions, code, a README and versioned outputs.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Table 1: means and standard deviations by treated and comparison counties, before and after.
- Figure 1: event-study coefficients with 95 percent confidence intervals; Figure 2: map of treatment timing.
- Export tables and figures directly from scripts; no hand-typed numbers.
- Cross-check sample sizes and coefficient values in text against output logs.
- Replication folder with raw data links, cleaning and analysis scripts, README and software versions.

### 45.0601-P5. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6640 (lesson plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach a subject through explanation, discussion and demonstration.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan a 50-minute high school lesson on how supply and demand set prices. Include a short explanation with a hand-drawn graph, a classroom demonstration (for example, an auction of a limited item or the trading simulation), a discussion that applies the model to a current real-world price change students care about (such as concert tickets or gasoline), and an exit question. Show how you check understanding during the lesson and how you support students who find graphs difficult.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Economic accuracy: Correctly distinguishes changes in quantity demanded from shifts in demand and explains equilibrium.
- Engaging demonstration: The demonstration generates evidence students use in the discussion and fits the time.
- Checks and supports: Includes checks for understanding during the lesson and specific scaffolds for graph reading.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Hook: why do concert ticket prices jump when a tour is announced?
- Explain curves, equilibrium, and shifts with one example drawn step by step.
- Demonstration: auction with limited supply; record bids to build a demand schedule.
- Discussion: apply to a current price change; identify which curve shifted.
- Exit question and scaffolds such as a labeled graph template and think-pair-share.

### 45.0601-P6. `19-3011.00` Economists, O*NET task 7542 (student research team supervision plan (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Oversee research projects, including projects carried out by students.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice: imagine you are a senior research assistant asked to run a semester-long project with three undergraduate economics students who will code local housing-permit data for a faculty study. Write the supervision plan: the project's goals and milestones, how you train students and check their work quality, weekly meeting structure, how tasks are divided fairly, how you give feedback, and how students get credit and learning from the experience.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear milestones: Goals and milestones are concrete with deliverables and dates.
- Quality control: Includes training, a coding manual, double-coding samples and agreement checks.
- Supportive supervision: Describes fair workload, regular feedback, mentoring and appropriate credit.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Milestones: training in week 2, pilot coding by week 4, full coding by week 10, write-up by week 14.
- Coding manual with examples; 10 percent double-coded; resolve disagreements weekly.
- Weekly 45-minute meeting: progress, problems, one skill session.
- Feedback: written notes on coding accuracy and a mid-semester check-in.
- Credit: acknowledgment in the paper, a short research presentation, recommendation letters.
