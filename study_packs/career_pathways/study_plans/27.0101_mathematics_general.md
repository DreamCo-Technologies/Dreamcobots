## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading proofs and mathematical texts carefully); Critical Thinking (constructing and checking proofs and counterexamples); Active Listening (collaborative problem-solving sessions).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Mathematics (calculus, linear algebra, proofs, real analysis, abstract algebra, and probability); Computers and Electronics (programming and numerical or statistical computing); English Language (mathematical writing and proof presentation).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Data Scientists; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Bioinformatics Technicians: Education and Training (tutoring or teaching assistant experience in mathematics); Customer and Personal Service (explaining analyses to non-specialists in consulting or tutoring); Engineering and Technology (applied mathematics electives such as modeling or operations research). Keep building Speaking (presenting proofs at the board); Mathematics (weekly problem sets with written solutions) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Data Scientists; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Bioinformatics Technicians; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Mathematics, Computers and Electronics.

### 27.0101-P1. `15-2051.00` Data Scientists, O*NET task 21823 (exploratory data analysis)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Analyze and process large datasets with statistical software.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Download a large public dataset such as a city's bike-share trip records for one year (millions of rows). Write an analysis plan and short report: how you load and process data that may not fit in memory, cleaning rules (zero-length trips, station errors, outliers), three questions you answer (for example, how ridership depends on weather and time of day), the methods and visualizations used, and the limitations. Include the main code steps in pseudocode or a language of your choice and cite the data source.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Scalable processing: Uses chunking, databases or columnar formats appropriately and states hardware assumptions.
- Sound cleaning and methods: Cleaning rules are explicit and methods fit the questions (aggregation, regression with weather data).
- Honest reporting: Visuals are clear and limitations (missing data, station changes, causality) are stated.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Read monthly files into a columnar format; aggregate with a query engine.
- Rules: drop trips under one minute or over 24 hours, fix station ID changes.
- Questions: hourly and weekday patterns, rain and temperature effects, busiest station pairs.
- Methods: grouped summaries, regression of daily trips on weather, heatmaps.
- Limitations: docked bikes only, membership changes, weather station distance.

### 27.0101-P2. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (math classroom norms plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You will teach algebra to four sections of ninth graders using frequent group problem-solving at vertical whiteboards. Write the norms and routines plan: expectations for group work (everyone contributes, mistakes are shared), how groups are formed and roles rotated, routines for starting a task, asking for help and sharing solutions, how you handle off-task behavior or one student doing all the work, and how you introduce the norms in the first week.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Norms for math talk: Norms support productive struggle and respectful critique of reasoning.
- Concrete routines: Grouping, roles, help-seeking and sharing routines are specific and practiced.
- Fair, consistent responses: Responses to off-task or unequal participation are consistent and preserve students' dignity.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Norms: explain your thinking, ask before telling, mistakes help us learn, one marker passes around.
- Visibly random groups of three each day; roles rotate.
- Help routine: ask group first, then signal the teacher with a question.
- Unequal participation: assign the marker to the quietest student; check in privately.
- First week: practice norms with low-stakes puzzles; reflect at end of class.

### 27.0101-P3. `15-2099.01` Bioinformatics Technicians, O*NET task 17708 (database monitoring and maintenance plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Watch how a database performs and carry out the maintenance, upgrades or repairs it needs.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A genomics lab runs a PostgreSQL database of 400 million variant records that researchers query through a web portal; queries have slowed and the database version reaches end of support next year. Write a monitoring and maintenance plan: the performance metrics you track (query latency, slow-query logs, index usage, disk, cache hit rate), alert thresholds, routine maintenance (vacuum, statistics, backups with restore tests), how you diagnose a slow query, and the upgrade plan with testing and rollback.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Meaningful monitoring: Metrics and thresholds reflect database health and user experience.
- Correct maintenance: Routine tasks and backup restore testing are appropriate for the database engine.
- Safe upgrade: Upgrade plan includes a test environment, compatibility checks, a maintenance window and rollback.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Track p95 query time, slow-query log, connections, disk growth, replication lag.
- Alerts with thresholds and an on-call contact.
- Maintenance: autovacuum tuning, analyze statistics, weekly full backup and monthly restore test.
- Diagnose with query plans; add or adjust indexes; partition large tables by chromosome.
- Upgrade: clone to test server, run portal test suite, schedule window, keep old cluster for rollback.

### 27.0101-P4. `15-2051.00` Data Scientists, O*NET task 21824 (feature selection study)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Use feature-selection methods on models that predict outcomes such as sales, customer loss or health-care use.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A subscription meal-kit company gives you a dataset of 50,000 customers with 120 candidate features (order history, delivery issues, discounts used, survey answers) and asks for a model to predict cancellation in the next 60 days. Write the feature-selection plan: how you split data to avoid leakage (including features recorded after cancellation), at least two selection methods compared (for example, L1-regularized logistic regression and permutation importance from a tree model), how you evaluate the final model, and how you explain the selected features to the business team.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Leakage prevention: Uses time-based splits and removes features that would not be known at prediction time.
- Sound selection methods: Compares at least two methods correctly, with cross-validation and stability checks.
- Evaluation and explanation: Reports appropriate metrics (AUC, precision at a contact budget) and explains features without implying causation.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Train on older customers, validate on later months; prediction date fixed for each customer.
- Drop leaked fields such as cancellation survey answers.
- L1 logistic path versus permutation importance in gradient boosting; keep features stable across folds.
- Evaluate AUC and precision among the top 10 percent flagged.
- Explain with plain charts; note correlations are not causes; propose retention test.

### 27.0101-P5. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6640 (lesson plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach a subject through explanation, discussion and demonstration.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan a 50-minute Algebra 2 lesson introducing exponential growth. Include a demonstration (for example, folding paper or a simulated viral-sharing activity), a short explanation contrasting linear and exponential patterns in tables, graphs and equations, a discussion of a real context such as compound interest, and a formative check. Explain how you address the misconception that exponential growth is just fast linear growth.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Mathematical accuracy: Correctly distinguishes constant differences from constant ratios across representations.
- Engaging demonstration: Demonstration generates data students analyze.
- Misconception and check: Directly confronts the misconception and checks understanding with a task that requires distinguishing the two.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Hook: would you take a fixed daily amount or a penny doubled daily for 30 days?
- Demo: paper folding thickness table.
- Compare tables: add versus multiply; graphs and equations.
- Discuss compound interest with real rates.
- Check: classify four tables as linear or exponential and justify.

### 27.0101-P6. `15-2099.01` Bioinformatics Technicians, O*NET task 17714 (change and incident documentation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Record every change, modification and problem in a database.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. In the genomics database from your earlier task, you added an index, changed a column type, and fixed a bug that had mislabeled 2,300 variant records for three weeks. Write the documentation: a change-log template and filled entries for the two changes, an incident report for the mislabeling bug (timeline, cause, affected data, users notified, fix, prevention), and the rules your team will follow so every future change is documented before it reaches production.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete change records: Entries include date, author, reason, exact change, testing done, approval and rollback steps.
- Thorough incident report: Report covers timeline, root cause, scope of affected data, notification and prevention.
- Sustainable process: Defines a lightweight but enforced process (version-controlled migrations, review before production).

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Change log fields and two filled examples.
- Incident timeline from introduction to detection to fix.
- Affected records listed by query; researchers who downloaded them notified.
- Prevention: automated test for label mapping, peer review.
- Process: migrations in version control, ticket required, release notes posted to users.
