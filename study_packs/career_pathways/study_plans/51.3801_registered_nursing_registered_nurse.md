## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (clinical documentation, care plans, and an evidence-based practice paper), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Speaking (giving structured SBAR handoffs in simulation lab); Active Listening (therapeutic communication practice with standardized patients); Critical Thinking (clinical judgment case studies and prioritization exercises).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Medicine and Dentistry (anatomy and physiology, pathophysiology, pharmacology, and adult health nursing); Psychology (developmental and abnormal psychology and psychiatric-mental health nursing); Customer and Personal Service (patient-centered communication practiced in clinical rotations).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Registered Nurses; Acute Care Nurses; Critical Care Nurses): Education and Training (patient and family teaching, including discharge education with teach-back); Therapy and Counseling (therapeutic communication and motivational interviewing basics); Biology (anatomy and physiology, microbiology, and pathophysiology with labs). Keep building Reading Comprehension (reading drug references and nursing research articles for evidence-based practice); Monitoring (tracking vital-sign trends and early-warning scores during clinical rotations); Active Learning (applying new clinical evidence in care-plan assignments and simulation debriefs) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Registered Nurses; Acute Care Nurses; Critical Care Nurses; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Medicine and Dentistry, Psychology.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Mathematics (3.35): ranked below the knowledge areas the outline already covers (the outline gives at most six course slots to knowledge); worth an elective if a target role needs it.

### 51.3801-P1. `29-1141.00` Registered Nurses, O*NET task 1839 (charting and handoff exercise)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Keep patient records and reports that are accurate, complete and detailed.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: on a medical-surgical unit, Mr. R., 72, two days after bowel surgery, became confused at 02:00, pulled at his IV, had a temperature of 38.4 C and a heart rate of 112. You notified the on-call provider, a blood culture was drawn and his daughter was called. Write (1) the narrative or focused nursing note for these events and (2) the SBAR handoff you would give the day nurse at 07:00. Then list three charting errors a reviewer commonly finds in notes like this and how your note avoids them.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Accurate, objective record: The note is factual, timed, and objective (observed behaviors and measured values, not labels like 'difficult'), and includes interventions and the patient's response.
- Complete handoff: SBAR covers situation, relevant background, current assessment and a clear recommendation or pending items such as culture results.
- Error awareness: Identifies real charting pitfalls (late entries without labeling, vague times, copying forward, unapproved abbreviations) and shows how the note avoids them.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Timed entries: assessment findings, provider notification time and orders received, actions taken, reassessment.
- Describes confusion with orientation findings and safety measures (bed alarm, IV secured) rather than judgments.
- SBAR: new-onset confusion with fever and tachycardia; post-op day 2; current vitals and mental status; recommend follow-up on cultures and reassessment.
- Pitfalls: missing times, unexplained abbreviations, charting care before it is done; corrections made per policy.
- Family notification documented with who was called and what was said.

### 51.3801-P2. `29-1141.01` Acute Care Nurses, O*NET task 18297 (risk-benefit analysis)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Weigh the reasons for and against a treatment, its possible complications, and its costs against its benefits.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: Mrs. T., 81, is on day 3 after hip-fracture repair and still has an indwelling urinary catheter placed in surgery. The night nurse wants to keep it because she is slow to walk to the bathroom. Write a one-page analysis comparing keeping the catheter with removing it today: clinical indications and contraindications for each option, risks (including infection, retention, falls, skin breakdown), cost considerations, what you would assess before deciding, and the recommendation you would bring to the provider. Cite the type of evidence (e.g. hospital protocol, nursing guidelines) you would check.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Indications reasoning: Correctly distinguishes valid catheter indications (such as retention or strict output monitoring) from convenience, and applies them to the case.
- Balanced risks and costs: Compares infection and retention risk with fall and mobility risk, and notes cost of a hospital-acquired infection versus nursing time.
- Safe recommendation: Recommends a clear action with a monitoring plan (bladder scan, voiding trial, toileting schedule) and when to escalate.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Convenience is not an indication; check for retention, strict output needs or sacral wounds.
- Keeping it raises catheter-associated urinary tract infection risk with each day; removal risks retention and falls if toileting support is poor.
- Assess voiding history, bladder scan capability, mobility and cognition, pain control.
- Recommend removal with a voiding trial, scheduled toileting, fall precautions and bladder scanning per protocol.
- Check the unit's catheter-removal protocol and current nursing guidance on catheter-associated infection prevention.

### 51.3801-P3. `29-1141.03` Critical Care Nurses, O*NET task 18326 (care procedure with family communication)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Care for a patient's body after death in a respectful way.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a patient in the ICU has died after the family chose comfort care. The family is on the way, the patient is Muslim, and the attending mentioned the case may need medical examiner review because of a recent fall. Write the after-death care procedure you would follow: what you check before touching lines and tubes, how you prepare the body and room for the family, how you respect religious and cultural wishes, the notifications required (including organ and tissue donation referral), and what you document.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct sequencing and legal checks: Confirms pronouncement and whether a medical examiner case requires leaving lines and tubes in place before removing anything.
- Dignity and culture: Prepares the body and room respectfully, asks the family about religious practices (such as same-sex handling or washing), and offers time and support.
- Notifications and documentation: Includes required notifications (provider, donation organization per hospital policy, chaplain if wanted) and documents times, belongings and who was present.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Verify time of death documentation; ask whether the case is reportable before removing devices.
- If not reportable, remove or cap devices per policy; position the body, close eyes, clean gently, fresh linen, dim lights.
- Ask the family about rituals; offer a chaplain or imam; respect requests regarding who handles the body.
- Notify the organ procurement organization within the required time per policy.
- Document belongings, notifications, family presence and transfer to the morgue or funeral home.

### 51.3801-P4. `29-1141.00` Registered Nurses, O*NET task 1840 (monitoring and escalation plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Watch for symptoms or changes in a patient's condition, record them and report them.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: you are caring for five patients on a step-down unit. One, a 58-year-old with pneumonia, had a respiratory rate of 18 and oxygen saturation of 95 percent on 2 L at 08:00; at 11:00 the rate is 26, saturation 90 percent and she says she feels 'off'. Write (1) a monitoring plan for this patient for the rest of the shift, (2) how you calculate an early-warning score or apply your unit's rapid-response criteria, (3) the exact SBAR call to the provider, and (4) what you record and when.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Recognition of change: Identifies the trend as deterioration, considers other vital signs and mental status, and reassesses promptly rather than waiting for the next scheduled check.
- Escalation: Applies early-warning or rapid-response criteria correctly and gives a concise SBAR with a specific request.
- Recording: Records values, times, interventions and responses, and states the reassessment interval.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Immediate reassessment: full vitals, work of breathing, lung sounds, mental status, oxygen delivery check.
- Calculate an early-warning score from respiratory rate, saturation, heart rate, blood pressure, temperature and consciousness; trigger the rapid-response team if the threshold is met.
- SBAR: rising respiratory rate and falling saturation over three hours; request bedside evaluation and orders such as blood gas or chest imaging.
- Interventions within scope: raise head of bed, titrate oxygen per protocol.
- Document each value with time, notification time, response and recheck every 15 to 30 minutes until stable.

### 51.3801-P5. `29-1141.01` Acute Care Nurses, O*NET task 18300 (pain and sedation care plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Manage pain and sedation with both drug and non-drug measures, watch how the patient responds, and change the plan as needed.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: Mr. L., 45, is one day after open abdominal surgery, using a patient-controlled analgesia pump with an opioid. He rates pain 8/10 when coughing, 4/10 at rest, and has snoring episodes with brief pauses in breathing; he has a history of sleep apnea and his CPAP machine is at home. Write a nursing care plan: assessment tools you will use (pain and sedation scales), non-drug measures, what you would ask the provider about multimodal analgesia, the monitoring needed for respiratory depression, and the criteria that would make you change or escalate the plan.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Assessment tools: Uses a validated pain scale and a sedation scale (such as POSS or RASS) and assesses function, not only pain numbers.
- Safety with opioids: Recognizes sleep apnea plus opioids as high risk and plans continuous monitoring (pulse oximetry, capnography if available) and getting CPAP.
- Adaptive plan: Includes non-drug measures, multimodal options to discuss, and explicit criteria for holding doses or escalating.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Pain at rest and with activity; sedation score before and after doses.
- Non-drug measures: splinting pillow for coughing, positioning, early walking, cold or heat as allowed.
- Ask about scheduled non-opioid analgesics or regional techniques to reduce opioid need.
- Continuous oxygen saturation and, if available, end-tidal CO2 monitoring; arrange for his CPAP or a hospital device.
- Hold or reduce opioid and notify the provider if sedation rises or respiratory rate falls below protocol limits; naloxone available.

### 51.3801-P6. `29-1141.03` Critical Care Nurses, O*NET task 18337 (interdisciplinary rounds preparation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Partner with the wider care team to build and update the plan of care, grounded in the patient's needs and assessment findings.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: an ICU patient, 67, is on day 6 of mechanical ventilation for pneumonia, is now more alert, has not had a bowel movement in four days, and has a stage 2 pressure injury on the sacrum. Prepare what you will bring to tomorrow's interdisciplinary rounds: a two-minute nursing summary, the specific questions or proposals for the physician, respiratory therapist, pharmacist, dietitian and physical therapist, and how you will record agreed changes to the plan of care and follow up on them.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Focused summary: The summary prioritizes the issues most relevant to the plan (readiness to wean, mobility, skin, bowels) with current assessment data.
- Discipline-specific asks: Each team member gets a concrete question or proposal within their expertise (spontaneous breathing trial, bowel regimen review, nutrition for wound healing, early mobility).
- Closing the loop: Changes are documented in the care plan with owners and times, and the nurse checks they happen.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Summary: vent settings and trend, sedation level, skin and bowel status, family update.
- Respiratory therapist and physician: is the patient ready for a sedation pause and spontaneous breathing trial?
- Pharmacist: review opioid and sedative doses and add a bowel regimen; dietitian: protein and calories for wound healing.
- Physical therapist: progressive mobility plan; nursing: repositioning schedule and support surface.
- Record agreed actions with owners; check at end of shift and report to the next nurse.
