## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading datasheets, standards, and application notes); Critical Thinking (troubleshooting circuits methodically with measurements); Speaking (presenting design reviews and capstone demos).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Engineering and Technology (circuit analysis, signals and systems, electronics, and power systems); Computers and Electronics (digital logic, microcontrollers, and programming for embedded systems); Design (circuit and PCB design, control systems design, and a capstone project).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Electrical Engineers; Electronics Engineers, Except Computer; Computer Hardware Engineers: Mathematics (calculus through differential equations, linear algebra, complex variables, and probability); English Language (lab reports and technical specifications); Physics (calculus-based electricity and magnetism and electromagnetics). Keep building Active Listening (requirements gathering with project sponsors); Writing (documenting test procedures and results) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Electrical Engineers; Electronics Engineers, Except Computer; Computer Hardware Engineers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Engineering and Technology, Computers and Electronics.

### 14.1001-P1. `17-2072.00` Electronics Engineers, Except Computer, O*NET task 5339 (circuit design proposal)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Design electronic parts, devices and systems for uses ranging from business and industry to medicine, defense and science.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A greenhouse cooperative wants a low-cost, battery-powered light sensor node that measures photosynthetically active light from near darkness to full sun and reports hourly by Bluetooth to a phone. Write a design proposal: block diagram, choice of sensor and why, the analog front-end or digital interface with its range and resolution, power budget and expected battery life, enclosure and environmental concerns (humidity, heat), and how you would calibrate and test it.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Coherent architecture: Block diagram and component choices fit the requirements (range, resolution, low power, wireless).
- Quantified design: Includes numbers for measurement range, resolution, current draw and battery life with the arithmetic shown.
- Validation: Describes calibration against a reference meter and environmental testing.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Blocks: light sensor, microcontroller with Bluetooth Low Energy, battery and regulator, enclosure with diffuser.
- Sensor choice: photodiode with filter or a digital light sensor; dynamic range covered with auto-ranging.
- Power: sleep current in microamps, brief wake every hour; battery life estimate in months or years.
- Enclosure: sealed, UV-stable, condensation control, cosine-corrected diffuser.
- Calibrate against a reference quantum sensor at several light levels; humidity and temperature cycling.

### 14.1001-P2. `17-2071.00` Electrical Engineers, O*NET task 1347 (client requirements meeting)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Meet clients, fellow engineers and other parties to discuss engineering projects and products, existing or planned.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A dairy farm owner wants a rooftop solar array with battery storage so the milking equipment and milk cooling keep running during grid outages, which happen several times each winter. Prepare for and write up the requirements meeting: the questions you will ask (loads, outage duration, budget, utility rules, future expansion), a simple load table you will fill in together, how you will explain the trade-off between battery size and outage coverage in plain language, and the written summary of agreed requirements and open items.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Right questions: Covers critical loads, motor starting currents, outage length, utility interconnection rules, budget and site constraints.
- Clear explanation: Explains battery capacity, power rating and outage coverage in terms the owner can use to decide.
- Documented outcome: Ends with a summary of requirements, assumptions, open questions and next steps.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Load inventory: vacuum pump, milk cooling compressor, water heater, lights; running and starting power.
- Outage history and acceptable outage coverage target.
- Plain explanation: power rating sets what can run at once; storage capacity sets for how long.
- Utility interconnection and net-metering rules; roof structural check.
- Summary memo with critical-load list, target backup hours, budget range and follow-ups.

### 14.1001-P3. `17-2061.00` Computer Hardware Engineers, O*NET task 7391 (technology scan and learning plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Keep one's knowledge and skills current as computer technology changes quickly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your hardware team must decide within a year whether to move future embedded products from an Arm-based microcontroller family to a RISC-V-based family. Write a technology-scan brief and a personal learning plan: summarize the current state of both ecosystems in your own words with cited sources (vendor documentation, standards bodies, technical articles), list what you need to learn to evaluate them fairly (toolchains, debug support, security features, supply), and a three-month plan of courses, evaluation boards and small experiments with how you will share findings with the team.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Sourced and current: Uses verifiable, recent sources and distinguishes vendor claims from independent evidence.
- Decision-relevant criteria: Learning targets connect to the actual product decision (tools, ecosystem maturity, cost, long-term availability).
- Concrete plan: The plan has specific activities, deliverables and a way to share results with colleagues.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Brief: instruction set licensing, available parts, toolchain and debugger support, real-time operating system support.
- Criteria: power, performance per dollar, security features, supplier longevity, team skills.
- Plan: month 1 reading and an online course; month 2 port a small firmware module to a RISC-V board; month 3 benchmark and write up.
- Share via a lunch-and-learn and a written comparison table.
- Note uncertainty and what would change the recommendation.

### 14.1001-P4. `17-2072.00` Electronics Engineers, Except Computer, O*NET task 5346 (incoming inspection procedure)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Inspect electronic equipment and systems to confirm they meet specifications, safety standards and applicable codes.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your company buys 500 external 48 V power supplies from a new vendor for a medical cart product. Write the incoming inspection procedure: the sampling plan, visual and marking checks (safety certification marks, ratings label), electrical tests (output voltage regulation, ripple, efficiency, leakage current, dielectric withstand on a sample), the records you keep, and what happens with a lot that fails. Explain why medical use raises the bar compared with office equipment.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Appropriate tests: Tests match the specification and safety standards for medical electrical equipment (e.g. leakage current limits) with the right instruments.
- Sampling and records: Uses a stated sampling plan with accept and reject numbers and records serials, results and instrument calibration.
- Nonconformance handling: Defines quarantine, supplier notification and disposition steps for a failed lot.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Sampling per a standard acceptance plan; destructive dielectric testing only on a small sample.
- Markings: certification to the relevant medical safety standard, ratings, polarity, country of origin.
- Electrical: regulation from no load to full load, ripple with a bandwidth-limited oscilloscope, earth and touch leakage current.
- Records with instrument IDs and calibration dates.
- Failed lot: quarantine tag, supplier corrective action request, disposition by quality engineer.

### 14.1001-P5. `17-2071.00` Electrical Engineers, O*NET task 1350 (installation and commissioning coordination plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Coordinate installation, testing, maintenance or documentation work so it complies with specifications, codes and customer requirements.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A parking garage will install 20 Level 2 electric-vehicle chargers on a new 400 A service, with load management so the chargers share capacity. You are the electrical engineer coordinating the electrical contractor, the charger vendor, the utility and the building inspector. Write the coordination plan: the sequence from submittals to energization, the inspections and tests required, how you verify the installation meets the drawings and the electrical code, how load management is commissioned, and the documents handed over to the owner.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Logical sequence: Steps from submittal review through rough-in, inspection, utility energization and commissioning are in the right order with dependencies.
- Code and spec compliance: Identifies key verification points (conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, ground-fault protection, labeling) and required inspections.
- Commissioning and handover: Includes functional testing of load management under realistic loads and a complete turnover package.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Submittals: charger data sheets, panel schedules, single-line diagram; engineer review.
- Rough-in inspection, then final inspection by the authority having jurisdiction.
- Tests: insulation resistance, torque checks, ground-fault protection operation, phase rotation.
- Load management commissioning with several vehicles or test loads to confirm current sharing and limits.
- Turnover: as-built drawings, test reports, warranty, network configuration and training.

### 14.1001-P6. `17-2061.00` Computer Hardware Engineers, O*NET task 7397 (hardware recommendation)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Study what users need and recommend suitable computer hardware.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A college media department is replacing 24 workstations used for 4K video editing, color grading and some 3D animation, with a budget of about $3,000 per seat. Interview notes (invented) say renders take too long, the shared storage is slow, and two editors are color-blind-friendly-display advocates. Write the recommendation: how you translated user needs into hardware requirements (CPU, GPU, memory, storage, displays, network), two configurations compared, the benchmark or test you would run before buying, and the final recommendation within budget.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Needs-to-specs mapping: Each hardware choice is traced to a user need or workload characteristic.
- Comparison and testing: Two configurations are compared fairly, with a realistic pilot benchmark using the department's actual projects.
- Budget and system view: Stays within budget and addresses the network and shared-storage bottleneck, not only the desktop.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Workload: GPU-accelerated editing and grading, CPU threads for rendering, 32 to 64 GB memory, fast local scratch storage.
- Displays: color-accurate panels with calibration hardware.
- Bottleneck: 10-gigabit network to the shared storage or local cache workflow.
- Pilot: render and playback tests on two loaner configurations with real department projects.
- Recommendation with per-seat cost and the network upgrade cost.
