## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (APA-style research writing and literature reviews), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading and summarizing empirical journal articles weekly); Active Listening (practicing reflective listening in a helping-skills or interviewing course); Critical Thinking (evaluating study designs and claims about causation in research methods).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Psychology (introductory, developmental, cognitive, social, and abnormal psychology); Therapy and Counseling (introduction to counseling and clinical psychology: interviewing skills, ethics, and evidence-based therapies); Mathematics (statistics and research methods sequence).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Social Science Research Assistants): Computers and Electronics (statistical software such as R, SPSS, or Python, plus survey platforms); Sociology and Anthropology (social psychology and cultural psychology, plus one sociology course on groups and institutions). Keep building Writing (writing lab reports and a research proposal with revision); Speaking (presenting a research poster at an undergraduate conference); Active Learning (applying new research findings to class debates and research proposals) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Social Science Research Assistants; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Psychology, Therapy and Counseling.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Education and Training (3.8): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.
- Customer and Personal Service (3.5): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.

### 42.0101-P1. `19-4061.00` Social Science Research Assistants, O*NET task 12952 (data management plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Build, clean, transform and look after large research datasets.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A psychology lab ran an online survey on sleep and stress with 2,400 student responses exported from the survey platform: wide format, item names like Q14_3, some reverse-scored items, attention-check questions, and a free-text 'major' field. Write a data management plan for turning this into an analysis-ready dataset: a codebook template, the cleaning steps in order (including attention-check exclusions and reverse scoring), how you compute scale scores, how you keep the raw file untouched and record every change, and how identifying information is protected.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Reproducible pipeline: Raw data stays read-only and all transformations are scripted or logged so another person could rebuild the dataset.
- Correct psychometric steps: Reverse-scoring, scale scoring and handling of missing items are described correctly, and exclusion rules are stated before looking at outcomes.
- Data protection: Identifiers are separated or removed, storage and access are controlled, and the plan follows the study's consent and ethics approval.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Codebook: variable name, label, item wording reference, response scale, reverse-scored flag, missing codes.
- Order: import, rename, flag failed attention checks, recode reverse items, compute scales, recode free-text majors into categories.
- Scale scores as mean of items with a stated minimum number answered.
- Raw export kept read-only; analysis script under version control; change log.
- Remove emails and IP addresses into a separate locked file; access limited to approved staff.

### 42.0101-P2. `19-3039.02` Neuropsychologists, O*NET task 22193 (assessment session plan (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Carry out neuropsychological evaluations covering abilities such as intelligence, attention, language, learning and memory.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice: neuropsychological testing is administered and interpreted by licensed psychologists or trained psychometrists under supervision. Imagine you are a research assistant helping a supervising neuropsychologist plan a four-hour outpatient evaluation for a 66-year-old retired teacher whose family reports forgetfulness. Draft the session plan: the cognitive domains to cover and why, examples of the kinds of standardized measures used for each domain, the order of testing and breaks, how fatigue, hearing or vision problems and language background affect validity, and what information you would collect before the visit.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Domain coverage: Covers the relevant domains (memory, attention, processing speed, language, visuospatial, executive function, mood screening) and justifies each for a memory complaint.
- Validity awareness: Addresses performance validity, sensory limits, fatigue, education and language effects on norms.
- Scope and ethics: Correctly keeps administration and interpretation with qualified staff and includes consent and confidentiality.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Pre-visit: referral question, medical history, medications, education, primary language, collateral report from family.
- Domains with example measure types, such as list-learning memory tests and timed attention tasks, plus a depression screen.
- Order: harder memory tasks early with delayed recall intervals planned; breaks every hour.
- Check hearing aids and glasses; choose norms appropriate to age and education; include validity indicators.
- Assistant's role limited to scheduling, setup and supervised tasks; results reported by the neuropsychologist.

### 42.0101-P3. `19-3039.03` Clinical Neuropsychologists, O*NET task 22207 (pre-post change analysis)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Compare how patients are doing before and after a medication, surgery or behavioral treatment.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented data: a clinic tested 12 patients with epilepsy on a verbal memory test before and six months after surgery. Scores (standard scores, mean 100, SD 15): before 92, 88, 101, 85, 97, 90, 105, 83, 94, 99, 87, 91; after 86, 90, 95, 78, 98, 85, 100, 84, 89, 97, 80, 92. The test's test-retest reliability is 0.85. Write a one-page analysis: the group-level change, a reliable change index for individuals, how many patients show reliable decline, what practice effects and regression to the mean mean for interpretation, and how you would present the findings to the surgical team.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct calculations: Computes mean change and a reliable change index correctly from the stated reliability and SD, showing the formula.
- Interpretation: Discusses practice effects, regression to the mean, small sample size and the lack of a control group.
- Useful communication: Presents results clearly to clinicians, separating group trends from individual cases and stating limits.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Mean before about 92.7, after about 89.5; change about minus 3 points.
- Standard error of difference from SD times the square root of 2 times (1 minus reliability); about 8.2, so the RCI threshold is roughly 16 points at 95 percent.
- Few or no patients pass the strict threshold; some show declines of 5 to 7 points worth monitoring.
- Practice effects could hide true decline; repeat testing would ideally use practice-adjusted RCI and a comparison group.
- Report a table per patient and a short summary noting uncertainty.

### 42.0101-P4. `19-4061.00` Social Science Research Assistants, O*NET task 12956 (data verification audit)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Check that data entered into research databases are accurate and valid, and fix the errors found.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Paper questionnaires from 300 participants in a parenting-stress study were typed into a database by four student assistants. Design and run (on paper) a verification audit: how you will select records for double entry, the range and logic checks you will script (for example, a child's age older than the parent's, a 1 to 5 item coded 6), how you will decide what is a correctable error versus missing data, how every correction is recorded, and the error-rate threshold that would trigger re-entering a whole batch.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Sound sampling and checks: Uses a defensible sample (random or full double entry for key fields) and lists specific range, consistency and duplicate checks.
- Correction rules: Corrections are made only against the source document, with clear rules for unresolvable values.
- Audit trail and threshold: Every change is logged (who, when, old and new value), and an explicit error-rate threshold triggers re-entry or retraining.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Double-enter a random 10 to 20 percent of forms plus all primary outcome items.
- Scripted checks: out-of-range codes, impossible combinations, duplicate participant IDs, skipped pages.
- Look up each flagged value on the paper form; if illegible, code as missing with a reason code.
- Change log stored with the dataset; original entries preserved.
- If a batch error rate exceeds about 1 percent of fields, re-enter that batch and retrain that assistant.

### 42.0101-P5. `19-3039.02` Neuropsychologists, O*NET task 22195 (consultation note (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Discuss patients' neurological conditions with other professionals.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice. Invented case: a 16-year-old athlete had a concussion five weeks ago and still reports headaches and trouble concentrating; testing by the supervising neuropsychologist found slowed processing speed but normal memory. Draft the consultation materials the neuropsychologist could use: (1) a short consult note to the treating neurologist, and (2) talking points for a meeting with the school counselor about temporary academic accommodations. Keep medical decisions with the physician and share only information the family has authorized.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Audience-appropriate content: The note to the neurologist is concise and technical; the school talking points are plain-language and focus on function and accommodations.
- Accurate scope: Does not make medical decisions such as clearance to play; defers return-to-sport to the physician and follows concussion protocols.
- Privacy: Shares only what the family has authorized and avoids unnecessary diagnostic detail with the school.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Neurologist note: referral question, key findings (processing speed below expectation, memory within normal limits), impressions, recommendation to re-evaluate in a set number of weeks.
- School points: extra time on tests, rest breaks, reduced screen load, gradual return to full workload.
- Defer return-to-play to the physician's graduated protocol.
- Confirm signed release of information before school contact.
- Plan follow-up contact to review whether accommodations can be reduced.

### 42.0101-P6. `19-3039.03` Clinical Neuropsychologists, O*NET task 22210 (cognitive rehabilitation plan (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Design or deliver plans that help people recover or work around impaired thinking skills.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice. Invented case: a 52-year-old office manager had a stroke four months ago and has trouble remembering appointments, tracking multi-step tasks and staying focused in a noisy office. She wants to return to work part-time in two months. Draft a cognitive rehabilitation plan the supervising clinician could review: goals written in measurable terms, compensatory strategies and tools, any restorative practice, how the employer and family are involved, and how progress is measured every two weeks.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Measurable goals: Goals are specific and functional, e.g. uses a calendar system to keep a stated share of appointments without reminders.
- Evidence-informed strategies: Prioritizes compensatory strategies (external memory aids, checklists, environmental changes) and explains any restorative exercises realistically.
- Monitoring and collaboration: Tracks progress with defined measures and includes the patient, family and employer in planning with consent.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Goals for calendar use, completing multi-step tasks with checklists, and sustained work in 30-minute blocks.
- Tools: smartphone calendar with alarms, written task templates, noise-reducing headphones, quieter workstation.
- Strategy training using goal-setting and self-monitoring routines; practice with real work tasks.
- Workplace plan with graded hours and agreed accommodations.
- Biweekly review of goal attainment and a brief rating scale; adjust plan.
