Method: field fit = field fit = cosine similarity between the CIP program title's field terms and the occupation's O*NET 31.0 text (title, description and all task statements). Words are Snowball-stemmed, generic words (science, studies, technology, engineering, management, education, general, other, ...) are ignored, and terms are weighted by log term frequency times inverse document frequency over all O*NET occupations. Compound title words are also split into the O*NET vocabulary stems of 5+ letters they contain (electromechanical -> electr, mechan; telecommunications -> commun), so a compound does not count as a mismatch. CIP definitions are not in the pinned crosswalk file, so the CIP title is the field description. A rule target is dropped as clearly implausible when (1) its fit is 0, meaning no field term of the program title occurs anywhere in its O*NET title, description or tasks; (2) some eligible linked occupation in the same Job Zone tier has fit >= 0.02, so the crosswalk does offer a field match; (3) it is an O*NET detailed specialty code (not .00), whose link is typically inherited from a broad SOC code such as Engineers, All Other; (4) its base .00 occupation also has fit 0; and (5) its fit with the titles of all CIP codes in the program's 4-digit CIP series (the field family) is below 0.12, so it is not a close neighbour of the field either (e.g. a genetics program keeps Molecular and Cellular Biologists). The thresholds were set by inspecting the candidate drops of this release; they are not validated. Dropped targets are replaced from the same ranked tier with the next occupations whose fit is above 0; if none remain, the plan keeps fewer targets. A base (.00) target with fit 0 is kept but flagged better_field_match_available when a non-target occupation in the same tier has fit >= 0.1. Supervisory check = task statement matches a supervisory/managerial pattern (supervise, provide supervision, direct and coordinate, direct the work/activities/staff of, manage staff/personnel/employees/workers/teams, oversee the work/staff, hire, subordinates, assign work to, evaluate the work/performance of, train and supervise); 'under supervision' does not count.

## 4-year outline (DreamCo-generated template, not O*NET data; element names only, no per-major course mapping)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language, plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension, Critical Thinking, Active Listening.
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Courses in the major that build the highest-rated knowledge areas: Computers and Electronics, Communications and Media, Public Safety and Security.
3. Year 3, depth. Electives aimed at Medical Records Specialists; Health Information Technologists and Medical Registrars, with further work in Law and Government and applied practice in Speaking, Writing, Active Learning.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Medical Records Specialists; Health Information Technologists and Medical Registrars, with a portfolio drawn from the practice exercises below.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Administrative (4.25): office procedures and records handling; usually learned on the job, so it gets no course slot.
- Customer and Personal Service (4.0): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Administration and Management (3.33): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).
- Education and Training (2.7): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.
