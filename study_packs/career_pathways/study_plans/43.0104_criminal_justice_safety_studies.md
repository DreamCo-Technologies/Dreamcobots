## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Speaking (mock testimony and briefing practice); Active Listening (interviewing and de-escalation exercises); Critical Thinking (evaluating evidence in case studies).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Public Safety and Security (policing, corrections, emergency management, and security operations); Law and Government (criminal law, criminal procedure, and constitutional rights); English Language (report writing for justice settings).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for First-Line Supervisors of Correctional Officers: Administration and Management (criminal justice administration and leadership); Psychology (criminology, victimology, and crisis communication); Administrative (records management and case documentation). Keep building Monitoring (situational awareness drills and checklist audits); Reading Comprehension (reading statutes, court opinions, and policies) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at First-Line Supervisors of Correctional Officers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Public Safety and Security, Law and Government.

### 43.0104-P1. `33-1011.00` First-Line Supervisors of Correctional Officers, O*NET task 9428 (count procedure and discrepancy response)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Conduct, receive or check the regular counts of incarcerated people.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Practice for a later supervisory role (supervisor roles normally require prior line experience). You supervise the evening shift in a 400-bed county jail. Write the procedure for a scheduled formal count: how officers count, how counts are reported and reconciled with the population record, the time limits, what happens if the count does not clear (recount, lockdown, notifications), and how the count and any discrepancy are documented.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Accurate procedure: Count method ensures each person is physically observed and reconciled with records.
- Discrepancy response: Clear escalation for a count that does not clear, including recount and notifications.
- Documentation: Records times, officers, numbers, discrepancies and resolution.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- All movement stops; officers count by sight, verifying living, breathing persons.
- Housing units report to central control; reconcile with roster including court, medical and work assignments.
- Recount within set minutes; if still off, lockdown and name check.
- Notify the shift commander and follow escape or emergency procedures.
- Count sheets signed and retained.

### 43.0104-P2. `33-1012.00` First-Line Supervisors of Police and Detectives, O*NET task 564 (roll-call briefing)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Explain police operations and procedures to subordinate officers so they can do their jobs.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Practice for a later supervisory role. Your department adopted an updated body-worn camera policy: cameras must be activated at the start of every enforcement contact, officers may not review footage before writing a use-of-force report until after an initial statement, and certain sensitive locations require notification. Prepare a 10-minute roll-call briefing: key changes, why they matter, three realistic scenarios to discuss, likely questions with answers, and how you will check that officers understand and follow the policy.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear explanation: States changes accurately and explains reasons such as transparency and evidence integrity.
- Scenario-based learning: Scenarios are realistic and promote discussion of judgment calls.
- Compliance verification: Includes follow-up such as footage audits and coaching.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- What changed and effective date.
- Reasons: public trust, evidence quality, legal requirements.
- Scenarios: traffic stop that escalates, medical call, interview in a hospital.
- Answers to questions on privacy and malfunctions.
- Monthly random footage audits and individual feedback.

### 43.0104-P3. `33-1011.00` First-Line Supervisors of Correctional Officers, O*NET task 9432 (shift supervision plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Supervise and direct correctional officers so incarcerated people are kept safely, with discipline and care for their welfare.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Practice for a later supervisory role. You supervise 12 officers on a night shift with two officers out sick, a housing unit with recent fights, and a medical transport scheduled at 03:00. Write the shift plan: post assignments and minimum staffing, briefing priorities, how you supervise rounds and security checks, response plans for a fight or medical emergency, and how you ensure humane treatment and documentation throughout the shift.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Safe staffing: Assigns posts to cover critical areas and handles shortages within policy.
- Supervision practices: Defines checks on rounds, communication and incident response.
- Welfare and documentation: Includes humane treatment, medical access and full documentation.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Post chart; overtime or reassignments to cover minimums.
- Briefing: tension in the unit, transport details, officer safety.
- Supervisor tours and verification of round logs.
- Emergency response roles; medical escort procedures.
- Shift log entries and incident reports before end of shift.

### 43.0104-P4. `33-1012.00` First-Line Supervisors of Police and Detectives, O*NET task 567 (internal complaint investigation plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Look into and resolve personnel problems and complaints of misconduct against staff.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Practice for a later supervisory role. A resident files a complaint that an officer was rude and refused to give his badge number during a traffic stop; footage exists. Write the investigation plan: intake and classification of the complaint, notice to the officer and rights under policy and any labor agreement, evidence to gather (footage, dispatch records, witness statements), interview approach, the standard of proof and possible findings, and how the complainant is informed of the outcome.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Fair process: Follows policy and due-process protections for the officer and treats the complainant respectfully.
- Evidence-based: Collects and weighs objective evidence before interviews and conclusions.
- Clear findings and closure: Uses defined findings categories and communicates the outcome appropriately.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Log and classify as a courtesy complaint; assign investigator.
- Notify officer per contract; preserve footage.
- Gather footage, CAD records, witness information.
- Interviews: complainant first, then officer; record statements.
- Findings: sustained, not sustained, exonerated, unfounded; letter to complainant.

### 43.0104-P5. `33-1011.00` First-Line Supervisors of Correctional Officers, O*NET task 9435 (conduct monitoring and feedback plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Watch how officers behave to make sure they are alert, courteous and professional with incarcerated people, coworkers, visitors and the public.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Practice for a later supervisory role. After visitor complaints about disrespectful treatment at a jail's visiting area, you are asked to improve officers' professional conduct. Write the monitoring and feedback plan: observable behaviors you will look for, how you observe (walkthroughs, camera review, visitor feedback), how you give feedback and coaching, recognition for good conduct, and when you escalate to formal discipline.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Defined behaviors: Professional conduct is described in observable terms.
- Multiple monitoring methods: Uses direct observation, video and feedback sources fairly.
- Coaching and escalation: Balances coaching and recognition with consistent escalation.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Behaviors: greeting, clear explanations of rules, calm tone, no sarcasm.
- Weekly walkthroughs, random video review, visitor comment cards.
- Same-day private feedback; coaching plans.
- Recognition in briefings and evaluations.
- Repeated or serious incidents referred under the discipline policy.

### 43.0104-P6. `33-1012.00` First-Line Supervisors of Police and Detectives, O*NET task 569 (records and reporting plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Keep logs, write reports and direct how departmental records are produced, handled and stored.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Practice for a later supervisory role. Your squad's incident reports are often late or incomplete, and a recent court case was harmed by a missing evidence log entry. Write a records plan for your squad: report quality standards, review and approval timelines, evidence and chain-of-custody logging, storage and retention requirements, public records requests, and how you train officers and track compliance.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Quality standards: Defines what complete, accurate reports must include.
- Chain of custody and retention: Addresses evidence logging, secure storage and retention rules.
- Compliance tracking: Includes training and measurable tracking such as on-time report rate.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Report standards: who, what, when, where, how, narrative clarity.
- Supervisor review within one shift; returned reports corrected within 24 hours.
- Evidence logged at intake with continuous custody records.
- Retention schedule; public records requests routed to records unit.
- Weekly metrics and refresher training.
