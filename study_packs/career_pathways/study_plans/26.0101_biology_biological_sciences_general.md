## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading primary research articles in journal clubs); Active Listening (following lab protocols explained verbally and confirming steps with mentors); Speaking (presenting research at a departmental symposium).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Biology (cell and molecular biology, genetics, ecology, and evolution with labs); English Language (scientific writing: lab reports and a journal-style research paper); Computers and Electronics (bioinformatics tools and data analysis in R or Python).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Biological Technicians; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Clinical Research Coordinators: Customer and Personal Service (working with research participants or the public, such as clinic volunteering or museum outreach); Education and Training (serving as a lab teaching assistant or tutoring introductory biology); Administrative (lab notebook, sample tracking, and regulatory documentation practice). Keep building Critical Thinking (designing controlled experiments and interpreting unexpected results); Writing (drafting methods and results sections with peer review) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Biological Technicians; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Clinical Research Coordinators; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Biology, English Language.

### 26.0101-P1. `19-4021.00` Biological Technicians, O*NET task 3737 (lab notebook entries)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Keep a thorough written log of all lab work activities.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are a technician running a PCR assay to screen 24 mosquito samples for a virus. During the run the thermocycler showed a lid-temperature error, you repeated two samples, and the positive control came up weaker than usual. Write the lab notebook entries for the day as you would in a regulated lab: header information, reagents with lot numbers and expiry, the plate map, the protocol and any deviations, the instrument error and what you did about it, results, and how corrections are made in the record.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Completeness: Entries let another technician repeat the work: reagents, lots, volumes, equipment IDs, plate map, cycling conditions.
- Deviations and problems: The instrument error, repeats and weak control are recorded factually with actions taken and their impact on results.
- Record integrity: Entries are dated and signed, corrections are single-line strike-throughs or tracked edits with reason, and nothing is left for later memory.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Header: date, project, technician, sample IDs and chain of custody.
- Reagent table with lot numbers and expiry; master mix calculation.
- Plate map with positive, negative and no-template controls.
- Deviation: lid error at a stated cycle; repeated affected wells; noted weak positive control and decision on validity.
- Results summary with interpretation criteria; supervisor review signature.

### 26.0101-P2. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (classroom procedures plan (biology lab))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are starting as a high school biology teacher with five sections of 28 students, and the first lab is a microscope and live-specimen activity in week two. Write your classroom routines and lab-safety rules plan: the five rules you will teach, entry and exit routines, how materials are handed out and collected, what happens when a rule is broken (with escalating steps), and how you will teach and practice the routines in the first week rather than just posting them.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Clear, positive rules: Rules are few, specific and stated as expected behaviors, including lab safety items (goggles, handling glass slides, hand washing).
- Workable routines: Routines for entry, materials and cleanup fit the lab space and time and reduce idle time.
- Fair consequences and teaching: Consequences escalate fairly and consistently, and routines are modeled and rehearsed, not only announced.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Rules: follow lab instructions the first time, protect your eyes and hands, handle glassware and specimens as shown, keep your station clean, respect others' work.
- Entry: warm-up question on the board; materials pickup by table captains.
- Cleanup timer five minutes before the bell; checklist per station.
- Consequences: reminder, private conversation, temporary removal from hands-on work with an alternative assignment, contact home.
- Week-one rehearsal: practice runs, a safety quiz, and a signed safety contract.

### 26.0101-P3. `11-9121.01` Clinical Research Coordinators, O*NET task 15583 (current-awareness brief)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Stay current with clinical research practice and issues through reading the literature, continuing education and professional meetings.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You coordinate studies at a university clinical research office and the team asks you to keep them current. Prepare a one-page monthly current-awareness brief: choose two recent items you can actually find (for example, a regulatory guidance update on informed consent or decentralized trials, and a published paper on participant retention), summarize each in your own words, explain what changes for your studies, and list the sources you monitor regularly with how often you check them. Also describe how you would document continuing-education hours.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Credible sources: Items come from verifiable sources (regulator websites, peer-reviewed journals, professional associations) and are cited.
- Practical implications: Each summary ends with concrete implications for the team's current studies or procedures.
- Sustainable routine: Describes a realistic monitoring routine and continuing-education tracking, such as for coordinator certification.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Two cited items with dates and links, summarized in plain language.
- Implications such as updating consent templates or retention procedures.
- Monitoring list: regulator guidance pages, ethics board newsletters, two or three journals, a professional coordinator association.
- Monthly cadence; brief circulated by email and discussed at the team meeting.
- Continuing-education log with date, topic, provider and hours.

### 26.0101-P4. `19-4021.00` Biological Technicians, O*NET task 3738 (lab compliance checklist)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Oversee laboratory work to make sure it meets required standards.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your biosafety level 2 teaching-and-research lab is preparing for an internal biosafety inspection in a month, and a recent near miss involved a student pipetting a bacterial culture outside the biosafety cabinet. Create a monthly self-inspection checklist and corrective-action procedure: items covering engineering controls, personal protective equipment, waste, training records and practices; the pass condition for each item; and how findings are assigned, fixed and verified.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Relevant standards: Checklist items reflect real BSL-2 expectations (cabinet certification, signage, sharps and biohazard waste, eye wash checks, training records).
- Measurable items: Each item has an observable pass condition, not vague wording.
- Corrective-action loop: Findings get an owner, due date and verification, and the near miss leads to retraining and a root-cause review.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Cabinet certified within the last 12 months; airflow indicator working.
- Biohazard signage with agent and contact; door closed during work.
- PPE available and used; no open-toed shoes; gloves removed before leaving.
- Waste: sharps containers below the fill line, autoclave logs complete.
- Training records current for all users; corrective-action log reviewed by the lab manager.

### 26.0101-P5. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6640 (lesson plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach a subject through explanation, discussion and demonstration.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan a 50-minute lesson for 10th-grade biology on natural selection. Include a short teacher explanation, a hands-on demonstration or simulation (for example, a predator-prey 'bead' or 'paper moth' activity), a structured discussion with three questions that push students past the misconception that individuals change because they need to, and a two-question exit ticket. Note how you would support English learners and a student with a visual impairment.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Science accuracy: Explains variation, inheritance, selection and change in populations over generations correctly and targets a known misconception.
- Active learning: The demonstration produces data students use in discussion; timing is realistic for 50 minutes.
- Inclusion and checks: Includes specific supports for English learners and the visually impaired student and an exit ticket that tests understanding, not recall.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Hook question and 8-minute explanation using a real example such as beak size in finches.
- Simulation: colored beads on patterned fabric, three rounds of 'predation', students record surviving colors.
- Discussion questions: did any bead change color; why did the population change; what would happen if the background changed.
- Supports: word wall with visuals, sentence frames, tactile beads of different textures, partner roles.
- Exit ticket: explain a population change scenario in two sentences.

### 26.0101-P6. `11-9121.01` Clinical Research Coordinators, O*NET task 15587 (study visit procedure)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Carry out the procedures a study protocol requires at participant visits, such as interviews, vital signs and electrocardiograms.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented protocol: a blood-pressure medication trial requires, at week 4, a structured symptom interview, seated blood pressure taken three times after five minutes of rest, a 12-lead ECG, and a pill count. Write the visit procedure you would follow as the coordinator: preparation before the participant arrives, the step-by-step visit order, exact measurement conditions, what you do if a reading exceeds a safety threshold in the protocol, and how each item is recorded in the source documents.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Protocol fidelity: Follows the stated conditions exactly (rest time, cuff size, three readings, order of procedures) and checks the visit window.
- Participant safety: Defines the response to out-of-range readings or reported adverse events, including notifying the investigator promptly.
- Source documentation: Records values, times, equipment IDs, deviations and the coordinator's signature in source documents before entry into the study database.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Before: confirm visit window, consent version, calibrated equipment, ECG electrodes, case report form.
- Order: interview first, then rest and blood pressure, then ECG, then pill count and return of unused study drug.
- Correct cuff size, arm supported at heart level, no talking, readings one minute apart.
- If above the protocol threshold, repeat, notify the investigator the same day, and document as a possible adverse event.
- Source notes signed and dated; deviations reported per the sponsor's process.
