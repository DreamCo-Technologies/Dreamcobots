## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Critical Thinking (isolating faults across hardware and software layers using oscilloscope and debugger evidence); Reading Comprehension (reading component datasheets, reference manuals, and application notes); Active Listening (requirements meetings in team design projects, confirming specifications before building).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Computers and Electronics (digital logic, computer architecture, embedded systems, and operating systems); Engineering and Technology (circuits, signals and systems, and a hardware-software capstone); English Language (engineering lab reports, datasheet-style specifications, and design reviews in writing).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Computer Hardware Engineers; Software Developers; Software Quality Assurance Analysts and Testers: Mathematics (calculus through differential equations, linear algebra, discrete math, and probability); Design (digital design with hardware description languages, printed-circuit-board layout, and system design reviews); Education and Training (mentoring first-year students in a makerspace or serving as a lab assistant for a logic-design course). Keep building Speaking (presenting design reviews and capstone demos to faculty and industry sponsors); Writing (writing test reports and design documentation that another engineer could follow) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Computer Hardware Engineers; Software Developers; Software Quality Assurance Analysts and Testers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Computers and Electronics, Engineering and Technology.

### 14.0901-P1. `15-1253.00` Software Quality Assurance Analysts and Testers, O*NET task 14638 (hardware-in-the-loop test plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Plan how a piece of software will be checked: which scenarios to exercise, which scripts or manual steps to run, and in what order.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your team is releasing new firmware for a battery-powered soil-moisture sensor that reports over LoRa every 15 minutes and must last two years on two AA cells. Write a test plan for the firmware release: the test scenarios (normal reporting, weak radio signal, battery near cutoff, sensor disconnected, clock drift), which tests run on a simulator versus on real devices in a test rack, how you will measure power draw, and the release criteria.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Embedded scenarios: Scenarios reflect embedded realities: power states, radio retries, brown-out, sensor faults, and long-duration behavior.
- Test environment: Explains what can be simulated versus what needs physical devices and instruments, and why.
- Quantified criteria: Release criteria include numbers (average current in microamps, packet success rate, no watchdog resets over a soak period).

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Scenario list with expected behavior, including retry and back-off on weak signal and safe shutdown at low voltage.
- Simulator for logic and timing; a rack of real sensors with programmable power supplies for power and radio tests.
- Power measured with a current analyzer across sleep and transmit cycles; projected battery life computed.
- 72-hour soak test with fault injection.
- Release only if projected battery life is at least two years and no resets occur.

### 14.0901-P2. `17-2061.00` Computer Hardware Engineers, O*NET task 7393 (verification report)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Check that computer hardware and attached devices meet their specifications by running tests and recording and analyzing the results.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You received the first ten prototype boards of a USB-C docking station meant to deliver 65 W to a laptop, drive two 4K displays and stay below 45 C on its case. Write a verification plan and a mock results summary: the tests you would run for each specification, the instruments needed, how many boards and repetitions, a results table (use clearly invented example numbers), and how you decide whether a failing spec is a design flaw or a board defect.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Spec-to-test mapping: Every stated specification has at least one test with method and instrument (electronic load, thermal camera, display test patterns).
- Data discipline: Sample size, repetitions and recording format are stated; example numbers are labeled as invented.
- Root-cause reasoning: Distinguishes a systematic failure across boards from a one-board defect and states the next diagnostic step.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Power delivery: step the load to 65 W and check voltage regulation and USB PD negotiation.
- Display: dual 4K at 60 Hz with pattern tests and cable variations.
- Thermal: case temperature at full load in a 25 C room after one hour, measured with a thermocouple and thermal camera.
- Results table across all ten boards with pass or fail and spread.
- Failure on most boards suggests a design issue (layout, component rating); a failure on a single board points to assembly; confirm by swapping parts.

### 14.0901-P3. `15-1252.00` Software Developers, O*NET task 21670 (driver porting plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Change existing software to fix defects, keep it working on new platforms or interfaces, and make it faster.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A lab's data-acquisition program talks to an analog-to-digital converter through a driver written for an older microcontroller. The board is being redesigned around a newer chip with a different SPI peripheral and DMA support, and users also report that samples are occasionally dropped above 10 kHz. Write a plan to port and improve the driver: what you would isolate behind a hardware-abstraction layer, how DMA could fix the dropped samples, the tests that prove timing and data integrity, and what you would keep unchanged.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Hardware-software boundary: Identifies which code is chip-specific (registers, clocks, interrupts) and proposes a clean abstraction so application code is untouched.
- Performance fix: Explains the dropped-sample cause plausibly (interrupt latency, buffer overrun) and how DMA with double buffering addresses it.
- Integrity tests: Tests include known input signals, sample counting, and timing measurement with a logic analyzer or oscilloscope.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Define an ADC interface (init, start, read buffer) and move register-level code into a chip-specific module.
- Use DMA with ping-pong buffers so the CPU handles full buffers instead of every sample.
- Feed a known sine or ramp signal and check for gaps and correct sample count over long runs.
- Measure SPI clock and chip-select timing with a logic analyzer against the converter's datasheet.
- Keep the file format and user interface unchanged so lab users see no difference.

### 14.0901-P4. `15-1253.00` Software Quality Assurance Analysts and Testers, O*NET task 14639 (pre-release test checklist)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Check that changes to a system work correctly and do not break anything before they are released.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A smart-thermostat company is pushing an over-the-air update that changes the heating control algorithm and the Wi-Fi reconnect logic. Before the update goes to 50,000 homes, write the checklist of tests and rollout gates: functional checks of heating and cooling, behavior when Wi-Fi drops mid-update, rollback if the update fails to boot, a staged rollout plan, and the telemetry signals that would stop the rollout.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Change-focused coverage: Tests target the two changed areas plus the update mechanism itself, including power or network loss during installation.
- Staged rollout: Uses a staged rollout (internal devices, small percentage, larger percentage) with explicit gates between stages.
- Stop conditions: Names measurable telemetry thresholds (boot failures, reconnect failures, temperature-overshoot reports) that halt the rollout.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Bench tests of the control loop with simulated temperature profiles; check overshoot and cycling.
- Interrupt Wi-Fi and power at several points during update; the device must boot the previous image.
- A/B firmware slots with automatic rollback on failed health check.
- Stages: employees, 1 percent, 10 percent, everyone, with 48-hour observation each.
- Halt if boot failure exceeds a small set rate or reconnect failures rise above baseline.

### 14.0901-P5. `17-2061.00` Computer Hardware Engineers, O*NET task 7403 (power budget)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Decide what the power supply must provide and how it is arranged, based on how the system must perform and on its design specifications.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are designing the power system for a single-board computer used in a museum kiosk: a quad-core processor, 4 GB of RAM, a 10-inch touchscreen, Wi-Fi, and two USB ports that visitors may use to charge phones. Produce a power budget and supply specification: list each load with typical and peak current per voltage rail, choose the input supply rating with margin, decide which rails need switching regulators versus linear regulators, and explain how you protect the board if a visitor plugs in a faulty device.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete budget: All loads are listed per rail with typical and peak values from plausible datasheet-style figures, and totals are computed correctly.
- Justified supply choices: Input rating includes margin, and regulator choices are justified by efficiency, heat and noise.
- Protection: Includes current limiting or electronic fuses on USB ports, reverse-polarity and overvoltage protection.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Rails such as 5 V for USB and display, 3.3 V for I/O and Wi-Fi, and around 1 V for the processor core.
- Peak budget includes simultaneous USB charging at the port limit; supply sized about 25 to 30 percent above peak.
- Buck converters for high-current rails; low-dropout regulator for the noise-sensitive radio or analog parts.
- Per-port current-limit switches with fault flag; TVS diode and fuse on input.
- Thermal check on regulators at peak load inside the kiosk enclosure.

### 14.0901-P6. `15-1252.00` Software Developers, O*NET task 21668 (performance requirements specification)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set the performance standards a system has to meet.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A city transit agency is commissioning onboard software that shows next-stop announcements and real-time arrival predictions on bus screens, using GPS and a cellular link. Write the performance requirements section of the specification: latency from GPS fix to on-screen update, prediction accuracy, behavior during cellular outages, startup time, and resource limits on the onboard computer. For each requirement give a number, how it will be measured, and why that value matters to riders.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Quantified requirements: Each requirement has a specific value and unit rather than vague words like 'fast'.
- Measurability: Each has a test method (logged timestamps, field trials on set routes, induced outages).
- Rider-centered rationale: Values are justified by rider experience or safety, such as announcing a stop before the bus reaches it.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Stop announcement at least a set number of seconds before arrival at typical speeds.
- Screen update within about 2 seconds of a GPS fix; prediction error within a stated band for most trips.
- During outages, fall back to schedule-based predictions and label them; resume within a stated time after reconnect.
- Boot to usable display within a stated time after ignition.
- CPU and memory ceilings leave headroom on the onboard computer; measured during field trials on named routes.
