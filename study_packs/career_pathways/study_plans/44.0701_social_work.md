## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (case documentation and professional writing), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Active Listening (practicing reflective listening in interviewing labs); Speaking (presenting case summaries in supervision); Critical Thinking (assessment case studies weighing risks and strengths).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Psychology (human behavior and the social environment and abnormal psychology); Therapy and Counseling (social work practice methods with individuals, families, and groups); Customer and Personal Service (client engagement in field practicum).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Child, Family, and School Social Workers; Probation Officers and Correctional Treatment Specialists): Law and Government (social welfare policy and child welfare law); Sociology and Anthropology (sociology of family, race and poverty, and community organization). Keep building Reading Comprehension (reading policy documents and practice research); Writing (writing case notes and service plans); Monitoring (monitoring client progress against case plans in field placement) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Child, Family, and School Social Workers; Probation Officers and Correctional Treatment Specialists; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Psychology, Therapy and Counseling.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Education and Training (3.36): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.
- Administrative (3.32): office procedures and records handling; usually learned on the job, so it gets no course slot.

### 44.0701-P1. `21-1021.00` Child, Family, and School Social Workers, O*NET task 235 (intake interview guide)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Interview clients alone, with family members or in groups to understand their circumstances, strengths and difficulties and decide which services they need.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a school refers a family because a 9-year-old has missed 22 days this semester; the mother works night shifts and the family recently moved in with relatives. Write the intake interview guide for a home visit: how you open and explain your role and confidentiality limits, strengths-based and open questions for the parent and age-appropriate questions for the child, how you assess safety, the information you need to decide on services, cultural humility considerations, and how you close and agree on next steps.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Engagement and ethics: Explains role, consent and mandated-reporting limits clearly and builds rapport respectfully.
- Holistic assessment: Questions cover needs, strengths, supports, housing, work schedules, health and child safety.
- Service decision: Links information gathered to specific service options and agreed next steps.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Introduction: who you are, why the referral, what is confidential and what is not.
- Parent questions: daily routine, barriers to attendance, supports, what has worked before.
- Child questions in simple language about school and home.
- Safety screening per agency tools.
- Next steps: transport assistance, school liaison, childcare, follow-up date.

### 44.0701-P2. `21-1092.00` Probation Officers and Correctional Treatment Specialists, O*NET task 1614 (case file organization)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Assemble and keep up a case file for each assigned offender.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You have been assigned a caseload of 60 people on probation. Design the case file structure and maintenance routine: the sections every file must have (court orders and conditions, risk and needs assessment, supervision plan, contacts, drug test results, treatment reports, violations, correspondence), naming and dating conventions, what must be updated after each contact and within what time, privacy and record-access rules, and a monthly self-audit checklist.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete structure: File sections cover legal, assessment, plan, contact and compliance records.
- Timely maintenance: Sets clear update deadlines and contact note standards.
- Confidentiality and audit: Addresses access rules for sensitive records (such as treatment information) and includes a self-audit.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Sections in a standard order, electronic or paper.
- Contact notes within 24 hours; objective and specific.
- Treatment records handled under substance-use confidentiality rules.
- Access limited to authorized staff; release requires proper consent.
- Monthly check: conditions tracked, assessments current, deadlines met.

### 44.0701-P3. `21-1023.00` Mental Health and Substance Abuse Social Workers, O*NET task 276 (progress monitoring plan (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Track, assess and record how clients are moving toward their treatment goals.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice (clinical social work is typically a master's-level, licensed role). Invented case: a 34-year-old client in outpatient treatment for alcohol use and depression has goals of reducing drinking days and returning to work. Design the progress-monitoring plan: measurable indicators for each goal, standardized measures you would use and how often, how the client participates in reviewing data, how you write progress notes, and what changes in the data would lead you to adjust the plan with the supervisor.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Measurable indicators: Each goal has observable, measurable indicators and a baseline.
- Appropriate measures: Uses suitable validated tools at sensible intervals.
- Collaborative adjustment: Client reviews progress with the worker, and clear criteria trigger plan changes.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Indicators: drinking days per week, depression score, job search activities.
- Measures: a depression questionnaire every two weeks, timeline follow-back for drinking.
- Shared graph reviewed in sessions.
- Progress notes in a structured format such as DAP or SOAP.
- No improvement after about six weeks leads to case review and possible referral.

### 44.0701-P4. `21-1021.00` Child, Family, and School Social Workers, O*NET task 241 (service plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Create and revisit service plans together with clients and follow up to check how much and how well services were delivered.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Continuing the school-attendance case, write a 90-day service plan developed with the mother: two or three family-chosen goals written in measurable terms, the services and who provides them, the family's own action steps, how and when you follow up to check whether services actually happened and helped, and how the plan will be revised. Write it in plain language the family can keep.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Client-centered goals: Goals reflect the family's priorities and are measurable and time-bound.
- Concrete services and steps: Each goal has named services, providers and family steps.
- Follow-up on quantity and quality: Plans checks on whether services were delivered and helpful, with revision points.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Goals: child attends at least 90 percent of days; stable morning routine; housing application submitted.
- Services: school transport, after-school program, housing navigator.
- Family steps: alarm routine, contact school when absent.
- Follow-up: biweekly calls, monthly home visit, attendance data from school.
- Review at 45 and 90 days; revise or close.

### 44.0701-P5. `21-1092.00` Probation Officers and Correctional Treatment Specialists, O*NET task 1618 (background investigation plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Collect background information on offenders by talking with them, their families, friends and others who know relevant facts.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A judge orders a presentence investigation for a 23-year-old convicted of a first-time felony theft. Write the information-gathering plan: whom you will interview (the person, family, employer, victim), the records you will request (criminal history, school, employment, treatment), how you verify claims and handle conflicting accounts, how you protect sensitive information, and the structure of the report to the court.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Comprehensive sources: Includes interviews and records relevant to risk, needs and circumstances, including the victim's perspective.
- Verification: Verifies self-reported information and notes unresolved conflicts.
- Ethical and structured report: Respects confidentiality and produces a balanced report format for the court.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Interviews: defendant, family, employer with consent, victim impact.
- Records: criminal history, school, employment, treatment with releases.
- Verify through documents and second sources; note conflicts.
- Sensitive information shared only as permitted.
- Report: offense, history, personal background, risk assessment, recommendation.

### 44.0701-P6. `21-1023.00` Mental Health and Substance Abuse Social Workers, O*NET task 281 (staff supervision plan (graduate-path practice))

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Supervise or direct the staff who work directly with clients.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Graduate-path practice: imagine you are a licensed clinical social worker supervising three bachelor's-level case aides at a community mental-health clinic. Write the supervision plan: weekly structure (individual and group), how you review case notes and caseload safety, training priorities, how you support aides after a difficult client crisis, how you evaluate performance, and the ethical limits on what aides may do.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Structured supervision: Schedule and content cover case review, skill development and administration.
- Support and safety: Addresses secondary trauma, debriefing and caseload risk.
- Scope and evaluation: Defines scope-of-practice limits and fair performance evaluation.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Weekly one-hour individual and biweekly group supervision.
- Case note audits and high-risk client review.
- Training: crisis de-escalation, documentation, cultural responsiveness.
- Debrief after crises; employee assistance referral.
- Evaluation against job standards; aides do not provide therapy.
