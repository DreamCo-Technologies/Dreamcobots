## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education, including English Language (scientific writing: lab reports and a journal-style paper), plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (reading safety data sheets, methods papers, and journal articles); Speaking (presenting research at undergraduate symposia); Active Listening (following lab safety briefings and research group discussions).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: Computers and Electronics (scientific computing and instrument software: data analysis in Python and computational chemistry); Mathematics (calculus and statistics for data analysis); Chemistry (general, organic, analytical, physical, and inorganic chemistry with labs).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets (Chemists; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Clinical Research Coordinators): Public Safety and Security (chemical hygiene and laboratory safety: hazard communication, waste handling, and safety data sheets); Psychology (learning and adolescent development, useful if you aim at teaching chemistry). Keep building Critical Thinking (troubleshooting experiments and evaluating data quality); Writing (writing lab reports with revision); Monitoring (monitoring experiments and instrument readings, and noting deviations in your lab notebook) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Chemists; Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Clinical Research Coordinators; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows Computers and Electronics, Mathematics.

Knowledge areas from the top list that the outline deliberately does not cover:

- Customer and Personal Service (3.5): service and client-relations knowledge; the outline leaves it to internships, part-time work and the practice tasks rather than a course slot.
- Education and Training (3.28): teaching and training others is built through presenting, tutoring and peer teaching in projects rather than a dedicated course.
- Administrative (3.12): office procedures and records handling; usually learned on the job, so it gets no course slot.
- Administration and Management (2.99): management and planning knowledge matters most after several years of experience, so the outline gives it no course slot (a management elective is optional).

### 40.0501-P1. `19-2031.00` Chemists, O*NET task 1505 (analytical method plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Determine what a substance is made of and how it behaves, using instrumental techniques such as chromatography and spectroscopy.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A consumer group asks your lab whether five energy drinks contain the caffeine amounts on their labels and whether any contain an undeclared stimulant. Write the analytical plan: sample preparation (degassing, dilution, filtration), the technique for quantifying caffeine (for example, HPLC with UV detection) and how you build the calibration curve, how you would screen for other stimulants (such as LC-MS), quality controls (blanks, duplicates, spiked recovery), how you calculate and report results with uncertainty, and how you decide whether a label is inaccurate.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Appropriate techniques: Chooses suitable separation and detection methods and justifies them for caffeine and for screening unknowns.
- Quality assurance: Includes calibration with standards, blanks, duplicates, spike recovery and acceptance criteria.
- Defensible reporting: Calculates concentration per serving with uncertainty and sets a clear rule for judging label accuracy.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Degas, dilute into the calibration range, filter through a 0.45 micrometer filter.
- Reversed-phase HPLC with UV near 273 nm; five-point calibration; check linearity.
- Screening for undeclared stimulants by LC-MS against reference standards.
- QC: method blank, duplicate injections, spike recovery between about 90 and 110 percent.
- Report milligrams per serving with expanded uncertainty; flag labels outside a stated tolerance.

### 40.0501-P2. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (chemistry lab rules and routines)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You are a new high school chemistry teacher; your first wet lab (reactions of acids with metals and carbonates) is in week three. Write the rules and routines plan: the lab-safety rules you will teach and how students show they understand them before any lab, routines for goggles, chemical pickup and waste disposal, how you signal stop, what happens if a student breaks a safety rule versus a general classroom rule, and how routines are taught in the first weeks.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Chemistry-specific safety: Rules address eye protection, chemical handling, waste, spills, and no food or drink, matched to the planned lab.
- Practiced routines: Routines are modeled and rehearsed, with a safety quiz or contract before the first lab.
- Proportionate responses: Distinguishes immediate removal for safety violations from graduated responses for other misbehavior and applies them consistently.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Rules: goggles on from start to cleanup, read labels twice, never return chemicals to stock bottles, report spills and injuries immediately.
- Routines: materials table pickup by one student per group, labeled waste containers, stop signal.
- Safety quiz with a minimum score required before lab participation.
- Safety violation: removal from the lab activity with an alternative task and family contact.
- Rehearse a mock spill drill in week one.

### 40.0501-P3. `11-9121.01` Clinical Research Coordinators, O*NET task 15585 (audit readiness plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Get ready for, or take part in, quality audits by study sponsors, government agencies or special review groups.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your clinical research site has been told a sponsor will audit a diabetes drug study in four weeks, and that a regulatory inspection could follow. Write the audit-readiness plan: the documents to review (regulatory binder, consent forms, delegation log, training records, investigational product accountability, source documents versus case report forms), a sampling approach for self-checking participant records, how findings are corrected without backdating, staff preparation, and the logistics for the audit days.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete scope: Covers essential documents, consent, eligibility, product accountability, adverse event reporting and data consistency.
- Honest corrections: Corrections follow good documentation practice (dated, initialed, explained); no backdating or recreating records.
- Preparation and logistics: Staff know their roles and the audit days are planned (workspace, access, contact person, daily debrief).

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Checklist of essential documents and current versions.
- Review all consents and a sample of participant files for eligibility and source-to-database agreement.
- Product accountability: dispensing logs reconcile with returns and stock.
- Corrections documented with date, initials and reason; notes to file where needed.
- Audit logistics: room, monitor access to records, coordinator available, daily wrap-up.

### 40.0501-P4. `19-2031.00` Chemists, O*NET task 1508 (technical consultation summary)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Work with scientists and engineers to study a project's chemistry, make sense of test data, or devise special-purpose tests.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Invented case: a process engineer reports that the last three batches of a polyurethane coating cure tacky, although every standard QC test passed. You meet the engineer and the formulation chemist. Write the meeting summary and plan: the questions you asked, hypotheses (moisture in raw materials, catalyst level, isocyanate-to-polyol ratio, cure temperature), the existing data you reviewed, a non-standard test you propose (for example, FTIR monitoring of isocyanate peak during cure or Karl Fischer water testing of raw materials), and how results will decide the fix.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Collaborative inquiry: Captures input from both colleagues and asks questions that separate hypotheses.
- Scientific reasoning: Hypotheses are chemically plausible and the proposed tests can confirm or rule them out.
- Decision path: States what result points to which corrective action and how quickly it can be tested.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Questions: raw material lots, humidity during mixing, mixing ratios, cure oven logs.
- Hypotheses ranked by likelihood with evidence for each.
- Tests: Karl Fischer on polyol lots, FTIR of residual isocyanate over time, small batches at controlled ratios.
- Decision tree: high water means drying or new supplier lot; off-ratio means meter calibration.
- Owners and dates; summary shared with QC and production.

### 40.0501-P5. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6644 (differentiated chemistry lesson)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Adjust teaching approaches and materials to suit students' different needs and interests.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Your 10th-grade chemistry class is starting stoichiometry. A pre-test shows some students are unsure about ratios and unit conversions, most are ready, and a few have already learned mole calculations. Plan a 55-minute lesson with differentiation: the shared goal, a hands-on hook (for example, a recipe-scaling activity), tiered practice sets, scaffolds such as worked examples and unit-tracking organizers, an extension involving limiting reactants, and a common exit ticket.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Accurate chemistry: Mole ratios and conversions are correct and the hook maps clearly to chemical equations.
- Effective tiers: Tiers vary support and challenge while targeting the same goal.
- Assessment: Exit ticket measures the shared goal and informs grouping for the next lesson.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Goal: use a balanced equation to convert moles of one substance to another.
- Hook: scale a sandwich recipe, then link to reactant ratios.
- Tier 1: worked examples with unit organizer; Tier 2: standard problems; Tier 3: limiting reactant puzzles.
- Teacher checks in with each group; peer explainers.
- Exit ticket: one mole-to-mole and one mass-to-mass problem.

### 40.0501-P6. `11-9121.01` Clinical Research Coordinators, O*NET task 15592 (enrollment and retention tracking system)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Keep track of participants' enrollment status and record why participants drop out and what was done to reach them.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A 12-month nutrition study at your site aims to enroll 150 participants and has lost 11 of the first 60. Design the tracking system: the fields in the enrollment and retention log (screening, consent, randomization, visit dates, status, withdrawal reason categories, contact attempts), how contact attempts are documented, weekly reports to the investigator, how you protect participant privacy in the log, and two retention strategies suggested by the dropout reasons you invent and label.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Complete log design: Fields capture each stage and use standardized withdrawal reason categories and contact-attempt records.
- Useful reporting: Weekly report shows enrollment versus target, retention rate and trends in dropout reasons.
- Privacy and retention: Log uses participant IDs rather than names where possible, and retention strategies follow from the reasons.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Fields: participant ID, screen date, consent version, randomization arm, visit windows, status, withdrawal reason code, last contact.
- Contact attempts: date, method, outcome; protocol-defined lost-to-follow-up rule.
- Report: cumulative enrollment chart, retention percent, reasons table.
- Identifiers kept in a separate restricted file.
- Retention ideas: flexible visit times, transport support, reminder texts.
