## 4-year outline (DreamCo-derived guidance, not O*NET data)

1. Year 1, foundations. General education plus deliberate practice in the top-rated skills: Reading Comprehension (close reading of literary and critical texts); Writing (drafting and revising analytical essays); Speaking (leading seminar discussions).
2. Year 2, core knowledge. Build the highest-rated knowledge areas for this major: English Language (literary analysis, linguistics, grammar, and advanced composition); Education and Training (writing-center tutoring and teaching methods); Communications and Media (editing, publishing, and digital media writing).
3. Year 3, depth toward the entry targets. Prioritize knowledge rated highest for Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Proofreaders and Copy Markers: Computers and Electronics (digital humanities tools and content-management systems); Customer and Personal Service (working with writers and readers at a writing center or library); Administrative (editorial workflow and style-guide management). Keep building Active Listening (peer workshop feedback); Critical Thinking (evaluating interpretations and evidence) through project work.
4. Year 4, launch. Internship or capstone aimed at Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education; Proofreaders and Copy Markers; build a portfolio from the practice tasks below that shows English Language, Education and Training.

### 23.0101-P1. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6639 (English classroom routines plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Set and consistently apply rules and routines that keep a classroom orderly.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. You teach 10th-grade English with daily independent reading, small-group literature circles and a writing workshop with peer feedback. Write the classroom routines and expectations plan: the start-of-class routine, expectations for literature circle roles and discussion, how peer feedback stays kind and specific, phone and device expectations, what happens when expectations are not met, and how you teach the routines in the first two weeks.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Routines fit the instruction: Routines directly support reading, discussion and writing workshop rather than generic rules.
- Specific expectations: Expectations for discussion and peer feedback are concrete (sentence stems, role cards, feedback protocol).
- Consistent follow-through: Responses to unmet expectations are graduated, respectful and consistently applied.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Start: 10 minutes of silent independent reading while attendance is taken.
- Literature circle roles rotated; discussion norms posted.
- Peer feedback protocol: one strength, one question, one suggestion, tied to the rubric.
- Devices in a pocket chart unless used for drafting.
- Responses: reminder, conference, adjusted seating, family contact; routines modeled with a fishbowl discussion.

### 23.0101-P2. `43-9081.00` Proofreaders and Copy Markers, O*NET task 5158 (proofreading pass and error log)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Fix or note any missing material, mistakes and inconsistencies found in copy.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Proofread this invented 120-word press release excerpt and produce an error log: "Riverside Community College are proud to announce it's new nursing lab, which open on Sept. 3rd, 2026. The lab, funded by a $1.2M grant from the Hartwell foundation, include 12 simulation beds, and a debriefing room. 'Our students deserve the best,' said Dean Maria Lopez. Dr. Lopez, who joined Riverside in 2019 will lead a ribbon-cutting on September 5. Tours are offered 10am-2pm and registration is required, visit riverside.edu/nursinglab." For each issue, record the location, the error type (grammar, punctuation, consistency, fact to verify), your correction, and whether it needs an author query.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Error detection: Finds the subject-verb agreement, its/it's, date inconsistency (Sept. 3 versus September 5 and opening versus ribbon-cutting), missing commas and run-on sentence.
- Correct, minimal fixes: Corrections follow a consistent style and change no more than necessary.
- Queries for facts: Flags items that need confirmation (names, title Dean versus Dr., date logic, grant amount) instead of guessing.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- 'are' to 'is'; 'it's' to 'its'; 'open' to 'opens'; 'include' to 'includes'.
- Remove serial-style comma before 'and a debriefing room'; add comma after 2019.
- Style consistency for dates and times (September 3; 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.).
- Fix the run-on: registration is required; visit the site.
- Queries: Hartwell Foundation capitalization, the dean's title, whether opening and ribbon-cutting dates are correct.

### 23.0101-P3. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6640 (literature lesson plan)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Teach a subject through explanation, discussion and demonstration.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Plan a 50-minute 11th-grade lesson on a short poem of your choice that is in the public domain (for example, by Emily Dickinson or Langston Hughes, if public-domain status is confirmed). Include a brief explanation of one literary concept (such as tone shift or extended metaphor), a teacher think-aloud demonstrating close reading of two lines, a structured small-group discussion, and a short written response. Explain how students move from your model to independent analysis.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Accurate literary teaching: The concept is explained accurately and the think-aloud models evidence-based interpretation.
- Gradual release: The lesson moves from modeling to guided practice to independent work.
- Meaningful response: The written task requires a claim supported by textual evidence and analysis.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Concept mini-lesson with a simple definition and example.
- Think-aloud on two lines: notice word choice, ask questions, form a claim.
- Groups annotate the rest of the poem using a guiding question.
- Share-out with evidence; teacher pushes for analysis beyond summary.
- Exit paragraph: claim, quotation, explanation of effect.

### 23.0101-P4. `43-9081.00` Proofreaders and Copy Markers, O*NET task 5161 (copy-against-source verification)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Check information or figures in one record against the same data elsewhere or against the original copy to catch mistakes.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. A nonprofit's annual report has a typeset table of program results that must match the finance office's spreadsheet and the program staff's original text. Describe your verification procedure: how you compare figures (reading aloud with a partner, column totals, cross-footing), how you check names and titles against an authoritative list, what you do when the typeset text and the source disagree, and how you record and return corrections. Then create a short invented example with three discrepancies and show how each is logged.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Systematic comparison: Uses reliable comparison methods (partner reading, totals, cross-checks) covering every figure.
- Correct handling of conflicts: Does not silently choose a value when sources conflict; queries the author or source owner.
- Clear logging: Each discrepancy is logged with location, both values, action and resolution.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Two-person read-back of each figure; recompute totals and percentages.
- Names and titles checked against the staff directory and donor database.
- Conflicts sent as queries to the finance or program owner with both versions.
- Corrections marked on the proof and logged in a tracker.
- Examples: transposed digits, a total that does not add up, a misspelled donor name.

### 23.0101-P5. `25-2031.00` Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education, O*NET task 6642 (assessment and rubric design)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Write, administer and mark tests and assignments that show how students are progressing.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Design the summative assessment for a four-week 9th-grade unit on argument writing: the prompt (with a choice of two debatable topics and a short source set), the analytic rubric with four criteria and performance levels, accommodations for students with IEPs and English learners, how you will grade consistently across 120 essays (anchor papers, calibration), and how students receive feedback they can use in the next unit.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Aligned assessment: The prompt and rubric measure the unit's goals (claim, evidence, reasoning, counterargument, conventions).
- Fair and consistent grading: Includes anchor papers, calibration and accommodations that keep the same standard.
- Usable feedback: Feedback is specific, timely and connected to a next-step goal.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Prompt with two topics and three short sources of varied perspectives.
- Rubric criteria: claim, evidence, reasoning and counterargument, organization and conventions; four levels.
- Accommodations: extended time, read-aloud of sources, bilingual glossary.
- Grade with anchor papers; second-read a sample of essays.
- Feedback: two strengths and one goal per student; a revision conference.

### 23.0101-P6. `43-9081.00` Proofreaders and Copy Markers, O*NET task 5159 (proof markup exercise)

Work activity (DreamCo paraphrase): Use standard proofreading symbols on copy to flag and fix typographic, layout, grammatical, punctuation and spelling mistakes.

DreamCo-original practice prompt. Using standard proofreaders' marks (as in a major style manual), mark up this invented proof line by line and explain each mark: (1) "teh committee will meet on monday"; (2) "The results were suprising , and the team celebrated."; (3) a heading set in bold that should be italic; (4) a paragraph that should start on a new line; (5) two words that should be transposed: "the final report draft first". Provide both the margin mark and the in-text mark for each and note any house-style decisions you assumed.

Rubric (0 to 2 points each):

- Correct marks: Uses the standard marks for transposition, capitalization, deletion, spacing, new paragraph and font change.
- Complete markup: Every error is marked in text and in the margin as conventions require.
- Style awareness: Notes assumed house-style choices and stays consistent.

Reference answer outline (what a strong answer includes):

- Transpose mark for 'teh'; triple underline for capital M in Monday.
- Insert letter in 'surprising'; close-up mark to remove the space before the comma.
- Circle and 'ital' in margin for the heading; note bold removal.
- New paragraph mark before the sentence.
- Transpose 'report draft'; state that the style manual's marks were used.
